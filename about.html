★ The Methodology ★

How we set the lines.

01

The Draft

At the start of each Oscar cycle, both hosts publish their preseason Big Board — a ranked top 25 of films we expect to be in the conversation. The contributing writers do their own. The composite becomes the consensus board, and we revisit it every Tuesday.

02

Weekly Rebalancing

Every wide release gets a review. Every review gets a star rating and a Buy/Hold/Sell call. The Buy/Hold/Sell isn't a critical verdict — it's an awards-reality call. A four-star film can absolutely be a Sell. A three-star film can absolutely be a Buy. That distinction is the whole show.

03

Scorecard Scoring

In January, before nominations, we lock in our final ballots — Best Picture, all eight craft categories, every acting race. Points are awarded for correct picks, with bonus multipliers for sleeper calls we made before the precursor circuit. The winner buys dinner. The loser writes the recap.

★ Why the Framework

A defense of the bit.

Fantasy sports works because it makes you watch closer. The minute you draft a player, you're not just a fan of the team you root for — you're a fan of that player's specific Tuesday night. You know who's injured on a third-string roster you'd never have looked at otherwise. You know the depth charts. You care about box scores. The game becomes more interesting because the stakes get personal.

The Oscar race, it turns out, does the same thing for film criticism — if you let it.

Treat a movie as a stock and you start to ask different questions. Not just is this good? — although that always matters first — but where is this going? what's the floor? what's the ceiling? what would have to happen for this to win? You start to care about the dailies and the trade reports and the festival screenings. You start to notice the campaign — and notice when a campaign is doing too much, or not enough. You start to see the Academy as a population of voters with their own taste and biases and exhaustion and goodwill. You start to see the whole shape of a year.

And — and this is the part we keep coming back to — you fall in love with the films you would have missed otherwise. The supporting performance in a movie you didn't think you'd like. The screenplay that everyone undervalued. The craft category contender that turns out to be the most exciting filmmaking of the year.

That's what the show is. That's what this site is. Welcome.
