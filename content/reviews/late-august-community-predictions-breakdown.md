The fall festivals are upon us! It is mere days before we'll be overrun with reactions, reviews, and discourse over heaps of buzzy premieres at a wide variety of film festivals, including Venice, Telluride, TIFF, NYFF, and more. We've updated our community aggregate predictions one last time to finalize where Filmball stands on the state of the race as we enter these game-changing weeks. Not *too* much has shifted since our last update, but there's been a few trends to note. Let's take a look.

<h2>Ink Enters the Race</h2>

If there's one movie that's really gaining momentum in the last month, I think it's gotta be *Ink*. News around Danny Boyle's latest film, about famed tabloid newspaper *The Sun*, went completely radio silent in the first half of this year, with a presentation at CinemaCon even implying that it was set for release in 2027. This all changed only a few days before our last update, with the surprise reveal that *Ink* would serve as the opening night film for the Venice Film Festival. Just a day later, it was picked up for distribution by awards juggernaut Netflix. Because this news came so close to the last update, the changes in predictions were there, but overall minimal.

Since then, *Ink* has been announced to be at several fall festivals, settling comfortably into the illustrious Special Presentations lineup at TIFF, with a listing that implies it may also pop up at the Telluride Film Festival. Just before this update, a leaked trailer also circulated online, further increasing excitement for the new movie from the previous Academy darling behind *Slumdog Millionaire* and *127 Hours*.

While our consensus doesn't quite have it in the Best Picture lineup as of now, it's jumped way up to #11 with 42% predicting the nomination. Guy Pearce is firmly placed in our Best Supporting Actor 5, not only with over half our community predicting him, but a significant 17% saying that this man can take home the golden statue in March. Further, *Ink* has nudged its way into the Adapted Screenplay category, knocking *All of a Sudden* and *The Social Reckoning* down a peg. This all goes without mentioning the major leaps made in Director, Actor, and Editing, promising a significant package should *Ink* truly break into the race.

We're only a couple of days away from knowing what the reception will be on this film, but the train is gaining steam. Personally, I'm buying some stock in this movie too. Danny Boyle is a reliably strong director, and while he hasn't gotten into the Oscars recently, he also hasn't been making Oscar-friendly films recently. His most recent awards-friendly film was *Steve Jobs*, which, while disappointing relative to expectation, still garnered Actor and Supporting Actress nominations, and was quite close to a Screenplay nom as well. We know Netflix can push two movies into Picture, and the full fall festival rollout shows a lot of confidence. I have *Ink* in for Picture, Supporting Actor (Pearce appears to be the standout role from the play this is based on), Supporting Actress (Claire Foy is playing a brand new character, and is being billed on the same level as Pearce…seems like she might have something to do here), Adapted Screenplay (this seems to be taking some departures from the initial play, that would likely stand out as impressive adaptive work), Editing (a Boyle film with showy editing? Say it ain't so), and I'm flirting with putting Boyle in Director and O'Connell in Actor depending on how the races shake out. Of course, it could be a dud, but if it's a good movie, I'm sure it'll make a splash.

5 of these 8 have over half the predictors calling the same film at the very bottom. This may be indicative of a firmer consensus forming in these categories, as many become confident in the same set of films getting into the same places due to the lack of major new releases to shift mindset.

This theory is further strengthened when you consider how high the conformity is for the top few slots in each category. The top 7 in Picture, as well as the top 3 in Director, Actor, and Adapted Screenplay are all above 80% in nomination tally, indicating that the vast majority of people are confident in many slots in these categories. Even the more contentious categories, such as Best Actress, have a few near-unanimously agreed upon picks like Julianne Moore for *The Debut* and Inde Navarrette for *Obsession*.

Personally, I think conformity is dangerous at this stage in the race. There is definitely such a thing as going off on too many limbs to the point of dismissing common sense, but the consensus ten is just not going to be correct at this stage of the year. The ideal predictor should be trying to determine what *will* happen to the race in January, not what *is* happening in the race in August. I think a few more people should be taking a few more shots in these big categories while there's still a chance to make some arguments for some unseen, underrated, or otherwise unknown quantities.

<h2>The Supporting Categories are a House of Cards</h2>

Of the top 10 most predicted for each of Supporting Actress and Supporting Actor, there are four from *Wild Horse Nine* (di Girolamo and Posey for Actress, Rockwell and Buscemi for Actor), three from *The Odyssey* (Hathaway and Morton for Actress, Leguizamo for Actor), three from *Digger* (Hüller for Actress, Goodman and Ahmed for Actor), and three from *La Bola Negra* (Cruz and Dueñas for Actress, Guitarricadelafuente for Actor). That's 65% of the top 10 for each category being taken up by only four films, including 70% of the Supporting Actress top 10 and 60% of the Supporting Actor top 10.

This is a highly variable amount of faith in movies that can, quite frankly, go in any direction. *Wild Horse Nine* and *Digger* are both unseen quantities, and both carry their own respective risks during predictions at this stage of the year. While Martin McDonagh is a remarkably reliable filmmaker, this film looks to be much more outright comedic than his previous two outings, and may serve as closer to his earlier work of *In Bruges* and *Seven Psychopaths* than his Academy darlings of *Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri* and *The Banshees of Inisherin*. This may well be the type of movie that's easy to laugh at, but not something that stands out as the best of the year. *Digger*'s risks feel even more obvious upfront, with director Alejandro G. Iñárritu known for his big swings and divisive landings, and a trailer that has split the Internet in two already. It looks like a movie that won't be pulling any punches, and might be so over the top in its execution that it simply gets crucified by the critics. Should either of these movies prove duds, both Supporting lineups would be dramatically affected, as it's hard to imagine any of these contenders making it in without their movies coming along.

*Odyssey* and *La Bola Negra* may seem like safer bets, given both have already publicly premiered and proven the goods for a strong Oscar showing, but when it comes to predicting their Supporting players there is still a fair level of variance. Hathaway feels like a layup here, but rumors of her potentially being campaigned for Lead Actress instead of Supporting could serve as a sign of danger. Given her obvious status as a supporting character behind Odysseus in the film, it's possible that there wouldn't be enough passion for her to get into a category that she was frauded up into, similar to Chase Infiniti last year for *One Battle After Another* (admittedly, there is a world of difference between this absolute newcomer and a former Oscar winner). Furthermore, it's hard to tell if Morton would truly work as an additional push for Supporting Actress, given her limited screentime in the film and it being generally difficult to single out a standout in the supporting cast beyond Hathaway. This difficulty in picking out an MVP may very well also translate to the Supporting Actor category, where any of Leguizamo, Pattinson, or Holland have their arguments for and against why the studio might focus on pushing them. If this author had his way, even Elliot Page would be thrown into the mix there. Can Leguizamo truly stand out enough to be the focused push of the FYC campaign and slip in despite never being nominated prior?

*La Bola Negra* may be even more dense when it comes to its potential acting campaign. Netflix loves to push acting nominations in, especially for their biggest Picture contenders, but there isn't an obvious choice here considering the cast of actors not widely known to an American audience. While many have centered around Penélope Cruz as an obvious play, tales of her limited runtime may stifle her ability to truly be seen as a push, while supposedly showier actress Lola Dueñas may prove too unknown for the Academy to go for. Guitarricadelafuente is one of the co-leads of the film, and thus may very well be run in the competitive lead category, but some are anticipating that the studio king of category fraud may move him down to supporting and push him for an additional nomination; however, even if frauded down, would the Oscars really go for such a young newcomer to the scene?

Any of these movies could experience dramatic changes in their ability to get these supporting players in. One of these movies missing the supporting categories would majorly impact predictions across the board. Two missing could result in a complete upheaval across the Filmball community. Personally, I still think it's a safer bet to predict Supporting categories based on what you have in Picture at this stage of the year, and I do consider these four films as safe as you can get at this point. However, I'm keeping my eye out for alternatives at all times.
