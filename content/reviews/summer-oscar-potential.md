The summer months can often be slim pickings when it comes to Oscar hopefuls. Over the last three years, just two eventual Best Picture nominees were theatrically released in North America during the months of July and August, those being (if you haven’t guessed already) *Barbie* and *Oppenheimer*. And aside from *F1*, which roared into multiplexes in late June last year, no other summer blockbuster has received a Best Picture nomination in that time. That said, it’s still worth speculating which films on the horizon could find their way to a nomination or two come Oscar season.

There’s no better place to start than the current Best Picture frontrunner of our Fantasy Filmball community, Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey*, which premieres on July 17th. We currently have the film predicted in five above-the-line categories, including Director, Actor (Damon), Supporting Actress (Hathaway), and Adapted Screenplay. Where the film is most expected to thrive, though, is in the tech categories, where users of Award Expert have predicted it for nine other nominations, bringing its potential haul to fourteen. If these predictions were to hold, *The Odyssey* would outdo *Oppenheimer*’s nomination total by one, though I don’t personally see that particular scenario happening. *Oppenheimer* took home seven Oscars, including Best Picture, which Nolan will surely hope to replicate with his follow-up.

The new Anthony Bourdain biopic, *Tony*, directed by Matt Johnson and starring Dominic Sessa, is set to hit theatres on August 7th. Johnson’s last biopic, *BlackBerry*, received some awards buzz for its performances and screenplay, while his latest film, *Nirvanna the Band the Show the Movie*, was a crowd favourite at last year’s Toronto International Film Festival and still holds an impressive 4.3 Letterboxd score. Meanwhile, Sessa received Oscar buzz himself a few years ago for his breakout performance in *The Holdovers*. One can only speculate that an awards campaign for the biopic would be a logical next step for both director and star. Currently, the release date is the only thing holding me back from calling this a prospect for Best Picture. If A24 had higher hopes for *Tony*, they’d surely have slated it for October, allowing the film to go to TIFF, which has been a staple for all of Johnson’s previous directorial efforts. Still, I trust the talent enough to deliver something that will find its way into a potential Indie Spirit lineup.

Moving on to some potential tech players, on July 31st, the newest entry in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, *Spider-Man: Brand New Day*, will look to replicate the success of its predecessor, which became one of the highest-grossing films of all time. *No Way Home*’s critical and commercial success propelled it to a nomination for Best Visual Effects, giving the upcoming sequel a decent chance at becoming an Oscar nominee as well, but frankly, if any Marvel film is going to crack into the category, I’d bet on *Avengers: Doomsday* instead. Other potential Visual Effects contenders coming out in August are David Robert Mitchell’s *The End of Oak Street* and Ridley Scott’s *The Dog Stars*, scheduled to hit theaters on the 14th and 28th, respectively. These two science-fiction films seem to have decent tech potential, provided they find some commercial and critical success first.

Lastly, it’s worth looking through some of the already-seen festival films that are finally being released in theatres. From Cannes, Hirokazu Koreeda’s *Sheep in the Box* and Pedro Almodóvar’s *Bitter Christmas*, both Palme d’Or competitors, will be released in the United States this summer, but although their directors have seen Oscar success in the past, neither of these films were particularly well-received. One Cannes film that did receive tremendous praise, though, was Jane Schoenbrun’s followup to her acclaimed *I Saw the TV Glow*, *Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma*, which competed in the Un Certain Regard section, ultimately winning the Queer Palm. Its obstacle this awards season will be the fact that it’s a slasher film, a genre the Academy doesn’t often recognize. Viral success or a big showing in the indie awards circuit could lift the film to industry awards attention, so it may be one to look out for when it releases on August 7th. As a massive fan of *I Saw the TV Glow*, I can’t wait to see her Cannes award winner, but if the two films are anything alike, I’d be shocked if *Camp Miasma* got close to the Oscar conversation. Is a sneaky Makeup & Hairstyling nod too much to hope for?

This past weekend, Olivia Wilde’s *The Invite*, which premiered at Sundance earlier this year, has been getting stellar reviews, with people eyeing its adapted screenplay and performances as possible recipients of awards nominations come the end of the year. Though Penelope Cruz will likely get more buzz for *La Bola Negra*, you can’t count out four-time nominee Edward Norton from the conversation.

One festival film whose Oscar chances seem to be in limbo is Black Bear’s *The Rivals of Amziah King*, which will finally see a limited release on August 14th before going wide a week later. After premiering at SXSW in the spring of last year, the film generated significant buzz for its lead, Matthew McConaughey, with many pundits predicting a big across-the-board awards campaign last year. As it happens, after a difficult process, Black Bear delayed the release until this summer, which isn’t ideal for an Oscar hopeful. Somewhere along the line, *The Rivals of Amziah King* went from being one of the most anticipated films in the awards conversation to potentially fumbling its campaign altogether. Only time will tell if it can manage to regenerate that buzz in August.

Money on the line, I’d say *The Odyssey* is probably the only big Oscar player that will premiere in the coming summer months. That said, I’m not counting out *The Invite*, which has been seeing pretty strong per-theatre numbers this weekend, and has gotten nothing short of rave reviews, bagging a strong 80 on Metacritic, and boasting a 4.1 on Letterboxd at the time of writing. And I still hold out hope for *Tony*, even if an Oscar nomination seems like a bridge too far. Among the tech contenders, *The End of Oak Street* is probably the safest bet among the summer releases, teetering just outside the top five for Visual Effects on Award Expert, in 8th place. So while awards talk usually finds itself in a lull during these summer months, best believe there are still plenty of films worth checking out for all of us Oscar nuts out there.
