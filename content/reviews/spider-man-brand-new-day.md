After one of the most all-out comic book movies of all time, *Spider-Man: No Way Home*, filled with nostalgia bait and fan service references to all of the live-action Spider-Man movies that came before it, ended with a spell that made everyone in the world forget who Peter Parker is, the follow-up movie dealing with the aftermath is far more self-contained. It feels like a sequel the way any other sequel would, with what happened in the prior MCU Spider-Man movies being integral to what happens in this one, but outside of a couple other fan-favorite characters appearing in small roles, the rest of the MCU might as well not exist, and that's a good thing.

Peter Parker is left yearning for the days he was close with Ned and MJ, clearly still hurting anytime he's reminded that they now have no idea who he is. He turns to helping catch criminals as an emotional crutch, the only thing that brings him joy, wanting to help out with anything and everything he gets an alert for, even if he's overworking himself. These emotions are physically affecting him and his abilities. This all gives Tom Holland some of the best material he's gotten to work with, and from the emotion to the anxiety to the confusion and everything beyond, he nails it.

Through this all, he's dealing with a villain who can move from one person's body to the next through telepathy. Peter is the only person this villain can't seem to possess, but he never knows who else that he encounters can actually be the telepath. This story being contained to New York, not really threatening anything beyond that, makes the movie feel much more pulled in, something superhero movies are really lacking these days that used to be far more common. The villain storyline is great, and really aids the other storylines about what Peter is going through personally and emotionally.

While it's hard to decide if this is Tom Holland's absolute best work in the MCU, as he also did great work in *No Way Home*, it's most definitely Zendaya's best. MJ has now been to and graduated from college, has a new boyfriend, and is completely unaware of all past memories with Peter. Despite her being unaware, she still ends up in danger through her association with him, which is nothing short of confusing for her. From there, she has some great material that shows off her range in a way she hasn't really been given before.

Sadie Sink and Jon Bernthal were also both great additions, each adding something to Peter's journey through this movie that was needed for his own personal growth.

A lot of this movie is going to feel familiar to people. The tropes are still there; the beats we've seen many times before are still there. Yet, the fact that it felt so refreshing at the same time is what really makes the film shine. Destin Daniel Cretton being one of the MCU's best directors was a big reason for that. Every step of the way, this movie felt directed by a director instead of a studio. Even if they feel familiar, we need more of these. We need these scaled-back movies where it really feels like our protagonist is the focus, and not the big Earth-ending threat or the multiverse. We need more character studies, where we really get to know our characters instead of them just being a replaceable hero off to fight a no-substance villain. Let the bigger movies feel like a big event again, with all of these small ones casually leading up to it.

This movie won't reinvent the superhero genre, but it will be the movie that fans of simpler comic book movies, fans who think the MCU has gone off the rails with the multiverse and making every movie feel like it has to tie into every other movie, will find to be a refreshing return to form.
