August is the most exciting month of the awards calendar. It's the last month before the fall fests ramp up, the trailers are dropping by the day, all the anticipation, all the word-of-mouth, and we're all itching to find out what's going to be a competitor, and what's going to be a non-starter. Best of all, it marks the month that the game of Fantasy Filmball kicks off. August is essentially the equivalent of when the March Madness bracket is revealed. No team has played yet, but they're all dreaming of the same thing: the gold trophy. So before the festivals kick off, while the possibilities seem endless, I thought it'd be as good a time as any to explore the one category that truly looks like anybody's game: Best Actor.

It's no surprise that Lead Actor looks as stacked as it does at this point in the season; nine of the ten films the Fantasy Filmball community has predicted for a Best Picture nomination has a male protagonist. From veteran comebacks to overdue legends and rising stars, this category is ripe with narratives that will see awards season journalists and campaign strategists have an absolute field day this winter. Unlike the Best Actress category, which will likely see at least a couple of actresses from non-Best Picture films get a nomination, Best Actor will be absolutely stacked, as long as the films we expect to be awards season darlings follow through with expectations.

Without further ado, let's meet our contenders...

<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Tom Cruise</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Digger</em> &middot; Alejandro G. Iñárritu · Warner Bros.</div>

**The actor:** A man that needs no introduction, Cruise is widely considered one of the last true movie stars of his generation. He has three acting Oscar nominations to his name, the last one being in the year 2000 for his supporting role in *Magnolia*.

**The role:** Cruise plays Digger Rockwell, a powerful oil tycoon, in Alejandro González Iñárritu's black comedy satire, *Digger*. The film will skip the fall festivals and premiere in theatres on October 2nd.

**Why it's happening:** The Oscars love a transformative performance, and Cruise going from *Mission: Impossible* and *Top Gun* action hero to an old man covered in multi-Academy Award winning makeup artist Kazu Hiro's makeup in a comedy certainly qualifies. He has that overdue narrative (and no, that Honorary Oscar doesn't count), and it also helps that Iñárritu has directed ten actors to Oscar nominations, including Leonardo DiCaprio, who won his overdue Oscar for *The Revenant*.

**Why it's not happening:** The trailer for the film looks wild, to say the least. Iñárritu takes big swings, and though they've mostly all been successes thus far, he's due a single movie in his filmography to miss at the Oscars. If *Digger* bombs, Cruise will sink with the ship.

<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 02</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">John Malkovich</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Wild Horse Nine</em> &middot; Martin McDonagh · Searchlight</div>

**The actor:** Malkovich is an industry legend, with two Best Supporting Actor nominations to his name: one for *Places in the Heart* (1984) and the other for *In the Line of Fire* (1993).

**The role:** Malkovich co-stars with Sam Rockwell in Martin McDonagh's *Wild Horse Nine*, about a couple of American CIA agents who are sent to Easter Island shortly before the 1973 Chilean coup. The film will premiere at Venice before heading to theatres in November.

**Why it's happening:** Like Cruise, Malkovich also has an overdue narrative. Unlike Cruise, it's also a comeback narrative, given that he hasn't been in the Oscars conversation since the mid-90s. But he's in good hands: McDonagh's last two films have received a combined seven(!) acting Oscar nominations and two acting wins.

**Why it's not happening:** He's barely received any recognition as a leading actor before. Though this could add to his overdue narrative, it could also be the case that the Academy has never really seen Malkovich as Best Actor-worthy. Also, though pundits are speculating that the film's campaign team will place him in Lead and Rockwell in Supporting, this has yet to be confirmed.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 03</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Matt Damon</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>The Odyssey</em> &middot; Christopher Nolan · Universal</div>

<img src="/posters/the-odyssey-damon.jpg" alt="Matt Damon in The Odyssey" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />

**The actor:** One of the biggest stars since his breakout hit *Good Will Hunting*, for which he won the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay. He's received three acting nominations throughout his career, but no performance of his has ever won.

**The role:** Damon plays Odysseus in Nolan's follow-up to Best Picture winner *Oppenheimer*.

**Why it's happening:** Unlike Cruise and Malkovich, whose films haven't premiered yet, Damon's performance and his film's quality are known quantities. *The Odyssey* is currently our frontrunner for Best Picture, and primed to be the tally leader come nominations morning. With glowing reviews and a killer box office, it's hard to see its lead actor missing.

**Why it's not happening:** For a filmmaker as prolific as Nolan, he doesn't actually have the best track record at getting his actors nominated. Only two of his nine Oscar-nominated films have received an acting nomination. *Oppenheimer* could easily be seen as the exception, given it's a World War II drama biopic, but among his action-adventure films (which would include *The Odyssey*), it's really just Heath Ledger who broke into the Oscar lineup, and he was a supporting actor. Perhaps Damon will get snubbed as DiCaprio famously did 16 years ago for *Inception*.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 04</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Ryan Gosling</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Project Hail Mary</em> &middot; Phil Lord & Chris Miller · Amazon MGM</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/project-hail-mary-gosling.jpg" alt="Ryan Gosling in Project Hail Mary" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Ryan Gosling as Ryland Grace in <em>Project Hail Mary</em></figcaption>

**The actor:** One of Hollywood's biggest stars working today, Gosling has racked up three Oscar nominations so far in his career. He was arguably in second place for his supporting role as Ken in 2023's *Barbie*, but no one was stopping Downey Jr. that year.

**The role:** He plays scientist Ryland Grace in Lord and Miller's *Project Hail Mary*, based on the popular sci-fi novel by Andy Weir, in which he has to befriend and learn to communicate with an alien to save mankind.

**Why it's happening:** Last season, we saw Michael B. Jordan win an Oscar for a spring-release genre flick and box office darling. Though I don't think anyone expects Gosling to win for his performance, a nomination seems likely. For most of the film's runtime, Grace is the only human character we see onscreen. Matt Damon got nominated for *The Martian*, another solo performance in a sci-fi Weir adaptation, and by the looks of things, *Project Hail Mary* is even more beloved than *The Martian* was.

**Why it's not happening:** Despite Jordan's win, blockbuster performances don't often get Academy Award nominations. Not only will Amazon MGM need to keep the movie relevant all the way to January, but it'll also have to fend off the swath of contenders highlighted in this article that will be fresher on voters' minds.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 05</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Pedro Pascal</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Behemoth!</em> &middot; Tony Gilroy · Searchlight</div>

<img src="/posters/behemoth-pascal.jpg" alt="Pedro Pascal in Behemoth!" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Pedro Pascal in <em>Behemoth!</em></figcaption>

**The actor:** Pedro Pascal has established himself as one of the biggest stars in Hollywood in recent years. Though the Chilean has starred in many acclaimed television series such as *Game of Thrones* and *The Mandalorian*, he's yet to star in a big Oscar contender.

**The role:** He plays a renowned cellist returning to Los Angeles to compose film scores in Tony Gilroy's *Behemoth!*, which premieres at NYFF before its December release.

**Why it's happening:** The film has been generating a lot of buzz, and Pascal seems due for his first nomination. If *Behemoth!* gets into the Best Picture lineup, it's safe to say that Pascal will be in the conversation.

**Why it's not happening:** Because Pascal has never been an awards contender before (at least in film), his nomination really hedges on the film getting into Best Picture. And even then, it's possible the Academy will think he needs to pay his dues before getting his first nomination. Also, Gilroy has only directed three films prior to *Behemoth!*, and only *Michael Clayton* has been a big Oscar player, which was almost twenty years ago.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 06</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Andrew Scott</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Elsinore</em> &middot; Simon Stone · Focus Features</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/elsinore-scott.jpg" alt="Andrew Scott in Elsinore" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Andrew Scott as Ian Charleson in <em>Elsinore</em></figcaption>

**The actor:** Scott is an Irish stage, film, and television actor who gained prominence for his role as Moriarty in the series *Sherlock*, before starring in the shows *Fleabag* and *Ripley*, and the film *All of Us Strangers*.

**The role:** In *Elsinore*, he plays the Scottish actor Ian Charleson, star of Best Picture winners *Chariots of Fire* and *Gandhi*, during his battle with AIDS. The film is widely speculated to be premiering at Telluride, though it will also be playing at TIFF and the Opening Night slot at the London Film Festival.

**Why it's happening:** The role is, to put it vulgarly, Oscar bait. Scott will be playing an actor, who has ties to the Academy, as he rehearses scenes from *Hamlet* during the last months of his life. An actor as talented as Scott will almost certainly receive rave reviews playing such a juicy role. At this point, he's probably the frontrunner at BAFTA.

**Why it's not happening:** Again, this category is so stacked that any film that fails to get a Best Picture nomination will struggle to keep their lead actor in the conversation, as great as the performance may be. And like Pascal, Scott is mostly known for his roles in television, and may need to flirt more with the Academy before feeling due for a nomination.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 07</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">John Turturro</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>The Only Living Pickpocket in New York</em> &middot; Noah Segan</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/the-only-living-pickpocket-in-new-york-turturro.jpg" alt="John Turturro in The Only Living Pickpocket in New York" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>John Turturro in <em>The Only Living Pickpocket in New York</em></figcaption>

**The actor:** Star of several Coen brothers movies, Turturro is a veteran of the industry. Like Malkovich, he hasn't appeared in many awards-adjacent films this century, but has several iconic roles dating back to the '80s.

**The role:** He plays an aging pickpocket in the crime thriller *The Only Living Pickpocket in New York*, director Noah Segan's sophomore feature, which premiered back in Sundance.

**Why it's happening:** Unlike many of the actors in this list, Turturro is a known quantity, having gotten rave reviews at Sundance. Having never been nominated before, he's overdue, and the role also represents a career comeback.

**Why it's not happening:** Because it's a known quantity, we also know that the film is unlikely to get a Best Picture nomination. Turturro is the standout, so if he gets in, he'll probably be a lone nominee for his film, which is a tough ask in any year, let alone in one as competitive as this.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 08</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Robert Pattinson</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Primetime</em> &middot; Lance Oppenheim</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/primetime-pattinson.jpg" alt="Robert Pattinson in Primetime" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Robert Pattinson as Chris Hansen in <em>Primetime</em></figcaption>
</figure>

**The actor:** Pattinson went from a teen heartthrob in *Twilight* and *Harry Potter* before taking on more obscure indie projects. His arc has taken him to being one of the most sought-after stars in Hollywood, starring in many of the year's biggest films.

**The role:** He plays Chris Hansen in Lance Oppenheim's *Primetime*, about the controversial host of the hit series *To Catch a Predator*. The film will premiere at the Venice Film Festival.

**Why it's happening:** The latest trailer, which came out last week, was the talk of the town, with Pattinson's uncanny portrayal of Hansen being one of the highlights. Playing a recognisable figure is always helpful for a nomination, and Pattinson is having a killer year, starring in the likes of *The Drama*, *The Odyssey* and *Dune: Part Three*. The Academy may want to reward him with a nomination.

**Why it's not happening:** This is Oppenheim's narrative feature debut, so it's hard to predict how good it's going to be. The trailer is reminiscent of 2014's *Nightcrawler*, where Jake Gyllenhaal gave a career-best performance that was famously snubbed at the Oscars. It isn't hard to imagine Pattinson suffering the same fate.

Who knows what the next two months will hold? Could half of these names face the chopping block? Could the list get even bigger, with perhaps a Jeffrey Wright-esque contender appearing out of nowhere? Time will tell, but from the looks of things, Best Actor seems poised to be as competitive as it's ever been. Here's where my ballot sits today:

But I don't think anyone's safe. If Malkovich, Cruise, Scott, and Pattinson are all home runs, even Damon could face a DiCaprio in *Killers of the Flower Moon* or De Niro in *The Irishman*-level snub. That's the beauty of August. It's anybody's game; may the best actor win.
