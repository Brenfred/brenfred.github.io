John Turturro plays Harry Lehman, an older pickpocket who is stuck in his old timer ways. He doesn't know how to use the technology of today and still primarily uses cash for payment when most have moved on to credit cards. Turturro is perfect casting for this role with a subtle yet authentic performance. He's not overbearing with the display of emotion, but it's always present under the surface. I was always invested in the character and what he was going to do next, and that's largely in part to Turturro's natural screen presence.

The setup is rather simple. We see Harry trying to sell the items he has pickpocketed, before learning he has finally chosen the wrong person to steal from, and has to go on another journey to right his wrong. Despite that simplicity, it's still engaging. Even if it's a more relaxed film than people would expect, it brings you on the journey and allows you to live in it.

The main potential knock against the film is how familiar it all feels. The themes and explorations of personal relationships don't feel entirely new. Regardless, that didn't hold back my enjoyment. The film found ways to feel so personal in its own exploration of these themes that it's arguably one of the better versions of this type of story.

Noah Segan, director and screenwriter, helped the movie breeze by. It comes in just under 90 minutes which is the perfect length for a more straightforward movie. Most movies today that should be that short aren't, which makes the ones that are feel refreshing. Segan also captures the feeling of current day New York, but from the perspective of an older resident quite effectively. It almost feels like it could have been set decades earlier if it wasn't for the current day technology that drives the plot.

The heart of the movie is in the personal relationships and stakes. Harry cares a lot for his bedridden wife, and would do anything for her. His whole motivation throughout all of this is to keep her safe. It drives the stakes in a more interesting way than if it was just about his own survival. His methods might not be right, but it's all he knows. Despite his flaws, he has his heart in the right place even if he doesn't always show it. This becomes more apparent as we get to know more about the other important people in his life, including his daughter and a police detective.

Tatiana Maslany gives the best performance in the film despite being in only a single scene. The estranged relationship between father and daughter is nothing new, but Maslany attacks the scene in such a heart breaking way. It's the most I felt for anyone throughout the entire film's runtime. While I almost wanted more, it didn't need more, and is one of the best one scene performances in recent memory.

The third act is by far the most effective, and it all adds up to a satisfying conclusion. The final shot is memorable and leaves the film on a good note. The final impression of a movie is one of the most important, and this one left me feeling very satisfied by the journey I just experienced.
