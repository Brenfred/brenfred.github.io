When I first heard about this movie, I very much felt like it was made with me in mind: starring one of my favorite actors working today, co-starring a member of a band I was obsessed with as a kid, accompanied by fun music. I was in.

At times, those elements really shine through. The song that leads this movie is actually quite great, even if we hear it just a tad too much. The other songs in the movie, whether they were covers of classics or sneak peeks at the original songs these characters write, were also quite fun and could have been used to even greater advantage. Whenever music was playing, I was locked in.

Paul Rudd and Nick Jonas are surprisingly a perfect match for something like this. They both have a similar charm, something about their natural personalities that makes it easy to believe these two characters would get along when they first meet. Their scenes together are the best scenes of the movie.

That said, the story struggled to land the way it intended, partly due to some narrative choices that felt odd. When Paul Rudd's Rick first plays the song for his family that Nick Jonas's Danny Wilson has stolen from him, his family doesn't remember the song, nor do they even seem to believe him. The same goes for his bandmates and other people in his life. It's easy enough to see that other people would react this way, but it was harder to understand the characterization of his wife and daughter.

Rachel, Rick's wife, played by Marcella Plunkett, and Aja, his daughter, played by Beth Fallon, are so uninterested in Rick's actual musical dreams and attempts to make music, either believing he's not good or that his aspirations are too far out of reach. The movie doesn't sell us on the idea that he's bad enough for his own family to be that disconnected. Even when he plays Aja a demo of a song that is at least decent, if not a little stereotypical, she acts like even having to listen to it for a moment is some huge burden. Maybe if it were just her, the attitude could be chalked up to teenage behavior, but that's not really how it's portrayed, given Rachel's complete disinterest, too.

No one believing him is what is supposed to drive him feeling as crazy about all of this as he does, but the disconnect in buying that none of these characters would believe him makes it feel too unearned.

There are also times where the narrative feels all over the place, like there's no consistent throughline of where the action is going. We're doing one thing, and then suddenly we're doing another. This is most prevalent when our leads aren't together, and we have to go back and forth between them in different countries doing different things. The two main times the leads are in the same location are where the good stuff is.

Thankfully, the charm outweighs the issues. Anytime we were back at the musical moments or anytime our two leads got to interact with their antics, I was reminded exactly why I thought this movie would be for me in the first place. It's why I wouldn't complain about watching this again if someone wanted me to.
