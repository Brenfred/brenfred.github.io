**“That’s not enough.”**

I wish I could remember where I originally heard someone discuss this so I might credit them, but the following take is one I’ve often seen repeated and that I generally agree with. One of my favorite things about the early entries of the *Mission: Impossible* franchise is that each film brought in a new director. The series consisted of different filmmakers translating similar action beats in their own way, bringing their stamp and creative vision to the Cruise action vehicles such that each film always feels right at home in the library of that filmmaker’s work, despite still remaining tonally consistent with the rest of the franchise. It defined the series as a sandbox where any action auteur could play around in and show off what they’d like to do.

The reason I bring this up is because I feel that the recent *Evil Dead* entries have taken a similar direction, except through horror instead of action. And I can’t help but delight in it.

The prior two entries of the post-Raimi series have allowed Fede Álvarez and Lee Cronin to play in the toolbox that Sam set out for them decades ago, and they created some addictively enjoyable thrill rides that I’ve quite enjoyed discovering over the past few years. Now, it’s Sébastien Vaniček’s turn to demonstrate what he wants to say in the world of horror, and here he clearly defines himself as a voice worth listening to in the future.

The *Evil Dead* movies have always been, at their core, fun. Crafting a strong entry in the franchise requires a sense of glee, whether it be through gore so absurd that you can’t help but grin along with the deadites at the copious buckets of blood, or the sardonic wit of the demons that hits with a surprising amount of levity at unexpected turns. *Evil Dead Burn* has both of these elements in spades, and from the first five minutes, you can see how clearly this film has learned at the altar of Sam Raimi just how to tonally balance itself to remain consistent with that which came before it. Strap in, because the ride starts fast, and it truly never slows down, so if you aren’t ready to go from the opening logos, you never will be!

Phillip Lozano’s cinematography feels distinctly inspired by past entries; if it’s a solid object, you can attach a camera to it, dolly it, rotate it, or move it through the air with the aggressive focus of a supernatural entity. This, along with the absolutely manic editing from Maxime Caro, lends to a movie that truly keeps you breathless, because there honestly isn’t a moment to catch your breath. Everything is pedal to the metal. There’s no time to stop and ponder or slow down. We are GOING straight for the finish line... and I was smiling like a maniac the whole way through because, honestly, that’s all I could ask for out of a film like this. If you’re going to put me on a roller coaster, you better pump that accelerator as fast as you possibly can.

Having said that, I would be remiss to label this film a new masterpiece in the world of horror. While Souheila Yacoub turns in a perfectly serviceable performance as a horror protagonist, she simply doesn’t have that pull or likability that Bruce Campbell or even Jane Levy have brought to past entries. The rest of the characters somewhat blur together, making it hard to really root for any of them other than through a vague interest in seeing gory happenings occur to them. There’s an attempt at some interesting thematic commentary here about abusive relationships and the effect they have on us, and in some moments this really does start to sing, but there is sadly not any real attempt to gain depth into this concept or flesh it out beyond the bare minimum. This isn’t a movie interested in having something profound to say, which is fine, but I did feel a little teased by the potential during some moments where I almost wished we did slow down a bit and get to know our characters a bit better.

While this does run a little long for my tastes, to the point where I’d argue it might run out of steam by the end, overall I had a delightful time in the theater with this. It’s meant to be seen with big handfuls of popcorn and your friend who loves to watch splatterfests, and in that it succeeds admirably. The *Evil Dead* franchise has yet to produce a dud, in my opinion, and *Burn* continues a wonderful tradition of guaranteeing a dumb, fun horror jawn for anyone who needs it.

Also, there’s a credit song that bumps hard. It may make me a fan of French rap.
