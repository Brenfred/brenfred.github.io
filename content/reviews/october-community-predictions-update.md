<h2>To Digg or To Die?</h2>

Throughout the year, Alejandro González Iñárritu’s *Digger* was one of the most anticipated films on the awards circuit, with the Warner Bros. release promising a treat for cinephiles looking for something outside the usual studio fare. If this reminds you of a certain 2025 Best Picture winner, you aren’t alone.

At the time of the last update, the film sat in the bottom half at number 8, with some astute server members seeing the writing on the wall, or the social reactions on the X timeline, for a divisive artistic swing that would ultimately upset just as many viewers as it pleased. On the bright side, Iñárritu’s latest sparked lively debate, with many celebrities coming out in favor of the film, including fellow directors Alfonso Cuarón and Guillermo del Toro. Overall, though, the picture is bleak: a huge money loser for Warner Bros., its final release ever, ravaged by critics, holding a 46 on Metacritic, and presumably left to rot with so many other Oscar hopefuls of the past.

The film is now predicted for just three nominations in total, down from five above-the-line in our August predictions: Best Actor for Tom Cruise, Production Design, and Makeup. While Cruise will certainly receive a push and the Makeup nomination for veteran Kazu Hiro is expected to stay in play, I’d expect it to fall further as the season comes into focus.

One particularly interesting race to watch going forward is Best Actor, as a consensus four has solidified, leaving Cruise to battle various challengers, headlined by Cinderella story Anthony Ippolito, for the last spot.

<h2>Telluride’s Mystery Contender Rises Further</h2>

Towards the end of this year’s Telluride Film Festival, rumors abounded about what the cryptically announced secret screening would be. It ran three hours, featured big celebrities, and required attendees to lock their phones away for the duration. What could possibly be this exciting that we didn’t already know about? As it turned out, it was a bombshell of an announcement. Erstwhile comedian Nathan Fielder (*The Rehearsal*) and up-and-comer Lance Oppenheim (*Primetime*) filmed a documentary in secret, examining the last days before tech pariah Elizabeth Holmes was sent to prison for defrauding investors of millions of dollars.

This latest update was published on October 15, one day before *You Can See Everything* opened in approximately 850 theaters around America. It rose three places in Best Picture, from 18 to 15, and two slots in Best Documentary, from 4 to 2, but I expect the film to keep climbing as its unprecedented buzz and box office numbers continue to build.

A contender quite like this has never been seen before. Opening to a strong $15 million in about 800 theaters before expanding to wide release this weekend, the long, talky documentary/metafictional psychological study has become an internet obsession. The biggest question for awards season: can this be the first documentary in history to score a Best Picture nomination?

An unusual contender in many ways, the film currently sits at number 15 in the community predictions for Picture, number 2 for Documentary, and number 8 for Editing and Cinematography. In the next update, I expect it to rise in all of these, break into the top 10 for Best Director, and maybe even land an additional surprise category! Given the historic nature of these nominations and the small potential nomination package, predicting this film is admittedly a long shot, but that hasn’t stopped many of our community members from jumping aboard A24’s hype train!

<h2>Fall Fests Come to a Close</h2>

NYFF has come and gone, and with it, all the major fall festival contenders have premiered. The two biggest stories to come out of New York: *Behemoth!*, the musical drama starring Pedro Pascal, premiered to lukewarm praise, while OpenAI origin story *Artificial* elicited slightly positive responses overall.

These two films are a tale of differing expectations. Despite similar scores on Letterboxd and Metacritic (albeit with *Artificial* faring slightly better on both), the former fell seven slots in the Best Picture ranking, while the latter rose six. Before the fall fests, Tony Gilroy’s film was expected to be a solid bottom-tier contender, while Luca Guadagnino’s Fincher-inspired dramedy drew heavy skepticism, especially when considering how Amazon completely dropped the film and Luca’s past Oscar failures.

Although the reviews for *Behemoth!* look solid on paper, few critics or audience members have shown much passion for it, leaving it as a fall-season contender that slowly fades as other films put up a stronger fight. It now sits at number 5 in the community predictions for both Score and Sound, fitting for its focus on the music of Hollywood. Importantly, there are still conflicting reports on the score’s Oscar eligibility, so clarification here could push it higher in the rankings. Pedro Pascal currently ranks number 8 for Best Actor, while the film joined the race at number 7 in Song, and is hanging in at number 10 for Original Screenplay. Its chances aren’t completely dead on the vine just yet, but things do not look good for a film that doesn’t have a particularly strong audience base with either critics or the general public.

As for *Artificial*, results of the New York screenings were a bit of a surprise. Many have cautioned against predicting Luca Guadagnino films: *Challengers*, *Queer*, and *After the Hunt* all had strong cases in the past two years, and all three ended up earning a grand total of 0 nominations at the big show. *After the Hunt*, in particular, was heavily predicted by much of the awards community, only to completely flame out. However, by tackling such a hot-button issue as the rise of AI, he may have finally figured out how to appeal to voters. Interestingly, some reviewers have also noted that the film feels much more middlebrow than Guadagnino’s previous efforts, which could actually be a good sign for its awards chances. There are signs for worry, though: several reviews note tonal inconsistency, flipping between serious drama and broad comedy, and, overall, the film pales in comparison to its generational forebear, *The Social Network*. Beyond its number 12 ranking for Best Picture, *Artificial* enters the race at number 8 for Original Screenplay and number 6 for Best Supporting Actor (Andrew Garfield), while holding the fort at number 9 in Makeup. Watch this space, as the film has so far only played for a NYFF audience primed to root against AI’s cultural dominance.

<h2>Other Tidbits and Factoids</h2>

<h3>Which Neon?</h3>

*All of a Sudden* and *Fjord* currently rank 9 and 10 for Best Picture, and just outside Best Director at 6 and 7, respectively. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto round out their respective fives in the acting categories, while Renate Reinsve represents *Fjord* at number 7 in Best Actress. Over in the Screenplay and International Feature categories, both films appear again. These rankings do not accurately reflect the general consensus, as most believe that Neon will be able to manage multiple nominations for only one of their non-English Cannes award winners. The problem is, nobody agrees on which one. Will one Neon contender rise to the top? Will neither surface as *Artificial* takes Neon’s #1 spot? This will be one of the most fascinating stories to watch as the season progresses.

<h3>Possible Breakthrough</h3>

Netflix’s second-place finisher for the TIFF International People’s Choice Award, Lee Chang-dong’s latest feature, rides stellar reviews and growing confidence in its chances as an unconventional Oscar film. History-making *Parasite* may still be the only South Korean feature to ever receive any Oscar nominations, but this talky class commentary and relationship drama has received glowing praise everywhere it’s gone thus far, and is backed by Netflix’s sturdy awards team (although rumors suggest that the streamer has recently laid off sections of said team). It rose five places to number 13 in Best Picture, rose to number 9 in Best Actress for Jeon Do-yeon and in Original Screenplay, entered Best Director at number 10, and held steady at number 4 in International Feature.

<h3>TIFF Biopic Corner</h3>

Two crowd-pleasing, inspirational TIFF premieres surprised many by placing in second and third, respectively, for the TIFF People’s Choice Award, just before our last update. Having been awarded over many perceived stronger contenders, the community is still deciding how to handle these two. As with Neon’s Palme contenders, most believe one of these will place in Best Picture, but which one will it be? On the one hand, *I Play Rocky* dramatizes the making of a classic Best Picture winner and is a legitimate box-office threat, but on the other, *Being Heumann* represents Apple’s main contender for 2026 and offers a rousing story about a historical legal battle for disabled rights. While the *Green Book* director’s film fares better in the main category (11 to 15), the *CODA* helmer delivers a more enticing breakout performance, with Ruth Madeley outpacing Anthony Ippolito at number 4 in Best Actress and the young fighter at number 6 in Best Actor. In casting, the battle is neck and neck: Heumann 6, Rocky 7. These two films add to an unusually large slate of bottom-tier contenders, making for an exciting season as we head for the first precursors.

<h2>The Bottom Line</h2>

After the fall festivals, the overall picture remains muddy as contenders both underperformed and sprung up out of nowhere. Even *Digger* still has its defenders! *The Odyssey* and *La Bola Negra* look strong atop the mountain, but there are many slots still up for grabs. This season is poised to be unusually exciting as we look for records to be broken and new trends to arise.

As the year draws to a close and the first awards bodies start revealing their nominees, we will be paying close attention to things such as Neon’s slate, the foreign language contenders, the TIFF crowd-pleasers, and especially how *You Can See Everything* fares. Happy Halloween, and happy predicting!
