Since 2019, the end of October has marked one of my favorite weeks of the year because the Virginia Film Festival (VAFF) kicks off in historic Charlottesville. Over the last seven years, VAFF has showcased an array of cinema from around the world for every type of movie fan. Want to see the next Best Picture winner? They played *Parasite* and *Nomadland*. Want to see a film with some of Hollywood’s biggest stars? They have you covered, with Mikey Madison attached to a Q&A for *Anora* or Miles Caton performing his hit songs from *Sinners*. Want to see some of the world’s most acclaimed films at a festival? They’ve got you with Cannes Award Winners like *The Worst Person in the World* or Venice winners like *All the Beauty and the Bloodshed*. And guess what? The Virginia Film Festival even gets some U.S. premieres, as it did in 2023 with Ava DuVernay’s *Origin*.

And instead of giving you a breakdown of all the great movies highlighted in this year’s lineup, because I would highly encourage you, if you live in the Virginia area or surrounding states and are open to the travel, to check out their press release that breaks down over 100 titles across 19 different series and themes being highlighted at this year’s festival… Instead, I wanna take a different approach to informing you why this festival has had my heart for the last eight years and why I’m very sad that I will have to end my streak of attending this year, but let you know what my ideal schedule and lineup would look like if I could.

Kicking things off, opening night is Wednesday, October 21, and as the festival has introduced over the last few years, you really have a good amount of options for how you would like to kick off your festival. Since 2023, Virginia has offered multiple choices for opening night, and this year includes documentaries such as *Time Warp*, a love letter to queer joy and the story of the first shadow-cast performance of *The Rocky Horror Picture Show*. They also bolster a fantastic trio of international cinema, such as *The Blood Count*, a highly camp vegetarian vampire flick out of Austria; the Indian film *Rehmat*, which focuses on three interconnected stories capturing grief and displacement in 1940s India and Pakistan; and the Best Director recipient out of the Venice Film Festival and the runner-up International People’s Choice Award out of the Toronto International Film Festival, Lee Chang-dong’s *Possible Love*.

If you’re a major fan of seeing films you may not have easy access to at a film festival, any of these movies would be a great choice. *Possible Love*, especially, catches my eye, but I am a sucker for a party, and every year I’ve gone to VAFF, I’ve made it my mission to go to the opening-night movie because that’s where the festival's energy really kicks off. This year, VAFF has lined up an absolutely hilarious film to get the party started: Jesse Eisenberg’s follow-up to his Oscar-winning *A Real Pain*, *The Debut*, which stars Julianne Moore, giving her career-best performance as a shy housewife looking to broaden her horizons with musical theater, with a cast that includes Paul Giamatti, Halle Bailey, and Eisenberg himself.

I would find myself in a similar situation in 2024, when I had already seen the opening-night film of *Anora* but couldn’t pass up a Q&A with Mikey Madison. And while *The Debut* does not boast a Q&A, being in that Charlottesville crowd with a sold-out audience at the Paramount Theater who just want to laugh and have a great time is something that is truly unmatched, and I would love to rewatch *The Debut* or, in the case of most people, see this film for the first time in a packed house.

Moving on to Thursday, October 22, festivalgoers can slot three different films into their day. Here’s where my article starts to leave a few films out, though, because I can’t cover all 100+ movies; as I mentioned before, I highly urge you to check out the website and find the schedule that works best for you.

But for this first film in Thursday’s slot, I'm leaning toward the Violet Crown, an amazing indie theater chain with a location in Charlottesville. You get recliners, you get AC, and they have pretty tasty popcorn as well, but to see the John Turturro acting vehicle from Sony Pictures Classics, *The Only Living Pickpocket in New York*, described as an aging con man who has found his hardest job yet, trying to figure out the best way to give something back.

If seeing John Turturro deliver a very good performance isn’t up your alley, you could go to a Screen Talk with director James Gray, famous for *Ad Astra*, *Armageddon Time*, and his new film *Paper Tiger*, which is showing in the next time slots on the Thursday schedule. So if you are a big James Gray fan, you have a match made in heaven. However, I know I will have the chance to see *Paper Tiger* very soon*,* with its November release date. Instead, I gravitate more toward the Sundance Audience & Grand Jury Prize winner *Josephine*, directed by Beth De Araújo, described as a heartbreaking masterpiece and starring Channing Tatum, Gemma Chan, and young actress Mason Reeves. All three have been applauded for giving phenomenal performances. But if you’re someone who would love to see a phenomenal performance from Andrew Scott, with *Elsinore* being the talk of the town at both the Telluride and the Toronto International Film Festival for his performance, you have another option.

This Thursday night slot is simply impossible to select. If you’re a documentary fan, *No More Punk* and *Daughter of the Force* could catch your eye. If you are a fan of Asian cinema, *My Name Is Cherry* and *Virgin* are slotted here. However, if you love yourself a Q&A, Academy Award winner Aaron Sorkin and Academy Award-nominated producer Todd Black will partake in a Q&A for *The Social Reckoning* at the Paramount Theater. That ticket, I imagine, will be hard to get.

But instead, I’d consider two other options. I could spend my whole day at the Violet Crown and see Germany’s submission for the International Feature category at the Academy Awards, *Everytime*, or my choice for this time slot, which has been hailed as one of the funniest movies of the year: Jordan Firstman’s directorial debut, which he also stars in and wrote, called *Club Kid*. As I mentioned before, Virginia is amazing at hosting Q&As, as the film’s composer, Cristobal Tapia de Veer, will be on hand for a post-screening Q&A.

After wrapping up an insanely difficult schedule on Thursday, we face even more challenges on Friday, October 23. Just like yesterday, festivalgoers can log three movies to their festival slates, and just like on Thursday, the afternoon slot could include a conversation with one of Hollywood's biggest names. Tony Award, multiple-time Academy Award, and Emmy Award-winning Jessica Lange will be on hand for a Q&A at the Irving Theatre.

You could see the Critics’ Week Grand Prize winner from the Cannes Film Festival, *La Gravida* from France, or you could see one of the buzziest titles out of the Venice Film Festival, the Telluride Film Festival, and the Toronto Film Festival: the newest movie from Academy Award nominee Martin McDonagh, starring Sam Rockwell, Steve Buscemi, and John Malkovich. Malkovich is receiving best-of-the-year praise and won the Volpi Cup at Venice for his leading performance as a CIA agent on a journey of self-discovery on a mission in *Wild Horse Nine*.

After *Wild Horse Nine*, we move into the midday selection of films, and if you are a massive fan of Jessica Lange, there is a Q&A attached to her film *Long Day’s Journey into Night*. What catches my eye in this mid-afternoon time slot is another acting vehicle. This one is Austria’s selection for the Academy Awards for Best International Feature, starring Silver Bear winner Sandra Hüller, delivering yet another phenomenal performance in a year that has already seen her receive raves for *Project Hail Mary*. This film, being *Rose*, is showing at the Violet Crown Theater.

Closing out my Friday, there are four amazing choices from around the globe: *Minotaur*, the official selection out of France and the Grand Prix winner from the Cannes Film Festival from celebrated exiled Russian director, Andrey Zvyagintsev, the Belgian selection for the Academy Awards for Best International Feature and the Best Actor winner at the Cannes Film Festival, Lucas Dhont’s follow ups to one of my favorite VAFF watches, *Close*, with his new film, *Coward*, a Canadian documentary which received the Documentary Grand Jury Prize out of the Sundance Film Festival, *Nuisance Bar*, or my selection at the Paramount Theater, U.S. director Tony Gilroy’s newest drama, starring Pedro Pascal, Will Arnett, Eva Victor, and Olivia Wilde, *Behemoth!.*

*Behemoth!* follows a cellist returning home and immersing himself in his newest project while rediscovering new and old connections. What really grabs my attention about *Behemoth!* Is that of course seeing Pedro Pascal give one of his career-best performances… but even more so is the fact that nine composers have teamed up to form the Behemoth collective to compose the original score for this film, and that just sounds amazing. I cannot wait to open my ears and enjoy whatever they are cooking up.

Moving into Saturday, October 24, and if it isn’t apparent yet, this is where things get really impossible: you could fit four, maybe five films on this day, but to do so, you would have to pass up some amazing options.

Right off the bat, the morning slot: you could spend three hours and 15 minutes for a ride with Ryusuke Hamaguchi’s newest, the Japanese submission for the Oscar for Best International Feature, *All of a Sudden*, which is a deeply humanistic, philosophical, and moving film about an elderly care facility director and a Japanese leader, director, and patient living with terminal cancer.

But the downside of going to *All of a Sudden* is that it kind of takes up two whole slots for films. *All of a Sudden* won a Cannes festival award for Best Actress for both leads, and it’s one I am definitely excited to see. Festival time is so cherished that choosing a longer film like this would mean you couldn’t see films like the animated *Julian*, the Toronto People’s Choice runner-up *Being Heumann* from the Academy Award-winning director of *CODA*, you couldn’t see Danny Boyle’s newest *Ink*, about the rise and the explosive birth of the Sun News publication, starring Jack O’Connell and Guy Pearce, and you would have to miss out on Sophie Okonedo’s critically beloved performance in *Mouse*.

Personally, I would like to stack two of those movies on my slate. The hard part is… that is not even possible. *Being Heumann* overlaps with both *Ink* and *Mouse*, and you could do *Julian* and either of those movies, but then you would miss what I most anticipated from this first half of Saturday’s slate: the IFC animated feature *Tangles*, starring Bryan Cranston and Julia Louis-Dreyfus, in a very stacked ensemble, which is a film dealing with Alzheimer’s and how it plays a part in a family at large.

So to make *Tangles* work at 2 PM at the Violet Crown, I'm actually just going to see *All of a* *Sudden* beforehand and stay in the same theater, seeing two highly acclaimed movies from the Cannes Film Festival. While it is a mighty price to pay, that’s why you come to the Virginia Film Festival… to see something magical.

By seeing *Tangles* the buzziest film on the fall film festival circuit plays in this next slot over at the Culbreth Theater: the People’s Choice Award winner at the Toronto International Film Festival, the Best Director winner at the Cannes Film Festival, and the film that placed in the top three of the Critics’ Poll at the Telluride Film Festival, Netflix’s *La Bola Negra*, a film that has been hailed as one of the best achievements of 2026 at large and is the Spanish selection for the Academy Awards.

If I could only see one film at this festival, *La Bola Negra* would be my choice. I am a little surprised it is not at the Paramount Theater. Netflix typically has its big awards-season play come to Virginia. *Maestro*, *Frankenstein*, *The Power of the Dog*, and *Marriage Story* all played at the Paramount, but *La Bola Negra* is a must-see for anyone coming to the Virginia Film Festival.

To close out my Saturday night, you could go to a special event: a Q&A with David O. Russell for *American Hustle*, but I could watch that at home. You could see the Jury Prize winner and the Official Selection for Bulgaria from the Cannes Film Festival, *The Dreamed Adventurer*, or you could see Sophie Okonedo’s buzzed-about performance in *Clarissa* (So if you are a big Okonedo fan, you could see her twice, with *Mouse* earlier in the day).

But Tim Blake Nelson is coming to the Virginia Film Festival with a film he wrote, directed, produced, and acted in, starring Amanda Seyfried and Wumni Mossaku. Sign me up to watch *The Life in the Depths of Wilson Shed* and stay for a discussion with the Virtuoso Award winner Tim Blake Nelson at this year’s Virginia Film Festival.

And closing out the 39th annual Virginia Film Festival, we arrive at Sunday, October 25. If you want, you could fit four films in once again, but you have to make some difficult choices, just like on Saturday.

In the morning, you could see the Toronto International Film Festival runner-up *I Play Rocky* for the People’s Choice Award, but I think I’m gonna go with the Cannes Director winner and Poland’s official selection for the Academy Awards, Paweł Pawlikowski’s *Fatherland*, starring Sandra Hüller and Hans Zischler.

Pawlikowski makes breezy but philosophical films, and this one follows Nobel Prize-winning novelist Thomas Mann and his daughter as they road-trip through a divided Germany. So I start my day at the Culbreth Theater, then rush over to the Violet Crown to catch the Un Certain Regard and Special Jury Prize winner from the Cannes Film Festival and the Jury Award at Annecy, *Iron Boy*, a beautifully animated French film.

After *Iron Boy*, I sadly miss out on films such as *Once Upon a Time in Harlem*, which has been applauded for its archival footage of some of the Harlem Renaissance’s most iconic inspirational figures, Lea Seydoux’s Austrian film *Gentle Monster*; and the documentary *Rehearsals for Revolution*.

But then I had a very tough choice to make because who doesn’t love Nathan Lane, and who doesn’t love *The Birdcage*? As VAFF awards Lane the Visionary Award, he joins a Q&A and discussion celebrating the 30th anniversary of *The Birdcage*. Also, Ava DuVernay’s brand-new film *14th*, the follow-up to her Academy Award-nominated *13th* documentary, is screening at the Culbreth Theater.

So I think my day goes from Culbreth to Violet Crown, back to Culbreth, and then to close out my night. As I mentioned at the very beginning of this festival breakdown, I want to be in the room where it happens. If everyone’s partying on opening night, I want to be with everyone on closing night as we celebrate the festival's conclusion. This year also features a powerful and relevant documentary for the Charlottesville area, *One 1 15 41: Tragedy to Triumph*, focusing on the unthinkable tragedy that occurred on the UVA campus and for the University of Virginia football team in 2022.
