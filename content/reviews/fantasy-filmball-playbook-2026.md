Hey, what's up, hello everyone, and welcome to the OFFICIAL Fantasy Filmball Playbook for the 2026–2027 season. Whether you've been playing for years or are just dipping your toes in for the first time, this article will be your guide through the new season of the game.

With each year, we learn new things that help inform changes to the scoring system and make the game smoother for players. As with every season, there are some updates that we hope will provide a balanced and competitive experience, specifically aimed at combating the recent era of Best Picture sweepers.

But before we dive into the rule changes for the upcoming season, let's do a quick refresher on how the game works.

<h2>The History</h2>

Back in 2017, this game started as a dream of mine, as someone who loved the concept of fantasy sports but knew absolutely nothing about football or basketball. At the time, I was about to head to film school in Toronto, where I knew I would finally meet some kindred spirits who also obsessed over awards season. As a way to build a film community, I started working on my own take on fantasy sports for people whose Super Bowl was the Oscars.

In 2018, I finally put the game to the test, and with a league consisting of filmmakers Arnaud Weissenburger, Billy Bryk, John Magee, Taye Alvis, and myself, Fantasy Filmball was born.

In 2020, Arnaud and I moved the game online, beginning to build leagues beyond our own and grow the game within a broader online film community. We figured a game this fun deserved far better than being kept to ourselves. That year, we had only two leagues running. Last season, in 2025–2026, there were 21 leagues with more than 120 players across the globe, and the game continues to grow and evolve every year.

<h2>The Basics</h2>

At its most basic level, here is how the game works:

A league of six players each drafts a team consisting of 10 movies. Each movie can only be selected by one player, meaning there are 60 movies selected in the initial draft.

Movies collect points based on three factors:

<ul>
<li>Awards season nominations and wins</li>
<li>Critical reception</li>
<li>Box office performance</li>
</ul>

After the "championship" of the annual Academy Awards, one team is crowned the winner based on its overall performance.

Simple, right?

This is the simplest explanation of the game, and all you need to know before drafting your team is that you should aim to pick movies that audiences, critics, and the industry all love. The ideal Fantasy Filmball team is full of beloved movies you are excited to cheer for throughout awards season, all the way until the words, "And the Academy Award for Best Picture goes to..." are spoken.

<h2>The Points System</h2>

As mentioned above, points are gained through a combination of awards season, critical reception, and box office. Let's get a little more specific.

First off, awards season performance. This is the cornerstone of the game and where films will mostly earn their points. When we talk about awards season, we break this down into a few categories: awards shows (Oscars, BAFTA, Golden Globes, Critics Choice), industry guilds (PGA, DGA, SAG, WGA), film festival prizes (Sundance, Cannes, Venice, and TIFF), and critics circles (New York, LA, and the National Society of Film Critics). Any awards, big or small, fit into one of these four categories. All awards are sorted by their importance to the overall race, with more influential awards bodies earning more points, and less influential ones earning fewer. Likewise, different categories at each show are worth a variety of points, with each category being sorted based on its general value within the overall awards season landscape.

Next, the critic score. On each film's opening weekend, points are awarded based on the score achieved on Metacritic, which is our chosen critical review platform, due to its averaging of a collection of scores, a superior method to Rotten Tomatoes' binary system, which lacks nuance. Extra points are still awarded to films that are "certified fresh" by RT.

As mentioned, box office is scored lower, as this is simply not the main point of this game. Box office points are calculated through a formula judging a movie's domestic box office up against its reported budget. This means that mega-blockbusters must perform far outside their budget in order to make big points, while indie films can score well with far less.

Additional points can be gained from other sources as well. One of my favorite examples is that each player in a given league can provide numerical ratings (out of 10) to all films in the game, with each film gaining that amount of points, rewarding populist sensations with the opportunity to rack up to an additional 60 points per film. For every team that a player watches in its entirety, they will receive a small point boost, totaling 100 points if a player manages to view all 60 films selected in their league.

Let's look at last year's highest-scoring film, **One Battle After Another**. In our YouTube league, the film came in with a total of 5,041 points — the highest scorer in the entire history of the game. Not every film gets so lucky, but this is what it looks like to essentially max out a season haul.

<h2>Additional Gameplay</h2>

After the initial draft, there are plenty of ways to stay active in the game. We have bi-monthly waivers, during which players have the opportunity to drop one of their weaker films for another film that is currently unowned, or in "free agency." There is also the option to trade films with other players, keeping the game dynamic. A film's value in October may be extremely different from where it ends up in March.

We also run playoffs, where the 32 highest-scoring players after Golden Globe nominations are placed into a bracket. Fantasy Filmball also encourages predictions, chatting with fellow players about awards season, and, of course, watching the movies.

<h2>Rule Change: Victory Conditions</h2>

After the 2023 and 2025 seasons, both of which were dominated by overall awards season sweepers (**Oppenheimer** and **One Battle After Another**), some changes are in order. This year, our goal is to keep the game fun and competitive, even for teams that do not have the season-sweeping film. We want to reward overall team management skills, not just "who can get one big movie on their team?"

With this in mind, for the 2026–2027 season, the game winner will no longer be decided solely by overall points, but rather by a series of win conditions. This year, there are five categories that players will face off in, and the player who wins the most categories will be the game winner.

The categories that determine the game winner are:

<ul>
<li><strong>Highest Points</strong> — the team with the highest overall point total.</li>
<li><strong>Best Picture</strong> — the team with the Best Picture winner.</li>
<li><strong>Most Variety</strong> — the team with the most individual films to win Academy Awards.</li>
<li><strong>Bench Performance</strong> — the team with the highest average score among films not nominated for Best Picture.</li>
<li><strong>Most Undervalued Prospect</strong> — the team with the highest-scoring film drafted between rounds 6–10 (excluding waiver adds).</li>
</ul>

Whichever player wins the most of these five categories will be crowned league champion. In the event of a tie, the winner will be the player who holds the highest-ranked victory condition. To break down how this will work, here are the last three years of our YouTube league broken down into this format.

<h2>Rule Change: The Auction Waiver</h2>

This year, we are bringing back an auction format for waivers. In previous years, players had the opportunity each month to drop one film and pick up another. This year, to add some competition, we are introducing an auction element.

Here's how it will work:

Each player will have 300 points to spend for the season (equivalent to a film like 25th-ranked **Pillion** in the 2025–2026 season). This budget can be used during any waiver period and on as many different films as the player wishes until they run out of points.

The player currently ranked in last place will nominate a film for auction, naming their opening bid. The league will bid until a sale is reached. The player with the highest bid receives the film and must "drop" one of the 10 films currently on their roster. That film then becomes a free agent and is eligible for auction during the next waiver period if another player chooses to nominate it.

Not every player is required to nominate a film, with nominations moving from sixth place to first until each player decides there are no more films worth bidding on for that period. Any money left at the end of the auction carries over to the player's next auction period or is added to their yearly final point total, rewarding managers who knock out their initial drafts. Importantly, the only way to increase your waiver budget is through trades, so once your budget is spent, it's gone for good. Be wise!

Once every player has had the opportunity to nominate a film and bidding has been completed for all nominated films, waivers are complete until the next period.

Be smart with your waivers! Remember, you can win the game by having a well-balanced team. Try to drop your low scorers and pick up higher-placing films.

<h2>Rule Change: Prediction Points</h2>

Each league will decide which award shows they would like to predict. Players lock in their picks on Awards Expert, and the League Rep tallies the player with the most correct winner picks. First place is awarded 50 bonus points, second place receives 25 bonus points, and third place receives 10 bonus points.
