Cannes has been an increasingly strong indicator for a film's international success over the last decade, but not all films break out. These days, a film must play other notable festivals to generate further interest, especially within the North American film sphere.

This early in the season, we can't know for sure which films are favourites among festival programmers, critics, and audiences. The titles we'll be watching and discussing the most will become clearer once the Telluride, Toronto (TIFF), and New York (NYFF) lineups are announced. But as we wait for their release, let's speculate as to which films are likely to play at the fall festivals and grow in popularity.

By observing the last three years of Telluride, TIFF, and NYFF Cannes holdovers, we can notice predictive patterns within each festival's programming, which helps us guess which films will maintain their festival momentum. What's also important to keep in mind is the sheer number of films selected at each of these festivals every year — around 30–40 for Telluride, around 100 for NYFF, and nearly 300 for TIFF. This is why TIFF always has the most Cannes holdovers quantitatively.

<h3>Award Winners</h3>

Competition award winners are the absolute hottest commodity when it comes to festival programming. As has been the case most of the last few years, the Palme d'Or winner, **Fjord**, should easily find its place in all three festivals. Likewise with **Minotaur** and **Fatherland**, where Zvyagintsev and Pawlikowski have been routinely selected for their last films. Los Javis should easily have their **The Black Ball** show up at Telluride and TIFF, but the NYFF programmers will be a bit more reluctant to program it. It could go the way of *Emilia Pérez* and land in the Spotlight section.

After that, most of the other films will settle for one or two selections among the major fall fests, tops. With its few remaining spots, Telluride will likely bat for **Coward** along with TIFF. However, New York will remain allergic to Dhont's take on queer sentimentality. **The Dreamed Adventure** won the Jury prize, which will boost it to a Special Presentations slot at TIFF, along with a near-certain NYFF Main Slate. Telluride has not yet selected a Hamaguchi film; it will likely leave out **All of a Sudden** from its lineup, unlike the other two. Finally, **A Man of His Time** will be too much of an acquired taste for Telluride and TIFF, but it'll settle for a Main Slate spot instead.

<h3>Competition Leftovers</h3>

Telluride and NYFF love James Gray, so expect them to select **Paper Tiger**. Fellow New York filmmaker Ira Sachs should also be picked up by NYFF for **The Man I Love**, but maybe that will be it. Given the star power attached, it would be hard to imagine **The Beloved** missing TIFF — though that movie may not be for New York's taste. Almodóvar, however, is beloved by high-brow programmers, which will land his divisive **Bitter Christmas** in the NYFF Main Slate. Speaking of divisiveness, Neon will find a way to screen its Léa Seydoux-starring **The Unknown** at TIFF and New York to keep the conversations going. Seydoux's other Cannes film, **Gentle Monster**, is a prime TIFF Special Presentations, but maybe not much else. And finally, to account for TIFF's Centerpiece program, which has fairly deep pockets for Cannes holdovers, Kôji Fukada's **Nagi Notes** seems like a likely option.

Habitual fall-festival filmmakers like Asghar Farhadi and László Nemes may have to sit these festivals out, as their **Parallel Tales** and **Moulin** were rather poorly received by their standards. **The Birthday Party** will also be hard to program — a film that isn't memorable or unique enough to warrant a big festival presence.

The Un Certain Regard holdouts will give us a better idea of which films will be embraced worldwide, but the surest thing right now is Jordan Firstman's **Club Kid**. Not only is this likely to play Telluride (rare for Un Certain Regard), but it could run a serious bid for the TIFF People's Choice Award. New York will surely want its city's LGBTQ scene represented with the film and give it a prime Spotlight slot.

Otherwise, the award winners this year all look fairly appealing. Some could go unselected, but they each have value. The Camera d'Or winner for best first film will certainly make its way to TIFF, and maybe New York on its best day. Meanwhile, the top prize winner, **Everytime**, is certainly getting the TIFF-and-Main-Slate combo. Other prize winners — **Iron Boy**, **Elephants in the Fog**, and **Congo Boy** — all seem poised for the Centerpiece program at TIFF, while **Forever Your Maternal Animal** will hope for a NYFF selection. TIFF always goes for a few other titles from the program, and while there are many more to choose from, **The Meltdown** and **I'll Be Gone in June** seem likeliest. **All the Lovers in the Night**, **Strawberries**, and **Words of Love** could also surprise at TIFF.

*Already released: The Samurai and the Prisoner*

In previous years, holdovers from the other sections of the Official Selection are primarily composed of major auteurs and critic favourites (think **Misericordia**, **Magelan**, or **A Private Life**). This year, there really aren't any of these from the Cannes Premiere, Out of Competition, or Midnight programs — but the Director's Fortnight will fill that void later on.

Before we get there, though, the Special Screenings have a few highlights. The very well-reviewed Golden Eye winner for best documentary, **Rehearsals for a Revolution**, will almost certainly find its way to the TIFF Docs program like previous winners of that award. Meanwhile, **Tangles** and maybe **Lucy Lost** will go to the Centerpiece program, where most indie animation has landed at TIFF. The same way that Gael García Bernal's directorial debut was programmed at TIFF, we could see Diego Luna's film **Ashes** follow suit. Lastly, Clockwork's event restoration of Ken Russell's **The Devils** will likely find its way to TIFF Classics, if not a Gala Presentation à la **Sholay** last year.

This year, Director's Fortnight has many big names that programmers will be keen on courting. NYFF will almost certainly program Radu Jude's new film, **The Diary of a Chambermaid**, and Bruno Dumont's **Red Rocks**. They'll also bring along Alonso's **Double Freedom**, which will find its way to TIFF, albeit in the Wavelength program. Neon and the Esiri brothers' **Clarissa** will certainly be a hot title for TIFF and NYFF, while Clio Barnard's People's Choice Award-winning **I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning** will have a prime spot in TIFF's Special Presentations. Once more, the animated film **Viva Carmen** will be included in the Centerpiece program, while **We Are Aliens** will be left out. **Once Upon a Time in Harlem** will be too good to pass up for both TIFF Docs and the NYFF Main Slate.

For NYFF's Currents program, **La Perra** seems like the likeliest option — though it's a program that has gone without Cannes holdovers in the past, instead favouring Locarno, Berlin, or Rotterdam films.

Finally, it would be surprising to see **Atonement** go completely unselected. This is where Telluride could surprise and champion this hidden gem, and maybe one of the other two festivals as well. Other highlights from the Fortnight — like **Butterfly Jam**, **Gabin**, **Low Expectations**, and **Death Has No Master** — will struggle to find their places, even if the latter could sneak into TIFF as a Canadian co-production.
