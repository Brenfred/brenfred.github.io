When you talk about some of the most iconic filmmakers, one of the first names that comes to mind is the man, the myth, the legend, Quentin Tarantino. The master filmmaker has brought us iconic cinematic experiences like *Pulp Fiction*, *Inglourious Basterds*, *Django Unchained*, and what many consider his magnum opus, *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*.

*Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* was his ninth released film, coming out in 2019. And if you know anything about Mr. Tarantino, the man has a little bit of a reputation for being a smartass, someone stuck up, who thinks he is the greatest, and someone who sticks true to his words. When he announces he is only going to release 10 films, he means it. But with his "masterpiece" being movie number nine, he still had one to go. And over nearly the last decade, it seems that maybe Tarantino has gotten somewhat cold feet about what to make his 10th and final feature.

There have been many rumors, from a film starring Leonardo DiCaprio to a movie about a film critic, and eventually, a spinoff and continuation of *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*. Clearly, these characters mean a whole lot to Tarantino. Beyond releasing a nearly three-hour film, he has released comic books, novels, and penned multiple screenplays to expand this world. So inevitably, a film was gonna come out of that.

So now we have arrived with *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*. But Tarantino wants to be perfect, and this Cliff Booth spinoff could not be his 10th released movie. Even though he counts *Kill Bill* 1 and 2 as one film, why could he not do the same here for *Cliff Booth* and *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*? That's not important.

Instead of directing it himself, Tarantino wrote the screenplay and brought in another of the most iconic filmmakers of our generation for direction: David Fincher, the man behind movies like *Fight Club*, *Gone Girl*, *Se7en*, and *The Social Network*.

The stylistic difference between these two creatives is immediately apparent. Tarantino is known for his comedy, witty, fast-paced dialogue, and instantly iconic characters, while Fincher is known to be a meticulous director, from his staging and framing to his precise visual identity, oftentimes to a fault.

From that angle, this is a match made in heaven, and now here we are, fresh out of an IMAX screening for the upcoming Netflix release, so what are my thoughts?

Well, *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth* made me scooch my ass on command and lock in for this absolute heater. This collaboration absolutely rips. It's a perfect companion piece to *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*. If I loved *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* for its kooky characters, vibrant visuals, mile-a-minute screenplay, instantly quotable dialogue, and trademarked Tarantino spin on history, *Cliff Booth* is a wildly entertaining detour doing the opposite in all the best ways. 

It's much more methodical with its approach, oftentimes invoking the title of the movie. These feel like various mis-adventures of the titular character instead of one single narrative. Instead of a wide array of characters and a giant ensemble, we focus on Cliff Booth himself, reprised by Brad Pitt, who once again showcases his charm, perfect comedic timing, and action-hero bravado. This time he's not joined by the likes of Leonardo DiCaprio's Rick Dalton and Margot Robbie's Sharon Tate, although we get more than our fair share of updates on what they've done in the eight years since *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*, with enough fan service and Easter eggs to make any die-hard Tarantino fan jump for joy. Instead, Pitt is joined by standouts Elizabeth Debicki and Scott Caan, who perfectly complement the suave Cliff Booth.

Starting off with Caan, he plays an actor with "little man syndrome" who does his own stunts, has a little bit of an ego, and doesn't have the best reputation around town despite his immense popularity. If you put some pieces together, I'm sure you can guess who the joke is about. Caan's comedic timing and mannerisms make this joke consistently land in new and effective ways throughout the runtime.

Then Debicki continues to pop up in these action movies where she grounds the storyline in both a comedic and seductive nature. If you thought her role in *Tenet* was a heater, she takes that to another level and runs with it with her long strides here in *Cliff Booth*, truly making the most out of her screentime.

With *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*, we feel the brutality and violence. Tarantino is a filmmaker known to be very bloody, and Fincher is a filmmaker known to hold no punches back. Whether it's Tarantino's God-given talent for screenwriting, from the unhinged opening sequence involving trying to retrieve a stolen Oscar, to a deliciously absurd romantic back and forth, or what I am sure is going to a clip destined to break Film Twitter and start massive think pieces revolving around a conversation about what is considered art and what is considered just entertainment in the film industry. Mixing Tarantino's electric screenplay with Fincher's surgical directorial style, sleek visuals of 1970s Hollywood, tightly edited car chases, and immaculately staged action set pieces, you get both filmmakers bringing their respective strengths to the table. It's controlled chaos at its finest, with Tarantino's dialogue providing the spark and Fincher's direction making sure every explosion lands. We can go from laughing our ass off to watching the most despicable and in-your-face violence within seconds.

There are multiple unhinged sequences in the screenplay that just ooze the creativity of the mind that is Quentin Tarantino. Ranging from an unmistakably Tarantino takedown of method actors, a provocative dialogue about what two people are and the next steps of their connection, and, of course, a masterclass in character-driven comedy with Cliff Booth talking about his misadventures in South Asia back in the war.

On the flip side, bringing Fincher's expert eye behind the camera only elevates Tarantino's screenwriting. I particularly adore how every frame feels intentional with Fincher, especially in the movie within a movie and how they actually feel like a legitimate '60s and '70s flick instead of just a crappy spoof. From the film grain and dark, moody style of the blaxploitation flick *Soul Brothers Die Hard* to the insane oner and "Hollywood magic" on display with the Kingpin flick at the nightclub. Fincher also lends his trademark controlled chaos to the scenes involving the Verdusco brothers, from their bone-crunching introduction to their visceral car chase culminating in a masterclass in controlled mayhem for our final square-off. It's very reminiscent of the abundance of chaotic brutality at the ending of *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*, allowing Fincher to inject his trademark chilling restraint and timidness before exploding when it feels natural. Along with Tarantino's razor-sharp screenplay, emphasizing why *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth* is a full-throttle ride and why these two are such a dream pairing.

I find *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth* to be the perfect companion piece to *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*. Stripping back the layers and giving us a fun, vignette-filled, chaotic experience of a week in the life of a stuntman-turned-movie-theater-manager-turned-side-gig hustler, Cliff Booth. Seeing these extra layers expanded upon with more time spent with Cliff Booth showcases that Tarantino has so much more to offer the world of cinema. If he is true to sticking to his word about only putting out 10 films in his directorial filmography, I hope he continues to take roundabouts like *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*, where we get to see his once-in-a-generation style of storytelling and world-building expanded upon by some of the best stars and directors in the game.

It doesn't even need to be Fincher every time around. We got the very dark and gritty Cliff Booth spinoff. How about for the next one around, we bring in a great comedic director and get to see the Australian miniseries and potential mis-adventures that Rick Dalton is getting up to with Dingo Dan?
