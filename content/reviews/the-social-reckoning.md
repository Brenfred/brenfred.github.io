Aaron Sorkin, I am what you call a fan. *The Trial of the Chicago 7* came out during COVID, no problem, I went to see it at a drive-in. The box office was still recovering when you released a streamer movie with *Being the Ricardos*, and I went to a local indie cinema to see it opening weekend. So I don't say this lightly...

But unc, you are washed.

Go back to working with directors who know how to frame a movie.

*The Social Reckoning* is devoid of any juice or sauce. Sure, Aaron Sorkin writes to create friction, but when Mikey Madison is giving an awfully bad performance and Jeremy Strong just gives the same one-note Zuck for the entire runtime, what's the appeal?

I was in the camp for a spiritual successor to *The Social Network*. I didn't need a direct sequel. I didn't even need Jesse Eisenberg or David Fincher returning. I trusted Sorkin to at least write something interesting. But this movie just feels like an unc who is mad that Facebook fed him some fake news that he eventually got clowned for reposting to his feed.

This film could have had one percent of cinematic integrity and I would have been cool, but this is unlike *The Trial of the Chicago 7*, which really makes you feel like you're a part of the courtroom proceedings, unlike *Molly's Game*, which gets right the combination of Sorkin's trademark rapid-fire writing with the appeal and intensity of a poker match, and hell, even unlike *Being the Ricardos*, which at least has some good acting performances. *The Social Reckoning* doesn't have any of that.

If I am attending a press screening (not a screening that is press mixed with general audiences, but strictly writers and people from the media), tell me why people are laughing at nearly every line of dialogue coming from Strong. Tell me why multiple audience members fell asleep halfway through the runtime, and tell me why, when the movie ended, all I heard on my walk out talking with my girlfriend, before I even got a chance to interact with the rep, was various members saying how bad it was, how much they hated the movie, and how much this just did not work for nearly anyone in the room tonight.

There are things worth complimenting about *The Social Reckoning*, because this movie is not nearly as bad as I'm making it sound; I am ultimately just very disappointed and let down. I think this would work as a play. Sorkin does have some very fun and playful lines of dialogue that I think would lead to great monologues and back-and-forth on stage. The framing device of telling the story non-linearly, jumping back and forth in time between our two different sets of protagonists, is something that could gel more on stage.

But as a film, there's nothing overly gripping about it.

The jumping through time periods, how these characters rarely, if ever, cross paths... you're just watching two parallel, back-and-forth conversations occur. We see Zuck practice for his courtroom hearing, but we never get to see it play out. And then we see our whistleblower and our investigative journalist go back and forth over the course of a year and a half, where the same beats play out every time.

She's hesitant to share information. He's dumb and doesn't understand when she tries to leave him breadcrumbs. She says, "I'm gonna leave. This is all." He sits in silence. She then turns around and tells him one more thing. And then we repeat it in the very next scene.

At the end of the day, I do think *The Social Reckoning* has something to say. But it's not original, it's not unique, and it's not what we come to an Aaron Sorkin movie to see.

We want to watch his scripts play out because of the way that he brings nuance to the conversation and the way that he can rile up an audience. But *The Social Reckoning* doesn't even capture the feeling of the platform that is designed to rile up its user base.

Instead, like I said before, we just have a man past his prime regurgitating the same talking points you would see in an engagement-farming Facebook post. Even the film ends with a wall of text that feels rage-baiting in nature.

I'm gonna stop my ramble now. I'll end with this:

I was one of the few people in this space, or at least in my pocket of the online film discussion, who was genuinely excited for *The Social Reckoning*. I think Aaron Sorkin has shown himself to be a very talented filmmaker. He is one of the GOATs when it comes to screenwriting, love him or hate him. Jeremy Strong gives 110% to whatever role he is doing, and Jeremy Allen White and Mikey Madison are fun, up-and-coming talents.

But this is a total misfire from everyone involved.

I know *Digger* is the talk of the town at the moment in regards to awards-season flops and films completely missing expectations, but I think it will pale in comparison to what *The Social Reckoning* was designed to be on paper.
