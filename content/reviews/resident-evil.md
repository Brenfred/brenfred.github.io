To be entirely honest, I have no real knowledge of the *Resident Evil* franchise. I'm not a gamer in general, and while I've heard that the game is brimming with detailed lore and complex characters, I wouldn't know anything about any of them. I've also never seen a film adaptation of this series before, whether it be the long-running series from the 2000s or the reboot from 2018 that no one talks about. This film was my first engagement with the series at all, and thus I will be approaching this not as a series fan, but as a film fan. I didn't sign up to see how my favored PlayStation property was adapted, but to see how the filmmaker behind the delightful romp of *Barbarian* and the cultural success of *Weapons* would continue his trajectory.

As a fan of both of Cregger's prior works, I am happy to report that I found this to be his best. He finally commits here to going all-in on the madness, crafting a 90-minute adrenaline hit of a movie that hits the gas from minute one and never lets up. It's a breathtaking, endless setpiece of horror that instantly feels rewatchable, setting a new milestone for what we can do with a video game adaptation if we put it in the hands of a truly talented filmmaker.

Cregger makes this movie feel the same way it might feel when you get lost in a game and can't help but keep playing. The mechanics of gaming are lovingly recreated here, making this cinematic experience feel as if we ourselves are running through decisions the same way the protagonist is. Whenever I thought of what I would do if this was my player character and I was in this situation, Abrams would do that; and the consequences of actions always made perfect sense to me. This is a film that intuitively understands the idea of cause and effect; there's constant setup and payoff, and every scare, action, and failure feels earned by the narrative. This is not a dialogue-heavy film with a showy script, but the simple construction of this is an undeniably impressive feat of story structure that's deeply compelling.

Austin Abrams is a perfect choice for the lead role. I've continually held the opinion that he was the strongest performance in *Weapons*, and I can easily see why Cregger wanted him to lead this, as he perfectly meshes with the tone. A performance requiring a certain level of intellect as well as a strong sense of physicality, Abrams is able to make this character feel realistic both in his smarts and in his stupidity. This is a much funnier film than one might expect, with multiple laugh-out-loud gags, and it's hard to imagine them working nearly as well without the quavering voice and shuddering mannerisms of Abrams here. There isn't much of an ensemble here, but there isn't a need for one with a compelling protagonist like this.

If you're a horror nut looking to get your fix, I'm sure you'll be well sated here as well. Cregger continues to shine in his ability to imbue his films with a freakiness that unsettles, creating fear in the simplest of scenes. There are a couple of great jumpscares, some excellent moments of splatter, and an engaging blend of action and horror that keeps the thrills going. I really appreciated just how gnarly this was; it didn't feel quite like any other zombie or monster film I'd seen before, and originality in a video game adaptation is a pleasant sight. Special credit should be given to the excellent prosthetic and visual effects on display here, which do a stellar job creating texture and gross-out, horrific visuals.

Overall, this is an unequivocal blast. Some may critique it for being lighter on plot and characterization, but that simply isn't the type of film this wants to be. It sets out to be a roller coaster ride, and it is unequivocally that with laughs, scares, and fights galore. If you think you'll be into this, you absolutely will be!
