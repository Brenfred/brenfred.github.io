“What’s the worst thing you’ve ever done?” is the central question that Kristoffer Borgli’s brand-new film *The Drama* wants to explore. Ever since it was announced, we’ve all been wondering: what could Zendaya’s Emma have possibly done to get that kind of reaction out of her best friends? And trust me… you will never guess what the actual reveal is.

But what’s even more impressive than delivering a reveal that truly lives up to the hype is the scene in which it takes place. This four-person, storytime-style sequence around a table is masterfully edited. From the pitch-perfect comedic timing to the exact moments when Borgli cuts to a new angle to capture the facial reactions of Zendaya, Robert Pattinson, Alana Haim, and Mamoudou Athie reacting to each other’s most despicable actions… It's really great stuff.

Now to speak about *The Drama* as a whole, GO SEE THIS MOVIE IF YOU HAVEN’T ALREADY. *The Drama* is a perfect example of an experience that deserves to be headed into as blind as possible for the full effect, I find it to just be a really great time at the theaters. I understand that some people may be taken aback by certain directions and plot points the film wants to explore, but I think that’s exactly what makes Borgli such an interesting filmmaker. We saw with his previous effort, *Dream Scenario*, starring Nicolas Cage, the things that character says and has to go through during the runtime can push audiences to their limits. Pattinson and Zendaya are given those same opportunities here and strive.

And speaking of those two… you’d better enjoy them, because you’re going to see a lot of them together on the big screen multiple times soon, between this, *The Odyssey*, and *Dune: Part Three*. I have to say, I can't be more excited because these two are a match made in heaven. The way they play off each other is so charismatic, you buy their steaming chemistry at the beginning, you buy their highs and lows as a couple, you even buy their disgust at certain reveals. These are two fantastic actors truly showcasing the best of their abilities in tandem.

*The Drama* works best when it is pushed to its narrative and visual boundaries. I especially loved the dreamlike sequences, they’re so vivid, giving us insight into what these characters imagine about each other in worst-case, nightmare scenarios we’ve all experienced. Whether it’s wondering what the person on the other end of your phone is thinking while you wait for a reply, or imagining what your significant other might be doing while they’re away… I found these moments incredibly relatable. The sound design elevates this even further. I will resist going into further detail to avoid spoilers, but it is an aspect of the film I genuinely found myself blown away by.

This will be a stressful watch for some, a hilarious ride for others, and the kind of movie where your one snob friend will call overrated. But for me, this is exactly the kind of movie I love, watching standout performers absolutely chew up the scenery. Pattinson continues to prove why he’s one of the best working actors today, and Zendaya shows us a whole new side of her range. We’ve seen her play stoic, funny, and lovable… and here, she does all of that in one film.

And looking at the supporting cast, Haim does a fantastic job playing an unlikeable character, handling some very difficult material with confidence. Athie as always is also a welcome presence, bringing the same refreshing screen energy he’s shown in films like Kinds of Kindness and Elemental.

*The Drama* is a mega recommendation from me, please go see it as soon as possible so you don’t get spoiled and experience one of the year's best films on the big screen with a packed audience.
