Jaafar Jackson is Michael.

Jaafar’s very first scene … he turns away from the window, faces the camera, delivers his first line… and the entire theater at the Las Vegas IMAX audibly gasps at how perfectly he captures his uncle’s essence. What you’re seeing feels like a hologram of the King of Pop. Jaafar embodies his uncle like no other, from the iconic voice, to the generation-defining dance moves, to the demeanor of the biggest pop star ever. Considering this is Jaafar’s first-ever acting credit, he blew my expectations off the wall. He pulls off one of the best lead performances in a music biopic of all time.

Speaking of performances, can we take a moment to appreciate Juliano Krue Valdi for being an absolute ray of light as young MJ? Just like Jaafar, Valdi has the vocal chops, the electric dance moves, and commands the screen at just nine years old during filming. I’ve seen a lot of hate thrown at Colman Domingo online for his take on Joe Jackson, some even clamoring for a Razzie for the two-time Oscar nominee, but… have you seen the real Joe Jackson? This is a role that demands someone to ham it up, someone over-the-top, someone almost immediately unsettling, and Domingo leans all the way in. These three really elevate what could easily become one of the highest-grossing biopics of all time.

Another chart-topping aspect of *Michael* is its pace. This movie moonwalks by… it’s moving at 100 mph, which honestly feels necessary for this kind of biopic. Sure, I can admit the story structure is checking the ABCs of the genre: youth, rise to fame, personal struggles, then success. But for me, what really works is how *Michael* frames his journey, breaking free from his father’s shackles and becoming his own man. That’s what *Michael* is all about: someone learning to stand up for themselves, redefining who they are, and doing it in a glowing fashion. As an audience, we just want to enjoy ourselves, and at my sold-out screening, the theater was rockin’ like Robin. People were literally shaking their bodies down to the ground during the credits and couldn’t stop loving what *Michael* was giving. Audiences want the recreations, the smash hits, the life-like performances, and *Michael* absolutely delivers.

*Michael* tells the rise to fame of the world’s biggest pop star, Michael Jackson. From his early days performing with his brothers in the Jackson 5, managed by his father and pushed to his limits, all the way to becoming the best performer of all time. The film spans the first half of his thrilling career, including his solo breakout *Off the Wall*, the instantly genre-defining *Thriller*, and concludes after Jackson cements himself as a solo artist, moving away from the Jackson clan with the release of *Bad*. Knowing the absolute production hell *Michael* went through on its journey to the big screen, constant delays, massive reshoots, and the challenge of packing in one of the most prolific public personas of the modern era, the creative team had a tall task. The fact that *Michael* is this enjoyable is a testament to the team for banding together and cooking up something this electric. Watching it with a crowd of superfans dressed head-to-toe in merch and costumes truly rocked my world.

If I had to dish out some criticism… because let’s be honest, some critics are calling this movie *bad*… my biggest issue comes down to the direction. I don’t have a major problem with Antoine Fuqua as a creative. When he was announced, a lot of people were let down because of his track record… but he’s got some heaters. Obviously, *Training Day* helped propel Denzel Washington to his first Best Actor Oscar, and Fuqua has made some solid action flicks. But has he ever brought a real sense of style behind the lens? Not really… and that’s where *Michael* falls a bit flat.

While I raved about how James Mangold captured the essence of live performance in *A Complete Unknown*, or how Baz Luhrmann brought that bombastic flair that perfectly matched *Elvis*, Fuqua doesn’t quite bring a unique perspective to the biopic genre. The concert scenes are fun because the music is timeless and Jaafar is killing it, but the camera itself doesn’t feel like a character. It just kind of… does random stuff. If we’re watching “Billie Jean,” let me watch Jaafar perform “Billie Jean.” I don’t need constant cutaways to the crowd or extra glitz behind the lens.

I missed a few press screenings and the opportunity to see *Michael* early, which was pretty upsetting because I try to avoid reviews before indulging in the medium. I don’t want my opinion shaped before I walk into a showing, I want my reaction to be raw and honest. But that’s not how the cards were dealt. The embargo lifts and… come on, we cannot be serious with some of these scores. A 39 on Metacritic? Some of you just need to “Beat It*”*. *Michael* is a blast. I found it to be so fun, because of an incredible lead performance by Jaafar, head-bopping bangers from MJ’s catalog, and genuinely impressive craftsmanship on all fronts. The recreation of iconic Michael Jackson costumes, the makeup and prosthetics done to create Michael’s various nose jobs, the period hairstyles from the entire ensemble, and especially the recreated stages, it’s all really strong work. At times, you forget you’re watching a Michael Jackson biopic and just believe you’re just watching clips of Michael Jackson. 

That’s a major reason why I personally enjoyed *Michael* so much, and why I do think there’s such a disconnect between general audiences and highbrow critics. The critics *wanted* one film, but *Michael* delivered what it knows the audience desires. It gives them what they want, and it does it well. Whether it’s Jaafar basically reviving his uncle, the technical elements transporting you back, or the film moving at a smooth, digestible pace… while yes, I can agree with some of the criticism and say I would’ve liked it to go deeper, I’m not going to ding *Michael* for what it’s not trying to do. I’m going to praise it for what it *does* accomplish.

For example, you can go into a *Fast & Furious* movie wanting precisely edited car chases, one-of-a-kind stunts, and a story with deeper meaning, but the Fast family aren’t trying to make a Tom Cruise-level spectacle. They’re giving their core audience what they expect going in. Now, it’s a whole other conversation whether they actually accomplish those goals, I mean, that’s why *Fast X Part II* hasn’t been greenlit, but I digress. I genuinely don’t understand how someone lands on a score that low without coming in with some kind of bias. Whether it’s wanting a film diving into Michael Jackson’s legal controversies (which they tried but legally couldn’t do), or just general fatigue with the music biopic genre. Because when these biopics work, the intended demographic really shows out. *Bohemian Rhapsody* grossed $910 million worldwide, *Elvis* became one of the biggest films of 2022 during a recovering box office, and more recently, *A Complete Unknown* had one of the best leg multipliers of 2024, posting a 6.4x return from its opening weekend. These films are fun, they connect emotionally by letting their icons be rejuvenated onscreen, and they hit audiences with exactly what they came for.

To make a quick parallel, a colleague and I recently compared *Michael* to a film both audiences and critics agree on: *Mad Max: Fury Road*. *Fury Road* is driven by immersive visuals, nonstop action, and generational craftsmanship. The story is minimal, yet it works so well everywhere else that we don’t care. So I don’t see how *Michael*, which arguably has a stronger narrative, more memorable performances, and brings these iconic hits back to life, is being treated like some cinematic abomination by critics at large.

To conclude, *Michael* is one of 2026’s best so far, backed by a truly incredible lead performance from Jaafar Jackson. Even those who have major issues with the film can agree that Jaafar is a one-of-a-kind talent in this production. *Michael does* a wonderful job of making you feel like you’re part of the moment, it’s the definition of a crowd-pleaser if you allow yourself to get sucked in. But sadly, some out there just don’t want this movie to succeed and that’s a pretty weak way to approach any film, in my opinion. Go see *Michael* with an open mind. Go in ready to feel something, and let *Michael* heal your world.
