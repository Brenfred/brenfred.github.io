“Because he’s bad!”

Travis Knight is a fascinating figure within the modern Hollywood landscape. A nepo baby whose father co-founded Nike, Knight went from being a terrible rapper to an executive and, eventually, the CEO of Laika, one of the best stop-motion studios in the business. Before he made his directorial debut with *Kubo and the Two Strings*, there was absolutely no indication that he’d have any real voice or ability to bring characters to life, and some might’ve feared that he would bring his corporate mind to make films as milquetoast as possible. Instead, he directed a heartfelt, emotional masterwork, and followed it up with *Bumblebee*, by far the most resonant and thematically interesting work of the *Transformers* franchise. I thought I couldn’t wait to see what story this man wanted to tell next.

I was wrong. If I knew what drivel was in store for me, I would’ve saved myself a few dollars and skipped the theater experience for this one.

The most frustrating element of viewing *Masters of the Universe* is that, when you really squint, you can almost see hints of a better movie peppered throughout. There are moments of standout quality that tempt you into thinking this is an experience worth having, that Knight has something to say. Whether it be a comedic bit that actually lands, a sonic motif that actually reverberates in the mind as more than just temp music, or visual effects that create a real sense of splendor for the world of Eternia, there are flashes of brilliance here that make this more than mindless noise. In my opinion, these are the worst parts of the movie, because they give you hope. Hope that will soon be crushed by the overwhelming weight of monotony.

The story of this film is painfully nonsensical, as the four screenwriters bumble through a tale rife with more half-started themes than you can shake a stick at. Is this about coming of age? The wonders of imagination? True power being within? Family? Communication? This movie careens wildly from one moral to the next every few minutes, rarely stopping to give the audience a moment to breathe. It honestly felt more like sitting in a kindergarten classroom being told virtues rather than actually learning anything about these paper-thin characters. 

Our cast, unfortunately, cannot save the work either. Nicholas Galitzine falls victim to overacting, trying so hard to sell his character that you can’t help but laugh at the movie, rather than with it. Jared Leto has a real knack for bringing the wrong energy to every scene he’s in, whether it's attempting to lighten up a moment of gravitas or being deadly serious in one of the goofier bits of the film, it never feels like Leto knows what he’s supposed to be doing. Wonderful performers like Idris Elba and Alison Brie are clearly phoning it in, and Camila Mendes “steals” the show with what I can honestly say is one of the worst, most lifeless, soul-deadening performances I’ve seen in a long time.

The tonal mismatch of *Masters of the Universe* makes me wonder if Travis Knight just wasn’t sure what to bring to this project. Some scenes attempt to deliver powerful emotional depth to a level that felt remarkably distracting, as if the film wanted us to move past the absurdity of this world. Others try to lampshade the issues inherent here, with jokes that land with a wet thud or actors practically winking at the camera, attempting to assure me that they too were in on the joke. I fear the joke is on them with this one, as whether it was an incoherently shot action sequence, a cringeworthy metareference to He-Man, or an attempt to rouse the viewer with an “epic” moment, there was one emotion I consistently felt throughout the entire runtime: boredom.

I will admit that I am not a He-Man fan in general. I’ve watched the 1980s film (which seemed much more aware of its campiness), but otherwise have rarely engaged with it beyond its status in meme culture. Nevertheless, this was a truly horrendous pitch to better appreciate the IP.

P.S. Daniel Pemberton and Brian May, I adore you both, but if I heard the same guitar sounds one more time I was going to walk straight out of the theater.
