In November of 2023, Warner Brothers made an executive decision to shelve *Coyote vs. Acme* for a tax write-off. In the shadow of Warner Brothers canceling *Batgirl* some time ago for similar reasons, the decision received some notable public backlash at the time. The decision hurts even more when taken into account that the film has been in development since 2015, when it was first pitched by James Gunn. But although Warner Brothers wanted little to do with the film, they gave the filmmakers a chance to shop the film around to different studios, and miraculously, the film was saved.

Ketchup Entertainment had previously handled distribution for *The Day the Earth Blew Up*. While it wasn't a success commercially, it got some eyes on the company. Given *The Day the Earth Blew Up* was a Looney Tunes film, Ketchup was a fitting distributor for *Coyote vs. Acme*. With the question of distribution finally settled, it became a waiting game to see whether the film that was saved was actually good. Expectations were a bit all over the place. While a few people had high hopes, *Coyote vs. Acme* soars higher than these hopes or Acme's products ever did.

Put simply, the movie is an unsubtle look at how modern corporations will abuse and con people to get their way. *Coyote vs. Acme* evokes the rage seen in movies like *Don't Look Up* and *One Battle After Another*, but does so in a much more optimistic way fitting for its younger demographic. Leading the film is a fantastic if imperfect performance by Will Forte, starring alongside the titular mute Coyote. Forte's relationship with Wile E. Coyote in the film is perhaps the single most important thing to get right. The movie hinges on the growth both characters go through within the film due to each other, and it ends up nothing less than perfect. It isn't even this one relationship, but so much of the cast and characters together excel just as much. Between Forte's constant bouts with John Cena, the relationship between every character that comes out of Eric Bauza's mouth, and one heartbreaking yet beautiful combination that I dare not spoil, the film has a phenomenal cast with equally phenomenal chemistry all around.

Evidently, a major player in making these parts of the film work so well is the ingenious script by Samy Burch, the Oscar-nominated writer of *May December*. Yes, I am as shocked as you are. Burch's script for *May December* was heavily praised, and even if she won't be recognized for her work here, she still delivers a truly incredible script. Maybe the script's biggest feat is its perfect mastery of tone. Rarely has a movie so skillfully swapped from comedy to heartbreak to pure unfiltered joy without feeling off-putting. Every scene, no matter how over the top or stupid, feels earnest in what it tries and succeeds in doing. It's not necessarily perfect, particularly the moments where it practically explains everything instead of letting the audience draw an evident conclusion. However, the script still works with so much heart and comedy that it just ends up being a delight either way.

The film's production quality is mostly stellar. Some bland set design sticks out as a bit of an issue, but there ends up being great editing, cinematography, and visual effects throughout the runtime. The visual effects in particular are fantastic, merging the real-life aspects with the cartoon world while not making either feel too out of place. It would have been so easy to make the decision of a hyper-realistic Road Runner or mostly cartoony humans, but they feel free to express both sides while keeping the cartoony identity of all of the famed characters.

Somewhat doubling back to the script, the final thing to highlight is the shocking thematic depth in the film. The immediate observation is the critique of companies abusing consumers for monetary gain, and the obviousness of that is heavily important for its audience. Movies like *Don't Look Up* are also highly unsubtle with their critiques, but for different reasons. While *Don't Look Up* screams its message at a world way past the line of no return, *Coyote vs. Acme*'s bluntness is an attempt to keep those growing away from that line. Given it is a Looney Tunes film, having a theme that sticks out clearly to kids is pretty important, and it does, but you also need to give it depth. Luckily, *Coyote vs. Acme* has no shortage of that. *Coyote vs. Acme* manages to balance themes of prejudice, revisionism, loneliness, perseverance, and frankly far more to mention without any of them feeling underdeveloped, forced, or overused. It all feels genuine. It's perhaps the most realistic movie about a cartoon Coyote suing a corporation because he can't catch a bird.

No movie is perfect, and *Coyote vs. Acme* is no exception. At times it can lean into being a kids film too much. It employs a few overused cliches that don't do much to elevate the plot. But when all is said and done, we still have an incredible movie. The story of some of the hardest-working idiots in the world facing off against a corporation, if not the whole system, is a joy to watch through and through. Whether the bigger miracle is the quality of the film or the fact it was released at all, there's little not to appreciate about *Coyote vs. Acme*.
