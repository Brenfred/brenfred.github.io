A major part of award season is digging through and analyzing the films premiering at major film festivals around the world. Sundance gives us under-the-radar indies, Toronto features studio crowd-pleasers, and Cannes is home to a slate of international auteur films. However, animated films consistently struggle to find the right platforms and visibility within the current festival framework. For the Annecy Film Festival, the world's leading animation festival, the goal is to platform anything from stop-motion and hand-drawn to 3D animation.

This year, the Annecy Film Festival runs for a week, from June 21st through June 27th, presenting a wide variety of shorts, features, television, commissioned, and student films. It's a gold mine for animation lovers and an essential pitstop for the industry. This year, many exciting films are playing, including high-profile early presentations of unfinished works like Laika Studios' *Wildwood*, Brad Bird's Netflix film, *Ray Gun*, and Disney's new 3D film, *Hexed*. In that way, the festival operates as a mini animation-focused CinemaCon.

Annecy is undoubtedly a haven for animated films, helping launch great festival and commercial runs, but it's also an underappreciated awards-season indicator. Here's what you need to know:

Like most European film festivals, Annecy has a "Competition" lineup. This year's lineup includes 11 films (one more than last year!), four of which are world premieres. Annecy has many more programs, like the "Contrechamps" competition reserved for more underground, low-budget films, and an Out-of-Competition section, which mostly includes studio films. Although this year's festival opens with the Out-of-Competition world premiere of *Minions and Monsters*, you won't see premieres of forthcoming major studio animated films. There is no *Forgotten Island* or *Shaun the Sheep*, and, as previously mentioned, films like *Wildwood* opt for a commercial presentation instead. These films are absent for the same complicated reasons that Christopher Nolan, Greta Gerwig, and Paul Thomas Anderson avoid film festivals altogether.

Despite this, Annecy's "Competition" program is always one to behold given that it would be hard to find a stronger and more varied crop of a given year's auteur and indie animated films. Annecy has thus become a beacon for great animated films that simply need more visibility, many of which wind up in the Animated Film race come awards season. Just last year, 2 of the 5 films nominated for Best Animated Feature at the Oscars were indies that stopped by Annecy (*Arco* and *Little Amelie and the Character of Rain*). With the recent Oscar victory of *Flow*, a historic win for indie animation, Annecy may have become more of a breeding ground for Animated Film contenders than ever before. The question is, how can we know which films from the "Competition" will go on to find love in awards season?

For our purposes, there are 3 significant prizes awarded to "Competition" films: the Audience Award (voted on by the public), the Jury Award (effectively a 2nd-place prize), and the Crystal (the top prize).

Looking back at the last 11 years of the festival, we see that recent winners of these categories have gone on to be nominated for the Academy Awards. The aforementioned Oscar-nominated *Little Amelie* won the Audience Prize, *Flow* won the Jury Award, and *Memoir of a Snail* won the Crystal. In this selection, we see that the Audience Prize (which did not run from 2020 to 2022 due to COVID) holds a fantastic 6/8 track record, while the Crystal is still solid but a bit more inconsistent at 6/11.

The Audience Award is the most reliable indicator. Even in a year like 2017, where the jury went in an unusual direction, the audience stayed in line and went with the impressive and accessible *Loving Vincent*, which, of course, went on to be Oscar-nominated.

Some years just don't yield contenders. It happens. In 2022, for example, there weren't any films that fit the bill as a strong awards-season contender. As good as *Little Nicholas* is, it wasn't acquired by a significant American distributor and was likely too local as an adaptation of French children's literature.

It helps to have a complete picture of the festival. When *Flow* failed to win the top prize, it ran against one of the most celebrated veteran animators, Adam Elliot, with his world premiere of *Memoir of a Snail*. In 2023, no film from the "Competition" seemed like a likely Oscar contender, but, inexplicably, *Robot Dreams* was programmed in the "Contrechamps" program, where it won the top prize. That was the animated player to watch out for.

The last 2 years have exceptionally yielded TWO nominees. For all we know, this could be the luck of the draw, but perhaps we can expect this to continue. Maybe not every year, but certainly in some future years.

Something I also want to draw attention to is that every film that has won both the Crystal and the Audience Prize has been nominated for the Animated Film Oscar. The Jury Award and Audience Award combo is strong too (*Flow* and *The Breadwinner* proving themselves worthy), but that track record isn't flawless.

Another important aspect to note is where the best films come from. While Annecy is a great platform, it is rarely the festival to world premiere the most successful titles. Most of the great Annecy films actually premiere just a month earlier at the Cannes Film Festival: 6 out of 11 eventual Oscar nominees within the previous selection (7 if you count *Flee*'s unique COVID-impacted case).

So, with all this information in mind, what can we expect from this year?

The 4 world premieres could certainly surprise us, but this year, it seems like the likeliest contenders are the films that have already premiered. Among these is the Spanish surrealist comedy *Decorado*, which looks like a blast, albeit not an awards contender. A few Cannes titles that are worth a watch but likely won't make much noise would be the family-oriented *Lucy Lost* and the Japanese bullying drama *We Are Aliens*. Sébastien Laudenbach won the Crystal in 2023 for *Chicken for Linda!*, but competition will likely prevent his fantastic *Viva Carmen* from going the distance.

That leaves us with Netflix's Cannes Critics' Week pickup *In Waves*, the Seth Rogen-produced Alzheimer's tear-jerker *Tangles*, and the Cannes Un Certain Regard Special Jury Prize winner *Iron Boy*. Despite Netflix's backing and a striking visual style, I would dismiss *In Waves* as nothing more than a mirage. Reviews have been uneven, and interest seems to have dissipated since Cannes ended. While this year seems very strong in the Animation category, there may be a way for both of the last two films to find a spot among the 5 nominees. Both have received very warm receptions at their world premieres, and interest is high. Sony Pictures Classics also picked up *Iron Boy* right after its premiere, demonstrating enthusiasm.

At this point, it seems likely that the Crystal and/or the Audience Prize will be given to, or be split between, these two. Something like *Iron Boy* wins the Crystal, *Viva Carmen* wins the Jury Award, and *Tangles* wins the Audience Award feels right, but we'll see the results very soon and analyze accordingly.

Either way, Annecy is the definitive cradle for indie animation and future Oscar nominees. When the awards are announced, it would be best not to glance over them. This can be pretty valuable information in a niche category that isn't always easy to read.
