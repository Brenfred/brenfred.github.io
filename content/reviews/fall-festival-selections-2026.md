Fall festival season is fast approaching, and the hundreds of films selected are finally coming into view. With Venice, indications of the Telluride lineup, Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF), and New York Film Festival (NYFF) programs revealed, let's dive into which films most and least benefit from their selections.

Quick disclaimer: as of writing, we do not have the lineups for the BFI London Film Festival nor the AFI Film Festivals, which can help further indicate which films are popular to fall festival programmers. We also do not have the Telluride lineup, which always releases at the start of the festival. That being said, it's common practice to infer the presence of several films selected thanks to the premiere status at other festivals.

<h2>Winners</h2>

<h3>Bucking Fastard</h3>

Surprisingly enough, the only film to be doing all four major fall festivals this year is Werner Herzog's *Bucking Fastard*. After opting out of Cannes (reportedly because the film wasn't offered a Competition slot), the film is making every major stop, starting with the Venice Competition program. The only slight disappointment for the yet-to-be-distributed film might be that it was slotted in the less prestigious Spotlight program at NYFF, but that feels like nitpicking.

<h3>The big three: Ink / Wild Horse Nine</h3>

Two additional films are doing the big three (meaning all but NYFF). This is another great sign of quality and confidence for Netflix (*Ink*) and Searchlight (*Wild Horse Nine*). Both films are starting their journeys in the Venice Competition, hoping to win a prize, and perhaps placing at TIFF for the People's Choice Award (PCA). This is especially promising for the Martin McDonagh film as the filmmaker's last two films haven't been selected by Telluride.

<h3>Telluride / TIFF / NYFF: A Long Winter / Tender Loving Care</h3>

Not the big three per se, but still a great bunch for these fall world premieres. Playing these three festivals and being selected at the NYFF Main Slate shows promise for these art-house British titles, although don't necessarily expect this popularity to translate to awards success.

<h3>Minotaur and… Cannes losers? The Unknown / The Man I Love</h3>

Three Cannes films are doing Telluride, TIFF, and NYFF Main Slate this year. *Minotaur* was an expected choice being the Grand Prix winner and a critic favourite, but *The Unknown* and *The Man I Love* really surprised in their festival presence. It goes to show that winning a prize at Cannes isn't a be-all end-all and that festival programmers really want to champion these more subdued and divisive films. *All of a Sudden* and *The Dreamed Adventure* also had a good showing with TIFF and NYFF selections, given that both filmmakers have never gone to Telluride.

<h3>Club Kid</h3>

After its incredible reception in the Un Certain Regard (UCR) section at Cannes, *Club Kid* all but confirmed that it's a festival programmer favourite. It's the only film from UCR to make it to the high-profile TIFF Special Presentations program (which bodes well for its chances for the PCA), and it managed to snag an NYFF Spotlight slot despite not being a highbrow favourite.

<h3>Iron Boy… and (most) indie animation</h3>

What a showing for *Iron Boy*. What is quickly becoming the clear favourite indie animated film of the year popped up as expected in the TIFF Centrepiece program, but also at NYFF. This festival routinely snubs non-Eastern animated films, making the selection all the more impressive.

TIFF went all in on animated films this year too, with Cannes titles *In Waves*, *Tangles*, and *Viva Carmen* popping up (while *Lucy Lost* was snubbed). Same goes with Annecy world premieres with *Muyi*, *Julián*, *Rogue Trooper*, and Crystal winner *The Violinist*. Clearly the success of *Flow* has pushed festivals to strongly consider programming animated films.

<h3>The Only Living Pickpocket in New York</h3>

After premiering at Sundance and playing Berlin, the Sony Pictures Classics film is making the most stops of any Sundance narrative film this year. Telluride is a mystery here, but playing both TIFF and NYFF (the only Sundance narrative film selected there) is a great sign of admiration for the John Turturro starrer.

<h3>Rehearsals for a Revolution</h3>

It's rare to see documentaries play outside of the TIFF Docs program unless they're about a major musician or celebrity. *Rehearsals for a Revolution*, the Golden Eye winner at Cannes, did just that. The in-part archival film is programmed in the Centrepiece section, giving it a stronger platform and making it stand out among the other films in the program.

<h3>1-2 Special: Everytime, La Gradiva, and A Man of His Time</h3>

The young boutique distributor 1-2 Special made very inspired purchases out of Cannes, and they seem to have paid off. *Everytime* and *La Gradiva* have secured TIFF and NYFF Main Slate slots, and *A Man of His Time* snagged a far-from-guaranteed Main Slate slot. All three films are from first- or second-time filmmakers who are (hopefully) about to break out.

<h3>Possible Love</h3>

The new Lee Chang-dong film is doing all the expected stops except Telluride. What really shows promise here is that it is the only Venice Competition film to be slotted in the NYFF Main Slate, which indicates quality, but also bodes well for its chances to win the Golden Lion.

<h3>Misty Green</h3>

*Misty Green* is the ONLY narrative TIFF world premiere that made it into the NYFF Main Slate. If that isn't promising, I don't know what is. Expect this film to show up at as many stops as it can further down the line.

<h3>Hope / Godzilla Minus Zero</h3>

Two of the most surprising festival programming decisions were for big East Asian blockbuster event films that were assumed to be skipping fall festivals. *Hope* landed a Special Event slot THE DAY BEFORE TIFF STARTS, which would've been the cutoff given the film releases the same day. *Godzilla Minus Zero* opening and world-premiering in the NYFF Spotlight program is a huge show of support for the sequel. Both of these films being programmed, and on top of that with a special distinction, is quite generous.

<h2>Jury's Still Out</h2>

<h3>Fjord / La Bola Negra</h3>

Arguably the two stand-out titles at Cannes this year fell a bit short of what we expected of them. *Fjord* is making every stop, but is the first Palme d'Or winner to miss the NYFF Main Slate selection since 2015, settling for the Spotlight section instead. On the other hand, *La Bola Negra* is doing Telluride and TIFF, but failed to make it to NYFF. We knew it wouldn't be a favourite of the programming team there, but we could've expected it to settle for a Spotlight slot the way Netflix's *Emilia Pérez* did two years ago.

<h3>Sandra Hüller: Fatherland and Rose</h3>

Is it strange that both Sandra Hüller films are skipping TIFF this year? Yes, absolutely. However, both *Fatherland* and *Rose* are making stops at NYFF Main Slate, and perhaps more interestingly, at Telluride. A puzzling no-show at TIFF but both films at Telluride? Could this be an arrangement for Hüller to receive the Telluride Silver Medallion for performance? That could explain the exclusion.

<h3>Once Upon a Time in Harlem</h3>

A great showing for this Sundance breakout (with Telluride still possible), but its inclusion in the TIFF Wavelength program (reserved for experimental and avant-garde works) is incredibly confusing. Perhaps the film is viewed as less accessible than past archive docs like *Summer of Soul* or *Moonage Daydream*.

<h3>NYFF Opening, Centerpiece and Closing: Paper Tiger / Behemoth! / 14th</h3>

The three premiere slots at NYFF are prestigious, so it should be expected that the selected films don't play many other fall festivals, but it may be too little when it comes to *Paper Tiger*, *Behemoth!*, and *14th*. Telluride loves James Gray, but the film won't make a stop there, and *Behemoth!* will have to wait until early October to have its world premiere. We'll have to keep an eye on these for sure.

<h3>Primetime and Josephine</h3>

Both *Primetime* and *Josephine* are playing only one fall festival, being Venice and TIFF respectively. That is unless they also find their way to Telluride. That seems unlikely for *Primetime*, which premieres during Telluride on Sept 5th, but maybe *Josephine* can make an extra stop there. If either film can pull it off, it would boost their festival trajectory from good to great.

<h3>Sundance docs: Nuisance Bear / One in a Million / Closure</h3>

None of the major competitive doc standouts from Sundance are making a stop at the fall festivals this year. While this has been a common occurrence over the past years, it would've been nice to see even just one of them pop up.

<h2>Losers</h2>

<h3>Klara and the Sun</h3>

TIFF favourite Taika Waititi is seemingly not having the comeback from *Next Goal Wins* he was hoping for, as his new film *Klara and the Sun* is missing from the lineup. Unless this is a deliberate decision from Sony/Columbia, it doesn't smell very good for this new project.

<h3>Cannes UCR titles left to the wayside: Forever Your Maternal Animal / The Meltdown / Victorian Psycho / I'll Be Gone in June</h3>

Many UCR titles were given new life, especially through the TIFF Centrepiece program (*Congo Boy*, *Ben'Imana*, and *Elephants in the Fog* to name a few), but many were left on the chopping block. Triple Actress winner *Forever Your Maternal Animal* couldn't find a home, *The Meltdown* had no champions, *Victorian Psycho* couldn't recover from searing reviews, and *I'll Be Gone in June* will have to look elsewhere for another platform. Those were the most notable omissions, but of course, there were a sizable number of Cannes films that haven't been selected anywhere else yet, as is the case every year. Difficult choices.

<h3>Coward</h3>

*Coward* did make it to TIFF… but that's it. Dhont has some trouble finding passion among the major fall festivals, this time unable to return to Telluride where *Close* played. Among the Cannes Competition prize-winners, it has the least desirable festival trajectory at the moment.

<h3>Sense and Sensibility / Wildwood</h3>

The October-dated films *Sense and Sensibility* and *Wildwood* were completely absent from the fall festivals. Perhaps these films don't have to make that stop, but it doesn't inspire much confidence given how similar films have, most notably at TIFF (*Hamnet* and *Women Talking* for *Sense and Sensibility*, and *The Boy and the Heron* and *The Wild Robot* for *Wildwood*). Matt even proclaimed that *Wildwood* would be a shoo-in to place at the PCA. I guess we'll never know.

<h3>Wicker / Mouse / Queen at Sea</h3>

Perhaps three of the most bizarre complete exclusions from fall festivals are the Sundance premiere *Wicker*, the celebrated indie *Mouse*, and the triple Berlin prize-winner *Queen at Sea*. Hopefully these films can find their footing at BFI and other regional festivals, but this is a bit disappointing.

<h3>I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning / Red Rocks</h3>

Other major exclusions include the Director's Fortnight People's Choice Award winner *I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning* and Bruno Dumont's new film *Red Rocks*. Clio Barnard has been a TIFF staple and her new film is among her very best, while Dumont rarely misses TIFF. *Red Rocks* will be present in the NYFF Currents program, but that is a puzzling step down. Why these two films haven't shown up more is a mystery.

<h3>Other NYFF world premieres: A Statement and Heil Jupiter</h3>

Good for NYFF for getting two major filmmakers to world premiere at their festival in Tom McCarthy and Carlos Reygadas, but we can't help but beg the question: why didn't this premiere elsewhere? What is most damning is that both of these films aren't programmed in the Main Slate, which gives their world premiere a smaller platform to start with. Is this a repeat of the disappointment of *Anemone* last year?

<h2>Missing in Action</h2>

Films like *Digger* and *Werwulf* either willingly opted not to go to film festivals, or weren't expected to show up in the first place. These films also have a studio and a confirmed release date behind them. But for *Cry to Heaven* and *Jack of Spades*, a no-show on the fall festival circuit virtually guarantees the films won't be coming out this year. Expect these two titles to pop up next year alongside *The Entertainment System Is Down* and *What Happens at Night*.

Recent reports of *The Adventures of Cliff Booth* going through reshoots may push it to next year, but either way, it would not have been ready for fall festivals. *Saturn Return* and *Here Comes the Flood* seem poised to be released next year as well, although their absence from festivals this fall may be more of a red flag than the other films listed.
