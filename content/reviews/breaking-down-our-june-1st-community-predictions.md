In the official Fantasy Filmball Discord server, over 50 members have their upcoming Oscar season predictions pulled from their Award Expert app and tallied up to see what we're predicting as a community. This year, our first predictions were filled out on April 3rd, before we even had the official lineup for Cannes and, simply put, when a lot of films weren't on our radar yet.

Almost two months later, Cannes has come and gone, and we have updated our predictions. Now, we are going to break down what the above-the-line predictions look like, and what has changed.

<h2>Biggest Movers</h2>

There were some obvious changes since the last update, like the fall of *Narnia: The Magician's Nephew* after its delay to a 2027 release date. In our April 3rd update, Gerwig's upcoming project ranked 6th in Best Picture, with just over 70% predicting a nomination, and was also as high as second in both Production Design and Costume Design. It will be interesting to see if we rally behind this movie the same way next year. Although it wasn't in the top ten in any categories in April, *Ink* was also revealed to be a 2027 release, and has subsequently been removed from all predictions.

Rumors of strong test screenings for *Behemoth!* have led many people to feel encouraged to predict it. The movie rose 10 whole spots in Best Picture, from 18th to 8th, with 56% of people now predicting it, up from 11% previously. This is by far the most notable rise in this category among the movies already on our radar in April. Pedro Pascal also rose from 11th to 6th in Lead Actor, just missing out on our top 5. Pascal feels like a perfect candidate to finally break into the awards race this season. Tony Gilroy's Original Screenplay also made its way into our official predictions with a rise from 18% to 53%. Only two people are currently predicting Gilroy in Director, leaving him down at 13th place. It feels a little strange that more people aren't considering him, given he's a previous Director nominee for *Michael Clayton*, but if *Behemoth!* continues this show of force, we might see Gilroy rise here.

Among movies already released this year, the most notable drop from April to June was for *Michael*, with the film's Best Picture prediction rate dropping from 42% to 7%. The movie also fell off in several other above-the-line categories, including Jaafar Jackson dropping from 46% to 9% in Actor, Colman Domingo dropping steeply from 75% to 17% in Supporting Actor, and Nia Long even losing all 10 of the predictors she had for Supporting Actress in April. The reason for this is obvious: as of writing, the movie has a 38% on Rotten Tomatoes and a 39 on Metacritic. The potential counterpoint? *Michael* is moonwalkin' its way to nearly $1 billion worldwide, showing proof of true audience passion. This is a movie I'll be looking out for to see if these stats ever go back up.

*No One Cares* is another movie with a downward trend, falling 7 spots in Best Picture, from 8th to 15th. However, unlike *Michael*, this drop is likely due to us still not knowing enough about it to keep our confidence. There's seemingly no proof *No One Cares* is even the official, final title. This is a movie I wanted to believe in, but other movies that feel more concrete are easier to predict.

As for the movies that premiered at Cannes, *Fatherland* had one of the larger moves of the update, dropping from 7th to 13th in Best Picture. Sandra Hüller is still in our Actress predictions but has dropped from 73% to 49%, making her status much more uncertain. *All of a Sudden* had mostly downward trends, with the exception of the two actresses, as Virginie Efira held her 7th-place position in lead and Tao Okamoto moved up from 5th to 4th in supporting.

*Fjord* is a more complicated case. On the surface, winning the Palme should've been a net positive for the film, and it is still very solidly in our predictions, but it had a slight drop across the board. It went from 87% to 82% in Best Picture, and Cristian Mungiu dropped from 79% to 71% in Director, but the bigger knocks were in its acting prospects, where Sebastian Stan shifted from 88% to 68%, and Renate Reinsve was notably down 78%, from 91% in April. Whether this movement is simply a result of our community being true *Fjord* believers in April or more people feeling skeptical after hearing the rumors about controversial themes is anyone's guess, but *Fjord* is yet another film to keep tabs on.

<h2>New Additions</h2>

*The Black Ball* is by far our biggest new hit. It enters our predictions ranked 5th for Best Picture with an 81% nomination rate, 4th for Best Director with 71%, 3rd for Supporting Actress (Penélope Cruz) with 63%, and 3rd for Adapted Screenplay with 67%. As far as Cruz goes, I'm not sure if she makes sense as the film's sole acting push, given that she's rumored to have such a small role. With the potential exception of *All of a Sudden*, we're still missing any real "unknowns." Spanish actor Guitarricadelafuente has also cropped up in both Actor and Supporting Actor, but only at 16th in lead and 11th in supporting. Even if uncertainty over which category he'll be in was cleared up, he wouldn't have made it higher than 10th in either, but he's a name to watch as the season progresses.

*Club Kid*'s stats aren't quite as high, but it is a movie we're now taking note of. It only made our official nominations in 5th place in Original Screenplay, with 40% of people predicting it. Additionally, it premiered just outside our Best Picture ten, at 11th, and Jordan Firstman appeared in both Director and Actor, at 17th and 13th, respectively. The best thing going for this movie is that it just feels different than anything else we're largely predicting, in the best way possible. It's an indie movie adored by audiences. Detractors argue that it is Firstman's feature-film directorial debut and that Firstman potentially lacks the connections and respect that are generally necessary for Oscar success.

The most notable non-Cannes title addition, *The Adventures of Cliff Booth*, made its first appearances at 17th in Best Picture and 9th in Adapted Screenplay, while Elizabeth Debicki rose 12 spots from 27th to 15th in Supporting Actress. If Netflix hadn't bought *The Black Ball*, and this was more universally looked at as their number one play at the moment, it would be interesting to see if it would have peaked higher. As a semi-sequel to a previous Best Picture nominee, it comes with the same hesitancy that other sequels as of late do. However, its current prime release date and IMAX theatrical rollout plan suggest a push is incoming.

Finally, we have a new prediction that has surfaced since April that, in all honesty, seems a bit puzzling: Michelle Williams in Actress for *A Place in Hell*. She made her first appearance in these predictions in an impressive 6th place, the highest new prediction placement for any movie that wasn't recently seen at Cannes. 45% of predictors, or 25 out of 55, are now predicting her for a nomination, while 18%, or 10 predictors out of 55, are predicting her for the win. Yet, among all of those people, only one person is predicting it for a Best Picture nomination, and only one person is predicting it for an Original Screenplay nomination. Daisy Edgar-Jones appears in Supporting Actress with 5 people predicting the nomination.

This random push for a solo Best Actress play for a movie people seem to not really believe in at all otherwise currently feels very off, but if enough people are truly that passionate about Michelle Williams getting an Oscar after losing five times, it will be interesting to see if the movie itself has more potential.

<h2>General Observations</h2>

In our first predictions on April 3rd, only one pick across all of the categories had a 100% nomination rate: Tom Cruise for Best Actor in *Digger*. This makes sense, as it's an easy narrative to buy into, as long as *Digger* is the hit we're expecting it to be. Tom Cruise doesn't have an Oscar yet, and this could be his time.

In this edition, two other above-the-line nominations have joined the 100% club. Martin McDonagh's Original Screenplay for *Wild Horse Nine*, which only missed one voter in April, is the first. McDonagh's movies are always screenplay-driven, so screenplay is still an easy bet for a predicted powerhouse.

The other is *Project Hail Mary*, which has a unanimous nomination rate in Best Picture, a solid increase from the 89% nomination rate it had in April, with 6 people leaving it out at that time. This pick might be a bit more surprising to some, but there's usually at least one movie from earlier on in the year, and it's hard to argue against this being the one.

These are three of the four movies that our predictors are seeing strong years for, with *The Odyssey* being the fourth. While *The Odyssey* doesn't have 100% in any category, it's still our top predicted movie overall. Only one predictor has it missing Best Picture, and 33% of our total body has it winning, the highest total of any movie. Similarly, a staggering 55% predict Christopher Nolan will win Best Director.

Of course, some categories have stronger consensus than others. Our top five in Best Director consists of Christopher Nolan for *The Odyssey*, Alejandro G. Iñárritu for *Digger*, Martin McDonagh for *Wild Horse Nine*, Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo for *The Black Ball*, and Cristian Mungiu for *Fjord*. All five of these directors have over 70% predicting them for nominations, with a steep drop-off to 6th place, where only 18% predict Phil Lord and Christopher Miller for *Project Hail Mary*, which is still enough to place them in sixth. This is our strongest consensus top five in any category.
