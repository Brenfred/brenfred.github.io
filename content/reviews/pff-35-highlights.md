The 35th annual Philadelphia Film Festival officially wrapped up last week, and the 2026 edition was jam-packed with Oscar hopefuls, international feature submissions, guest Q&As, and restoration screenings on the big screen.

The Philadelphia Film Festival is a celebration of cinema from across the globe, with programmers highlighting and showcasing over 100 titles across 10 days of festival screenings. I wanted to take some time to highlight some of the best offerings from this year’s record breaking festival, including films I saw and loved, films I missed out on due to just how much was jammed packed on the schedule, and even films I may not have adored but that clearly inspired passion from one audience or another.

Starting things off, I want to highlight my favorite film of the festival, a movie that absolutely moved me unlike anything else I’ve seen this year: Beth de Araújo’s *Josephine*. Mason Reeves delivers such a powerful performance that it deserves recognition not just among the best performances by a child actor, but among the best performances of the entire year. Watching a child lose their innocence in real time breaks your heart, but the care and compassion surrounding the story make you feel held and cared for throughout its two-hour runtime. Read my full review [HERE](/review.html?slug=josephine).

Other festival highlights include the recipient of the top prize at this year’s fest, the Narrative Audience Prize, *Club Kid*. *Club Kid* marks Jordan Firstman’s feature film directing debut, outside of the Director’s chair Firstman wrote and starred in this audience hit about a New York City club promoter who suddenly has to become a dad. Read my full review [HERE](/review.html?slug=club-kid). As well as the real-life married couple of Penélope Cruz and Javier Bardem in Florian Zeller’s new film *Bunker*. While the film received mixed reactions coming out of the Venice Film Festival, my Philadelphia Film Festival audience surely ate it up. The room was electric and buzzing throughout the screening and even after the credits rolled. Talking with fellow cinephiles afterward, everyone seemed to highlight what a phenomenal year Cruz is having and how blown away they were by watching the dynamic between Cruz and Bardem play out in this tense dramatic thriller.

Another pair of festival-stealing performances has to go to two potential frontrunners in the lead acting categories at this year’s Academy Awards. Julianne Moore in *The Debut* and John Malkovich in *Wild Horse Nine* both deliver hilarious comedic performances that rank among the best of their careers. You may think you already love Moore and Malkovich as actors after watching them for decades, but they showcase that the job is far from done, giving us two truly extraordinary performances that are surely going to earn them some awards-season love. Read Brad Derfner’s review of *The Debut* [HERE](/review.html?slug=the-debut) and Vitor Cecchinato’s review of *Wild Horse Nine* [HERE](/review.html?slug=wild-horse-nine).

Speaking of the Academy Awards, Netflix’s big awards juggernaut and potential Best Picture contender, *La Bola Negra*, was a centerpiece selection at this year’s festival. The Spanish Oscar submission hasn’t just won a major prize at the Cannes Film Festival and the Toronto International Film Festival’s People’s Choice Award; it has also captured the hearts and minds of the Philadelphia Film Festival audience. Across both weekends of the festival, throughout my various conversations with attendees, this film kept coming up. Whether it was someone saying they loved it and considered it the best film of the year, or someone expressing admiration and wanting to learn more about the filmmaking process, it was clearly a film that left an impression.

In addition to *La Bola Negra*, other major Awards contenders screening at this year’s festival include the Palme d’Or-winning *Fjord* starring Oscar nominees Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve in a legal thriller destined to provoke conversation, Andrew Scott’s acting vehicle *Elsinore*, which garnered the British actor a major claim in the awards conversation starring as famed thespian Ian Charleson in his final day, as well as Searchlight’s *Behemoth!*, starring Pedro Pascal as a cellist returning to his roots and uncovering deep truths. The film even brought its writer-director, Tony Gilroy, to the City of Brotherly Love for a fun Q&A, where he shared insights into the creative process behind getting his passion project funded, securing nine different composers to contribute to the original score, and standing his ground on such a catchy title. Siân Heder’s follow-up to her Best Picture winner *CODA*, *Being Heumann*, also screened at this year’s Philadelphia Film Festival. The film tells the story of disability rights activist Judy Heumann and the sit-in that eventually helped lead to equal rights for people with disabilities. It’s a film poised for Oscar success later this year.

One running theme of this year’s Philadelphia Film Festival was highlighting our big brother down the street: the Big Apple. New York City was on full display at this year’s fest, from the opening-night film, *The Only Living Pickpocket in New York*, a breezy film sprawling across the five boroughs and starring John Turturro, to the aforementioned laugh-out-loud audience favorite *Club Kid*, to James Gray’s latest epic diving into the heart of the New York mob scene. With all this love for the Big Apple, it’s even more confusing why Philadelphia’s own *I Play Rocky* was excluded from this year’s lineup.

One of my favorite parts of attending a film festival is the ability to see movies you typically don’t get the chance to catch on the silver screen, especially when they come from across the world. One of my favorites at this year’s festival was the South Korean journalism and police procedural drama *The Assassin(s)*, which had me hooked from start to finish. It’s absolutely the type of film that would typically fly under the radar without being platformed by a film festival.

Other highlights from films across the globe include the gut wrenching Palestinian documentary *Naza*, the Korean submission to the Academy Awards and the latest from renowned filmmaker Lee Chang-Dong, *Possible Love*, Poland’s Oscar submission, Paweł Pawlikowski’s *Fatherland*, once again showcasing the director’s brilliant eye behind the lens and his unique shot compositions in a breezy 80-minute drama starring Sandra Hüller. And, of course, there was Ryûsuke Hamaguchi’s latest three-hour-plus sprawling tale, *All of a Sudden*, about an assisted-care facility manager and a theater director who is also a cancer patient. It’s a film that makes you feel love, compassion, and heartbreak, all packed into a single experience. Read Terence Peterson’s full review [HERE](/review.html?slug=nyff-64-highlights).

And finally, one of my new favorite aspects of film festivals is the repertory screening: bringing classics back to the big screen so audiences can revisit them or experience them for the first time, sometimes in a format they may never have seen before. This year, the Philadelphia Film Festival brought a few iconic titles back to the big screen, including two legendary films presented in glorious 70mm. Both Steven Spielberg’s *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* and Stanley Kubrick’s *2001: A Space Odyssey* were shown in the City of Brotherly Love, giving audiences the opportunity to experience these cinematic landmarks in all their big-screen glory.

All in all, that’s a wrap on this year’s Philadelphia Film Festival!
