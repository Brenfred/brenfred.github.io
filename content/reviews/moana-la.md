In 2016, we got the original animated version of *Moana*, a lovely kids' musical adventure movie about a teenage Polynesian girl named Moana tracking down Maui, a demigod who stole the heart of the goddess Te Fiti, in hopes that he will help restore it. The story was great, the animation was vibrant and colorful, and the songs were catchy and memorable. If that were the movie being reviewed here, there would be a five-star rating at the top, followed by a glowing review.

A decade later, we get one of the clearest examples of why the shot for shot animated-to-live-action remake formula just doesn’t work.

It’s hard to fully hate a remake of a movie you love. The songs are still catchy, the story is still engaging, and it has a fun journey baked into it. The opening scenes set us in a more natural landscape, getting to see a live-action version of the island and humans playing these roles in front of the camera. At first, it can be kind of fun. Yet, that setup almost causes a problem in itself. Once we get to parts of the movie that are, by nature of the original story, more necessary to have animation or fantasy-esque qualities, they feel like they exist on a different plane than some of the more naturalistic scenes.

Maui’s big introduction song, “You’re Welcome,” in particular, feels plucked out of a different movie aesthetically than everything that came before it. The song is goofy, usually in a good way, but here it breaks out of the more realistic look of the movie to include animation and effects that feel inconsistent and even confusing, style-wise. This isn’t the only example of the movie not being exactly sure what it’s going for in the visuals department, but it’s the first one that really stood out. The scene including “Shiny” also stands out aesthetically in a way that feels misfit.

The use of color in general falls flat here. Not only is the contrast between the brighter colors and darker colors overly jarring, but the darker scenes in particular look so unappealing. There are scenes where it’s beyond clear that a green screen is being used, as well as scenes where the water effects and other effects surrounding the characters are mediocre, making it clear they’re not on actual water and lacking some of the magic of the moving water effects from the original. Almost everything leaves much to be desired visually that worked so well in animation.

The Rock reprising his role as Maui was, overall, a good idea. He brings the right personality to the role that worked well here. It just wasn’t always easy to take him seriously with the wig and bodysuit. The other performances are fine, if not mostly likable, but nothing to write home about or elevate the movie around them.

Overall, the original *Moana* was written with so many aspects that were meant to be animated. Directly translating that to live action was almost bound to fail no matter what, but it doesn’t even feel like they tried to work around it. They simply allowed this film to be so many steps down from a visual standpoint, unable to justify why anyone would choose this over the original, which looks so much more vibrant. While the story and music that made the original so great are still present to some extent, enough to save it from being completely atrocious, everything around it bogs it down into a movie that just never needed to be made.

One can only hope that this trend of live-action remakes will soon be put to an end, but if not, they deserve more creativity in their reimagining than this.
