*Young Washington* is Angel Studios’ attempt at a biopic of America’s first president. Released on the 250th anniversary of America, it would seem Angel Studios knew exactly what they were doing, making the most patriotic film of all time. They failed when you consider that the movie isn’t remotely about America or the American Revolution, but rather George Washington’s early days as an aspiring British subject who wishes to fight for the Royal Crown. It’s an… interesting direction, to say the least, but under most studios, you might have an inspiring film about overcoming the odds while questioning the very morals and goals Washington has lived by.

Angel Studios is not most studios.

On the technical side, *Young Washington* fails. The editing rarely builds tension, the sound design lacks impact, and the cinematography relies so heavily on handheld camerawork that it loses any dramatic effect it hopes to create. Rather than adding urgency, the constant movement makes every scene feel visually monotonous. Combined with a flat, desaturated color palette, even moments meant to showcase the beauty of the American frontier feel lifeless. Characters eventually have to tell us how beautiful everything is because the visuals certainly won't.

It's important to note that a director affects more than just a film's visual style. They are responsible for the creation of every element put to screen to portray a specific vision. Jon Erwin's direction never gives the material a distinct identity. Scene after scene simply moves the plot forward without creating memorable visual moments or emotional weight. When tackling an ambitious story such as that of *Young Washington*, it just felt like every scene was an aimless progression from one plot point to the next with no style. I do have to wonder whether the film was directed by a person or a board of executives.

For a period piece, you would expect *Young Washington* to boast a creative flair to transport you back to the time period. However, between the unmemorable makeup design, costumes and sets that offer the bare minimum expected of any period piece, and a score that doesn't possess a single memorable theme, I'm left with nothing to latch onto in *Young Washington*. That is not even mentioning the visual effects. Even if you are not expecting much from a film that openly admitted to using generative AI within the production process, the end result is awful and looks unfinished.

While *Young Washington* may boast an all-star cast with names such as Andy Serkis, Kelsey Grammer, Mary-Louise Parker, and Ben Kingsley, they all feel squandered because of underwritten characters that never evolve beyond broad archetypes. Even then, it's a film about George Washington. As long as you nail this casting, the rest can be forgiven, but newcomer William Franklyn-Miller never finds the dramatic depth needed for a convincing Washington. His performance lacks dramatic depth, building a stale protagonist that feels written by a five-year-old.

That said, I wouldn’t throw most of the blame on them. The roles the ensemble are handed feel flat and one-note across the board, which is especially a shame when this even applies to your central lead and the main draw of even coming to see *Young Washington*. In truth, the main culprit behind *Young Washington’*s quality isn’t the lifeless direction. It isn’t the unmemorable ensemble, and not even the AI visuals. The true issue plaguing *Young Washington* is an incredibly clumsy script. After spending nearly two hours chronicling Washington's rise within the British military, the story abruptly rushes toward what I can assume is why most came to the cinemas and gives us closing text, making the emotional payoff feel completely unearned. Rather than exploring Washington's ideological transformation, the film simply skips over it, leaving the central arc feeling incomplete. Every bit of growth comes through someone else's misfortune rather than his own actions, making the film somehow more boring than a history lecture.

*Young Washington*'s themes are questionable at best, taking significant liberties with history, from a set piece revolving around Washington running through a battlefield while dodging bullets like he's in *The Matrix* to another involving a group of Native Americans bringing him to a cliff and anointing him "The Chosen One." Creative liberties can work when they serve a larger thematic purpose, but here they only further distance the film from the grounded story it appears to be telling.

*Young Washington* isn't just disappointing because it's technically uneven or dramatically flat. It's disappointing because it never commits to the fascinating story sitting at its center. If the film was going to be this much of a misfire, I would have preferred if it were 5% worse. Make a *Velocipastor*-level comedy that's just George Washington doing a *John Wick* homage. Instead, Angel Studios put in just enough effort to seem like they tried and failed miserably. It's technically incompetent, poorly directed, awfully acted, and horribly written, but worst of all, a film released to commemorate America's 250th anniversary should leave audiences with a deeper understanding of George Washington's legacy. Instead, it barely explores the defining chapter of his life and is less patriotic than the Minions movie it was released alongside.
