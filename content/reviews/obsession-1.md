The horror genre hasn’t always been the most beloved to the general public, but the 2020s have brought about a revolution in horror movies, catapulting them to the mainstream like never before. Between the intrigue of unique premises of *Smile* and *A Quiet Place*, the incredible acclaim of *Sinners* and *The Substance*, and the social commentary of *The Menu* and *Heretic*, the genre is undeniably experiencing a golden age. Spearheading this movement is a group seemingly unrelated to horror movies: YouTubers. 

In the last few years, *Iron Lung*, *Talk to Me*, and the *Backrooms* film were all directed and largely produced by influencers. These debut features have gone on to be successful, with *Backrooms* meeting over 80 million in its opening weekend, well over triple the highest in A24’s history. While these have gone on to break records and garner a gigantic amount of love, there is one film more deserving to talk about love. 

***Barking Up the Box Office***

*Obsession*, directed by Curry Barker, premiered last year within the Midnight Madness program at the Toronto International Film Festival. At the festival it was largely praised, and it ended up as the runner-up for the Midnight Madness People’s Choice. After a run at multiple festivals, Obsession has released to incredible acclaim, debuting with a 4.2 on Letterboxd upon its wide release, placing it within the platform’s top 500 films of all time. This makes it one of just 24 horror films on the list, and the only one from the 2020s. It also scored an A- on CinemaScore, a score attained by fewer than 20 horror movies, putting it in the company of films such as *Get Out*.

*Obsession*’s release hasn’t just garnered critical success, but has been a financial wonder. It released to a 16 million dollar opening weekend, practically double its prior tracking of 7-10 million. This made it third for the whole weekend. *Obsession* would then continue to hold impressively throughout the week, putting up numbers not just comparable to last year’s *Sinners*, but making a case that it is doing noticeably better with holds.

Weirdly enough, the truly insane part of this film’s box office run would come on the following weekend. Despite the release of the horror movie *Passenger* and blockbuster *Mandalorian and Grogu*, *Obsession* would increase its weekend gross by 39% on its second weekend, the second-highest hold from first to second weekend for any summer movie playing in over 1,000 theaters. 

Despite the added competition of *Backrooms*, *Obsession* would still increase its weekend gross from its second weekend to its third. *Obsession* isn’t just a unique case within all of film, but it is particularly intriguing within the horror genre. A vast majority of horror films have disappointing drops, often ranging between 50% and 60%. To not just avoid dropping at all, but increase by nearly 40%, is unprecedented.

It does raise a question, however. What is up with…

***The Obsession with Obsession:***

The movie centers on Bear (Michael Johnston), a hopeless romantic with a crush on his friend Nikki (Inde Navarrette). After stumbling upon a novelty toy known as a One Wish Willow, he snaps it and wishes Nikki would love him. What follows is the exact chaos and insanity promised by the trailers. 

*Obsession* is the feature directorial debut of Barker, a YouTuber previously known from the sketch comedy duo, *That’s a Bad Idea*. On the duo’s YouTube channel, Barker directed horror short films alongside co-producer and actor Cooper Tomlinson, who would later play the character of Ian, Bear’s douchey friend. With the release of *Obsession*, Barker becomes not just one in the group of YouTubers to transition into horror films, but perhaps the most acclaimed.

*Obsession*’s praise is a result of many aspects of the film. People have mentioned things such as the score or the themes it touches on as excellent parts of it, but the two things that stand out are the excellent tension and the incredible performances. The film rarely relies on jump scares, instead building suspense naturally, and its ensemble includes characters perfect at hitting the notes required by the script.

At the center of all of this is the film’s most talked about aspect, the star-making performance of Navarrette. This is one of Navarrette’s first major performances, her first being a recurring role in the series *Superman & Lois*. While she had little to do there and little to no acclaim prior to her turn as Nikki, practically every review of *Obsession*, no matter the negativity, highlights her as the pinnacle of the film. Many say she’s providing an all timer performance of the genre, where comparisons have been drawn (if exaggerated) to Isabelle Adjani’s legendary performance in *Possession*.

Between the incredible acclaim for Navarrette’s performance, unbelievably high audience scores, and extremely promising box office results, one question has begun to be asked, one which may be all too familiar to many.

***In the Witch’s Shadow:***

I mentioned earlier three main factors: a unique premise, incredible acclaim, and prominent social commentary. Upon viewing *Obsession*, it’s safe to say it covers all three of those with ease, but just last year, another film did the same: Zach Cregger’s *Weapons*. While it did not have quite the insane Letterboxd score of *Obsession*, the film made up for it by matching its CinemaScore of A-, earning an even greater Metacritic score, and grossing a whopping 150 million dollars domestically and 270 million worldwide.

From the release of *Weapons* rose one beloved aspect: the performance of Hollywood veteran Amy Madigan as the haunting Aunt Gladys. Despite her performance being completely concealed from any prior marketing, the release catapulted Madigan into the spotlight. Despite *Weapons* being a horror film, a genre often ignored by awards, Madigan defied the odds, winning the Oscar for Best Supporting Actress without a BAFTA nomination or any other Oscar nominations for the film except her own. Madigan was a standout performance from an acclaimed horror movie that became an audience hit and thrived throughout awards season. With *Obsession*’s release, the question is simple: Can Inde Navarrette become this year's Amy Madigan?

In truth, Madigan and Navarrette’s performances could not be more different when looked at in a vacuum. Madigan is a true supporting performance found exclusively in the film’s latter half, who gained major strength on account of being a Hollywood veteran and being beloved within the industry. Navarrette is a complete newcomer who teeters on the brink of a lead performance and is found in practically every scene of the film. Despite the differences, comparisons have been drawn between the two, and Navarrette has found herself on the lips of numerous Oscar pundits.

On the app Award Expert, Navarrette is currently #7 in the Supporting Actress category. That may already be impressive, but there are two interesting facts to add. Just a month ago, Navarrette was hovering around #50 with almost no mentions in predictions whatsoever. In a month, she has risen from a complete irrelevance to a shockingly prominent contender. The second thing to note is that she has also been largely thrown around within the Lead Actress category, where she is currently #9. If the odds she has in both lead and supporting were combined, she would likely remain #7 in Supporting Actress but rise all the way to #6 in Lead Actress.

***The Road to Awards Season***

*Obsession* is one of few movies this early in the year to receive major chatter of awards contention, with the major name thrown around being *Project Hail Mary*, but the path Navarrette would need to take to reach the Academy Awards is a much steeper one than the Sci-Fi hit. Navarrette’s praise is practically unanimous among viewers, so the real difficulty would be bringing voters’ attention to the film. This rests Navarrette’s fate squarely on the shoulders of Focus Features, the film’s distributor.

Standing in her way is Georgia Oakley’s remake of *Sense and Sensibility*, another Focus release that carries its own Supporting Actress contender in Esmé Creed-Miles. The film’s original release date in September has been pushed back to October, a move many have theorized is an attempt to position it for fall festivals such as TIFF. If so, Focus could throw Navarrette under the bus to make the category easier for Creed-Miles. While there is promise in the movie, reactions to the trailer from CinemaCon and teasers prior to screenings of *Devil Wears Prada 2* have produced middling reactions. 

Despite Inde’s small time within the mainstream, it seems clear that there has been intense effort into launching her into the spotlight, down to an appearance on Jimmy Kimmel Live. While a misfire on *Sense and Sensibility* may not clear the way for Navarrette, it may open the door for her to be more present in the season than could have been imagined. 

On April 20th, I  was struggling with my predictions, particularly the Supporting Actress category. I had two contenders I felt very safe predicting and one I felt fine with, which left me with two slots to fill. After spending much longer scouring the category than I’d like to admit, I came to the conclusion that it was barren. The only contenders remaining were ones that reportedly do almost nothing or ones that I just couldn’t see a path for.

I then remembered a movie I had seen at a film festival only a few days earlier. A film that I left thinking had the best performance of the year by a mile, a performance that whenever I talked to people after the screening, produced the exact same reaction. When I thought back to that excellent performance, I chose to make a call and placed Navarrette into the category. I did so without much faith and told myself and many others that this was practically a placeholder, but I did say that out of everyone left, this was the only path I could somehow see. Today, *Obsession* is doing phenomenally, with a record-breaking box office, ludicrous reviews, and my faith fully placed in a nomination for Inde Navarrette.
