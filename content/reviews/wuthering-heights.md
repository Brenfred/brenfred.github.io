If you know me, you know that Emerald Fennell’s “*Wuthering Heights*” has been one of my most anticipated movies of 2026, that “fall in love” trailer is just so intoxicating. 

With that being said, I do want to start by talking about Fennell as a writer and director, because she has truly drives me mad. *Promising Young Woman* was such a promising start for a brand-new filmmaker, headlined by an outstanding lead performance from Carey Mulligan, a screenplay that always felt one step ahead of its audience, & a bold, distinctive directorial voice unlike anything we’d seen before. However, her next film, *Saltburn*, was basically the opposite experience for me. I found Barry Keoghan to be a massive miscast as our lead… very unlikable, very repelling, & not really hitting the narrative beats you’d want from a character like that. But you could also place a lot of the blame on the screenplay, which felt messy + pretty unoriginal… pulling from things we’ve seen before and not in a unique or interesting way. That blandness also extended to the direction. Overall, it was a massive miss for me… outside of Jacob Elordi’s supporting performance, who was very charming. Elordi has shown over the last few years that he’s often the best part of whatever he’s in. We just had *Frankenstein*, where he could potentially win the Oscar as the standout in a very technically impressive film. Obviously, there’s *Saltburn*, & then of course *Priscilla*, where he brought a whole new depth to the King of Rock.

But now we’re here with “*Wuthering Heights*” we have a film that feels like the full embodiment of Fennell as a filmmaker. A primal epic that takes the highest of highs of her previous work & combines them with the lowest lows. 

Let’s start with the positives. Fennell clearly has an eye behind the lens. Whether it’s the dreamy, dollhouse-like sets or the fairytale visual aesthetic of the cinematography, “Wuthering Heights” is a gorgeous film to look at. Your eyes yearn for the grand spectacle on full force … but at the same time, your mind kind of wants to drift somewhere else. Because just like Saltburn’s messy plotting, and some of the lack of attention to the ensemble in Promising Young Woman, you really don’t care about a lot of these characters. 

Cath & Heathcliff are very compelling as children, but once they age into our main stars, their dynamic feels a little off. Yes, Jacob Elordi & Margot Robbie are two unbelievably beautiful human beings who look as perfect as this film does… but their chemistry just doesn’t fully resonate. I found Cath to be pretty annoying as a protagonist… constantly whining, making the wrong choices, & hurting everyone close to her. On the other hand, Heathcliff remains a mystery. I know that’s kind of the point, but just like Elordi’s character in Saltburn, you admire the mystery, yet you fail to resonate with the reality. Hong Chau is an actress who never really gets her flowers, and once again she’s doing her best here as Nelly. But the role she’s given has almost no depth. We understand she’s jealous because of her childhood, but it’s never explored. Same thing with Edgar, Cath’s husband. We get that he cares, then he gets jealous, but we never really see how that shift affects him or changes the dynamic in a meaningful way. Honestly, the most compelling character might be Isabelle. Can I get a little woof woof?

Fennell is clearly a divisive filmmaker. We’ve seen the polar-opposite reactions to her previous films, which I very much embody. With this one, I opened Letterboxd and the first thing I see is one friend gave it half a star and another gives it five. That’s cinema. I think movies like this should be encouraged. It’s provocative. It starts conversation. It feels fresh and visually unique. My main reservation with “Wuthering Heights” is that while it’s doing all of that visually and aesthetically, at its core it feels like something we’ve seen a million times before, the classic period romance where two people are perfect for each other but don’t realize it until it’s too late, and by then there’s nothing they can do.

I also think a lot of people are heading into “*Wuthering Heights*” with some negative energy built up… whether it’s Fennell’s track record, the type of movie this is, or even the casting of the two leads. While I am a massive fan of both Margot Robbie and Jacob Elordi, this isn’t their best work. I’ve seen the complaints online that Robbie is “too old” to play Cath, since in the novel Cath is just beginning her adulthood and on the flip side Heathcliff is depicted as a man of color… which uhhhh, Jacob Elordi is not. It would be interesting to see another (maybe more focused) filmmaker’s take on this story with more novel-accurate casting. But I don’t think that alone is a reason to demean the film we got.

“*Wuthering Heights*” ultimately comes undone in a messy… but a vividly compelling way. Chains of Love is a banger but was kinda let down the rest of Charli’s musical contributions. "*Wuthering Heights*" may have the trailer of the year but I can definitely see some audiences be repelled by the movie Fennell actually does deliver.
