There’s a perfect irony in Martin McDonagh’s newest film arriving directly after a blue wave in America. It’s almost too perfect a moment for what I believe is one of the year’s best films. *Wild Horse Nine* is a comedy-thriller from the director of *The Banshees of Inisherin*, *In Bruges*, and *Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri*. With multiple Oscar nominations under his belt and a win for his short film *Six Shooter*, McDonagh returns with another witty filmmaking marvel, one that feels particularly relevant in the current political and cultural climate.

Following its premiere at the Venice Film Festival, *Wild Horse Nine* received lukewarm reactions from critics but glowing responses from audiences, with John Malkovich’s performance earning widespread praise and ultimately winning him Venice’s Best Actor award, the Volpi Cup. The film continued its festival run through Telluride, Toronto, and London, among several others, before its recent theatrical release. Despite the somewhat divided critical reception, there's plenty to celebrate here.

The first thing anybody has to talk about with *Wild Horse Nine* is John Malkovich, who well and truly deserves all the praise. Standing out as a phenomenal performer among a field of incredible performances, Malkovich is funny, emotional, and, all around, a very worthy awards contender. His ability to move between comedic absurdity and genuine emotional vulnerability makes for some of the film's most entertaining moments. It would be one thing if he were the sole highlight, but there are plenty of other great performances. Rockwell is simply incredible, delivering a performance deserving of an Oscar for Best Supporting Reactions to whatever Malkovich says. Mariana Di Girolamo is a fantastic find, right alongside a strong performance from Ailín Salas. Every other role is evidently well executed, from Steve Buscemi and Tom Waits to Parker Posey and Paola Giannini, all of whom make the most of their minor yet memorable roles.

While Malkovich has been the biggest topic of discussion, perhaps the most prevalent conversation by the season's end will center on McDonagh's screenplay, which is primed to finally earn him his coveted screenplay Oscar. There's no doubt in my mind that he would deserve it. McDonagh demonstrates once again his incredible ability to balance comedy and drama, weaving together moments of joy, genuine emotion, and sharp commentary before occasionally sprinkling in a bit of "cheesiness." His dialogue is as entertaining as ever, with character interactions that make even the smallest exchanges a joy to watch. More importantly, the screenplay never loses sight of its characters, allowing the humor and emotional weight to coexist without either feeling particularly forced. It's truly such a joy to watch every moment unfold, with fantastic dialogue and an unbelievably entertaining story.

Carter Burwell's haunting score is the standout element of the film's incredible technical design, giving the story an additional emotional dimension. Its cinematography and editing are electric and fun, complementing the film's comedic rhythm and more dramatic moments, bolstered by subdued yet excellent sound design. There are smaller elements to celebrate, such as the production and costume design, but *Wild Horse Nine* perhaps isn't the giant technical showcase that films like *La Bola Negra* and *The Odyssey* are this year. That's hardly a criticism, though, as McDonagh's greatest strengths have always been in his writing and his ability to bring out extraordinary performances.

*Wild Horse Nine* was never going to be everyone's favorite film. Its first half may feel a little too inconsequential for some, and its particular brand of humor and storytelling won't necessarily work for everyone. But for those who connect with McDonagh's sensibilities, there's little doubt they'll find one of the most entertaining films of the year. In a year full of ambitious filmmaking, it's a reminder that sometimes all you need is a brilliant screenplay, a phenomenal ensemble, and a director who knows exactly how to make the most of both.
