It’s easy to see why so many awards fans think that the fall film festival season is the most exciting time of the awards race. Right now, at the end of September, races are wide open. Unlike later on in the year, categories have multiple win contenders, and some of these categories even have up to double-digit numbers of contenders fighting for a nomination.

This year, early October is marked not only by the end of TIFF, but also by the imminent release of Alejandro González Iñárritu’s *Digger*. Some critics may have seen it early to get their reactions out for the social embargo, but most cinema fans have yet to lay eyes on the Tom Cruise Oscar vehicle before its October 3rd release date.

As such, these are probably some of the most important community predictions of the season, as we gauge how our Filmball community currently predicts the race to go, ahead of regional and national critic groups releasing their picks over the next couple of months.

So, without further ado, let’s dive into the latest update from the Fantasy Filmball community for our post-fall film festival predictions.

<h2>Fall Film Fest Winners</h2>

Let’s start with the biggest winners of the festival circuit. Firstly, arguably the biggest winner is Netflix’s *La Bola Negra*. Obviously, Los Javis’ latest was already a huge contender before the fall fests, as *La Bola Negra* won Best Director at Cannes. In our last update, the community had already had it at number two in Best Picture, number two in Best Director, number four in Supporting Actress, and number one in Adapted Screenplay.

The key reason it is such a big winner, though, is that it is the first film to premiere at the Cannes Film Festival to win the Toronto International Film Festival’s People’s Choice Award since 2013, and the first international feature to win since 2011. Additionally, the film was released in Spain on September 25th and has risen to a 4.25 Letterboxd score.

In addition to retaining its number two spot in both Picture and Director, Penélope Cruz is still in the race for Supporting Actress, and Guitarricadelafuente has climbed five spots since our last community predictions update to number three in Supporting Actor. *La Bola Negra* is still the expected winner for Adapted Screenplay, International Feature, and our Filmball community has it receiving several additional nominations, including Best Casting, Cinematography, Editing, Production Design, Costume Design, Sound, Score, and Best Original Song for “La Nieve”.

Currently, the positive reactions from Telluride and award-winning performances at Toronto have cemented *La Bola Negra* as the main competitor for Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey*.

Another major winner of the fall film festival slate was *The Debut*. The Jesse Eisenberg-directed A24 comedy was outside Best Picture, but has now risen seven spots to number five overall. The community also has Julianne Moore and Paul Giamatti both winning their respective acting categories, with *The Debut* also showing up in Original Screenplay, rising four spots to second place. The Filmball community also has *The Debut* receiving nominations for Best Casting and Best Original Song for “This City May Have Chewed Me Up (But It Hasn’t Spit Me Out),” performed by Julianne Moore.

Other winners include *I Play Rocky* and *Being Heumann*, two films that joined *La Bola Negra* to form the TIFF PCA top three. Both films are currently being predicted for zero nominations across the Filmball community, but both have entered into the prospective field in several categories. They are now in the top 15 for Best Picture, each with a lead actor just outside the top of their respective category, as Ruth Madeley is currently #6 in Best Lead Actress and Anthony Ippolito is #8 in the Best Lead Actor race. Both films are also around 15% predicted for a nomination in their respective screenplay category, and *Being Heumann* is currently number 7 for Best Casting.

<h2>Specialty Categories Added to the Site</h2>

With awards season now fully underway, it was time for our website to reflect all below-the-line categories, including the specialty categories of International Feature, Animated Feature, and Best Documentary Feature.

In International Feature, *La Bola Negra* is currently dominating the predictions, with every single member of the Filmball community predicting it for the nomination and all but two members currently predicting it to win. The Spanish film is head and shoulders above the rest of the competition, with the Romanian submission *Fjord* and the Japanese submission *All of a Sudden* being next in line, as both are predicted for a nomination by at least 97% of the community.

The #4 slot is the last consensus pick of the category: South Korea’s submission *Possible Love*, which has 84% of the community backing a nomination. However, slots five through nine are a pretty tight race. Currently, Poland’s film *Fatherland* edges out the competition, while France’s *Minotaur*, Austria’s *Rose*, Germany’s *Everytime*, and Belgium’s *Coward* are on the outside looking in.

As for Best Animated Feature, Laika’s *Wildwood*, the studio’s return to the Oscar race after many years, is widely predicted to win, currently at 69%, with 95% of the community predicting it for the nomination. However, the *Toy Story* series is stiff competition, having won the award for both *Toy Story 3* and *Toy Story 4*. Even so, *Toy Story 5* is currently predicted to win by just 15% of the community, while films like *Iron Boy*, *Ray Gunn*, *Forgotten Island*, and *Tangles* have minimal support.

*Forgotten Island* and *Tangles* are currently on the outside looking in for community predictions, and Pixar’s other 2026 release, the original film *Hoppers*, is predicted by 71% of the Filmball community.

One of the biggest shakeups in the festivals was actually the introduction of three brand-new documentaries, all of which are currently being predicted by the Filmball community to be nominated for Best Documentary Feature.

First is the Venice award-winning follow-up to the Oscar award-winning *No Other Land*, *Naza*, which is being predicted by 64% of the Filmball community for a nomination. The A24, Nathan Fielder, and Lance Oppenheim team-up documentary covering Elizabeth Holmes, *You Can See Everything*, is currently being predicted for the nom by half of the Filmball community, with around 22% of the members predicting it for the win. Ava DuVernay’s follow-up to her Oscar-nominated documentary *13th*, *14th* is predicted by 41% of the Filmball community, with 11% of members predicting it to win.

However, the top two in this race remain unchanged: *Once Upon a Time in Harlem* currently sits in second place with over 78% of the vote, and *American Doctor* leads the way with nearly 90% of Filmball predicting it for the nomination and 34% predicting it for the win.

<h2>Addition of Below-the-Line Categories</h2>

For the first time this Oscar season, the Fantasy Filmball website reflects the community’s below-the-line predictions, and I want to use this next section to highlight the films the community believes are major below-the-line players.

Obviously, Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey* will be a below-the-line juggernaut. I mean, every Filmballer is predicting it for a Best Picture nomination, and over half of them are predicting it for the win, including above-the-line wins such as Best Director and Best Supporting Actress, and receiving nominations in Best Lead Actor, an additional nomination in Supporting Actress, Supporting Actor, and Adapted Screenplay.

But in the below-the-line categories, the community currently has *The Odyssey* winning seven awards and receiving three additional nominations, which would be one of, if not the biggest, sweeps in Academy Award history. The community currently has it winning Casting, Cinematography, Editing, Production Design, Costume Design, Sound, and Score, and receiving nominations for Song, Visual Effects, and Makeup and Hairstyling.

Two other popular blockbusters also have an impressive performance with the Filmball community in the below-the-line categories, with one being *Dune: Part Three*, which is currently predicted for nominations in Cinematography, Editing, Production Design, Costume Design, and Sound, and is projected to win Best Visual Effects by 74% of the Filmball community. The other, *Project Hail Mary*, is receiving nominations for Best Cinematography, Editing, Production Design, Sound, Visual Effects, and Score.

Of all the below-the-line categories, the most competitive is Best Makeup and Hairstyling, where 45% of the Filmball community have Alejandro González Iñárritu’s *Digger* coming out victorious, while 19% have Robert Eggers’ newest horror film, *Werwulf*, and 28% of Filmball has Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey* adding an additional below-the-line victory.

Meanwhile, *The Odyssey* is head and shoulders above the competition in three below-the-line categories, currently receiving over 80% of the win prediction vote in Best Cinematography, Production Design, and Costume Design.

<h2>Time to Digg or Die</h2>

The timing of this community prediction update was no mistake: it came after the social reactions to Alejandro González Iñárritu’s newest film, *Digger*, but before the review embargo and before the community at large could lay eyes on Warner Bros.’ big awards-season contender.

Currently, the Filmball community has *Digger* receiving seven Academy Award nominations, including the aforementioned victory in Makeup and Hairstyling. However, in six of those categories, *Digger* is either the last or second-to-last film based on nomination percentage.

Alejandro González Iñárritu currently has 34% of the Filmball community backing him for Best Director, which would put him in 4th place. Tom Cruise has fallen three places down the Best Actor chart, down to fourth place, with only 64% supporting the nomination and only 16% predicting him for the win, which is down nearly 40% since the last update.

The Filmball community additionally has *Digger* in the last slot in Best Original Screenplay, Cinematography, and Production Design. Filmball previously had Cruise winning Lead Actor and John Goodman at the top of Supporting Actor, and *Digger* was the runner-up in Original Screenplay in every update. Goodman is now down to just a 43% prediction ratio and 5% for the victory, currently in sixth place in the Best Supporting Actor race.

And *Digger* is down to just 49% of the Filmball community predicting it for Best Picture and 0% to win Best Picture, when previously, when we first opened up the community predictions back in April, *Digger* was number two in the Best Picture race, with 93% of the community predicting it for a nomination and 13% predicting it for the win.

<h2>Changes Since Last Update</h2>

Other small notable changes since our last update saw *Behemoth!* falling off of our Best Picture nominations in favor of *The Debut*. However, *Behemoth!* is still in the hunt, currently 12th from the community.

Off the back of his Volpi Cup win at Venice, *Wild Horse Nine*’s John Malkovich has now overtaken Tom Cruise and Matt Damon to become the only actor to receive a 100% nomination ratio from the community and is leading the way with a 55% win prediction, where he previously was just at 18%.
