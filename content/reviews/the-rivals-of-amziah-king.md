During the 2025 iteration of the South by Southwest Film Festival, the talk of the town was *The Rivals of Amziah King*. The film was first screened at the festival and received a rare standing ovation. This instantly got awards pundits talking. People said it could be nominated for a few categories, such as Best Picture. Some went as far as to say it would find itself win-competitive in editing, among other categories. There were even some who believed it could contend for the Best Picture win. The question came down to the distributor who would acquire the film.

News of its acquisition was anticipated for days. Then days became weeks, and weeks became months, and *Amziah King*was nowhere to be found. Its confusing absence during fall festivals such as Telluride and TIFF left one clear message: *Amziah King* was not a contender. That was all but confirmed when the distribution was finally announced, that being the new distribution branch of the film's production company, Black Bear. The studio's first distributed film was Sydney Sweeney's *Christy*, a record-breaking flop in most commercial aspects. The failure certainly doesn't bode well for Black Bear's future as a distributor, which is a shame considering *The Rivals of Amziah King* is a movie that simply deserved better.

Starring Matthew McConaughey and Angelina LookingGlass, the film follows McConaughey as the title character reuniting with his former foster daughter, Kateri (LookingGlass). Although the film features a wide cast, including Tony Revolori, Kurt Russell, and Owen Teague, the evident strength of the film is the relationship between the leading duo. A mostly skilled screenplay and beautiful direction, both credited to Andrew Patterson, make every moment the two have together beautiful to witness.

McConaughey's role is certainly not an easy one to pull off, but he ends up a fantastic fit for the character. Amziah's general ambivalent nature makes for so many enjoyable moments, and it's hard to think of many actors as capable of portraying it as McConaughey. The performance that seems to have resonated most with audiences is that of LookingGlass in her debut performance. The Native American actress certainly has a challenge portraying the character of Kateri, requiring a great range of emotion to be executed. Luckily, she mostly rises to the challenge, nailing most scenes. I can't say I found her flawless, though, with some scenes not quite feeling like the actress's best work. While some more takes might have been beneficial, the performance still managed to do what was needed.

When the movie was discussed for awards, a notable quantity of people had it as a potential editing winner, and it's not hard to see why. *The Rivals of Amziah King* has one of the most unique visual styles in recent cinema, largely due to its editing, incorporating freeze frames and sudden cuts to black. While there is justification for the prediction, justifying the nomination is another story. The freeze frames, in particular, feel like an afterthought, just randomly tacked in whenever the editor feels like it for the sake of doing something unique. While it's not particularly awful or even bad, it leaves quite a bit to be desired.

Fortunately, *The Rivals of Amziah King* excels in most other technical standards. M.I. Littin-Menz's cinematography is likely the strongest aspect among them, working to capture every moment of beauty as well as it does the tension within the film's later moments. The sound design is nearly as good, between the moments of happiness with music and the suspenseful moments. The two remaining standouts are the score and production design, both more subtle yet still adept works.

The biggest struggle for the film might be the issues it has with its tone and narrative. It is inherently split into two different movies across its two halves, the first being much more enjoyable and coherent than the second, where it becomes hard to enjoy without wishing you could just be watching the first. The film ends up being tonally all over the place, even within those segments, hopping from joy to grief to laughter as if the differences are nonexistent. While the first half manages to have some extremely fun moments and the second has some intensely engaging ones, it never feels like one coherent image. The story also plagues itself with some minor issues, such as Amziah King himself being a bit underdeveloped and many moments that go on just a bit too long. Everything combined just makes it hard to see the script as a positive, even though there are some great moments, leaving the script still enjoyable.

In the end, Patterson directs a great film with an interesting, albeit unsubstantial, visual style. A cast of good actors surrounds his work, coated in mostly beautiful technical work. *The Rivals of Amziah King* is not too far from a complete knockout. It's unfortunately held back by a script that never rises to the occasion. Not quite a perfect film, but still a good time that I would recommend.
