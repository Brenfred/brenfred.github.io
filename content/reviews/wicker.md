Marriage is the most well known tradition out there, with it being seen as the norm for most people in many cultures. People who get far into life without being married are sometimes seen as a minority, and it's not uncommon they get judged for it. For some people who remain unmarried, it's simply due to preference, but for others, the right circumstances just never came about. That is the exact situation explored in *Wicker*, when the local Fisherwoman, played perfectly by Olivia Colman, commissions the Basket Weaver to make her a husband made out of wicker.

Ostracization plays a major role in the themes explored throughout this film. The Fisherwoman is already treated as an outsider, not really integrated with the group as a middle aged woman who is still single and works mostly alone. The addition of her hand made husband doesn't help her status, as he's at first seen as strange and unusual. It's a set up that seems rather familiar but with an interesting enough spin.

Elizabeth Debicki as the Tailor's Wife serves as the main antagonist of the movie, the stereotypical oppressor who simply doesn't like the Fisherwoman or the fact that this new husband is bringing her extra attention. It's one of the more by the numbers aspects of the storyline, but Debicki still has enough natural charm to make it work.

The makeup work for the Wicker Husband is fantastic, giving the character a unique design that really makes him look like he's made out of wicker but also fitting right into the world the way he's meant to. Alexander Skarsgård gives an interesting performance, making the character feel not quite human but still allowing him to feel the desire the romance requires. Skarsgård and Colman play off each other effortlessly, allowing the chemistry to shine through.

The more interesting scenes in the movie come when they're being intimate. There's not only the intense passion, but also some solid humor weaved in from the husband being an unusual character. These scenes deliver on their purpose and aid in building the necessary dynamic. Outside of those scenes are when the story is a little underbaked.

Even at under two hours, the film can feel a bit too dragged out for the story at hand. Multiple moments feel like we're just reiterating the point that's already been made and is fairly straight forward. Whether the film should have been shorter or if the exploration of these themes should have dug deeper, there was something to be desired. It's not enough to ruin the experience of the film, as there's plenty of delightful elements to take in, but it does hold it back from reaching its full potential.

Overall, the movie works and is a delightful time when it's at its highs. Anyone into romance or movies with a more weird premise should get what they're looking for out of this, and the cast is top notch all around. For me, it just feels like the point is easy to see from a mile away, yet the movie keeps going.
