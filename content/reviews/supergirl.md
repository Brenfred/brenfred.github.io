“Not our monkey, not our circus.”

I’m a superhero fan at heart, and I don’t think I’ll ever shake that feeling. From when I was a young child, I was awestruck by the magic of the colorful characters zooming across my television screen, and I couldn’t help but imagine the ways in which honor, justice, and moral strength were defended by powerful, exhilarating characters. My brothers have always been more supportive of DC, but I was more of a Marvel kid myself. Nevertheless, I’ve harbored my fair share of appreciation for DC media over the years, and some of my favorite superhero movies are based on characters like Batman, Harley Quinn, and the Suicide Squad.

Needless to say, I’ve been quite invested in seeing James Gunn attempt to construct a new and improved DC Universe out of the ashes of the messy, incomprehensible nonsense that was the DCEU. I’ve not only seen Superman, but taken the time to watch *Creature Commandos* and *Peacemaker* as well, ensuring that I’m fully caught up on the new vision for this cinematic universe. Overall, I’d say I’ve enjoyed what’s been built thus far! *Superman* was a refreshing glass of hopecore with surprising relevance to our times, *Creature Commandos* was a fun adult cartoon with a killer finale, and *Peacemaker* had its moments of emotional heft despite still not quite being my sense of humor.

I came into the newest edition to James Gunn's DC Universe, *Supergirl*, confident that I’d be in for a good, fun time, with engaging characters, fun action, and enough heart to really tie it all together. Instead, when the credits started rolling, I felt as if I had finally escaped from a stupor of disinterest.

The saddest thing about *Supergirl* is that it’s not even the worst thing in the world; it’s just empty inside. There are definite pros to this film. Milly Alcock is doing the best with what she’s given, and honestly, it works reasonably well. She brings a real depth to the role that makes her feel instantly likable, even when she’s forcing out clunky dialogue, and I left feeling quite charmed by her character even more than I was with her brief cameo from prior in the DCU. Matthias Schoenaerts is also doing his best with the limited screentime afforded, actually putting together a scenery-chewing villain role that feels alive and like shards of light shining through the burlap sack of this script. David Krumholtz was a pleasant surprise and proves that no matter what bit role you grant him, he will make it work. The Krumholtz scenes are by far the best in the film, not just because of him, but because there we see some real emotional depth and effectively communicated ideas that bring us closer to the characters and to the story. We never get that close to something significant again, so I wouldn’t get used to it.

The strangest paradox of this film is that it’s simultaneously overstuffed with plot and about nothing at all. There are constant expository dumps as characters attempt to verbally communicate emotions to us, as if this convinces us of the thematic stakes being presented. Tell, don’t show, is in full force here, unfortunately, and it kills the momentum constantly. Gillespie also brings surprisingly little whimsy to this film considering his previous outings as a director. Action scenes are slapped together choppily and nigh-incoherently, and an ugly tan color grade makes everything look as unappealing as possible throughout the runtime. I think this is supposed to be funny, but I don’t recall laughing once. Every “joke” lands with a wet thud.

One of the best things about the other DCU projects is the characters, and that’s perhaps the greatest disappointment here. The past projects have been doing an excellent job building up characters with genuine arcs that we instantly grow to like, if not love. None of that is present here. Ruthye is more of a plot device than a character, Lobo is quite genuinely only here as fan service and feels horribly out of place within the narrative, and even our titular character has little growth in the film’s actual runtime. It feels like we’re watching a side quest, and the more interesting movie is just not here.

I wouldn’t say this was horrible, but it’s definitely a mixed bag. Even as I write this review, I feel the memory of it fading away from me. Alcock is such a standout that I think I won’t be tossing her performance aside, and I hope she gets to continue with this character in better stories than this one. DCU, this is strike one.
