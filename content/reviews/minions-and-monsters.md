As Allison Janney’s tour guide proudly proclaims at the start of the film, “The Minions saved cinema.” *Minions and Monsters* is a hilarious homage to the history of Hollywood and filmmaking across the ages. From the moment the movie begins, it’s clear this is more than just another Minions adventure. Even the opening Universal Pictures logo is reimagined through the ages with our beloved yellow troublemakers, immediately setting the tone for a celebration of cinema.

*Minions and Monsters* is a love letter to old Hollywood, packed with blink-and-you’ll-miss-it references to some of the most iconic films ever made. Whether it’s recreating legendary line deliveries like “Rosebud” from *Citizen Kane* in Minionese, riffing with the trademark Minions hijinks on the unforgettable set pieces from *Modern Times*, sneaking the golden statue from *Metropolis* into the background of a shot, or even interpolating on modern classics like *Babylon*, the film is overflowing with references that reward true movie lovers.

The first half of *Minions and Monsters* is the perfect balance of what makes the Minions franchise so lovable. It’s packed with jokes of all types, from slapstick to gags and, of course, Minionese. This time, we’re introduced to a brand-new tribe of Minions on a quest to find their next big boss. As we have seen play out over the course of the previous two Minions films, this quest to find their next evil overlord always ends with one of the Minions slipping up and inadvertently harming their leader, leading to the whole tribe being fired. Inside of *Minions and Monsters*, time after time, the fingers are pointed at our two new stars, one of which is the real standout of *Minions and Monsters*, James. He may not quite fit in with the rest of the tribe, but he has an eye for the arts. Whether he’s crafting scenic portraits of his Cyclops Big Boss, which then results in a Tom & Jerry-esque sequence where the Cyclops steps on the painting, made to resemble a LEGO, resulting in his doom, reading from a book of spells, resulting in an evil kaiju evaporating his wizard overlord, or dreaming of directing films (little Sammy Fabelman, am I right?), opening a whole new can of worms that threatens to take down Hollywood. James brings an entirely new dynamic to Minions lore. Then there’s Ed, who’s funny, lovable, caring, and surprisingly emotional throughout the flick. These two additions draw the viewer into the film and give us actual Minions to latch onto, which some could argue has been a weakness in previous installments. And who could forget the Minion Tribe’s rude, stuck-up, and downright mean leader, Dick? I mean… look at him. He’s Dick.

The film’s greatest strength is undoubtedly its humor. I found *Minions and Monsters* to be the funniest installment in the franchise so far, and that’s largely thanks to how many cinematic references it crams into nearly every scene. Whether it’s playing off Warner Bros. Studios with the “Bright Brothers,” the aforementioned interpolations of some of the most iconic films of all time, some of which result in Minions reciting lines in Minionese, or callbacks and plot points revolving around all the various traditional Hollywood genres, we get monsters, robots, cowboys, aliens, romance, slapstick, and so much more.

More than anything, though, the film’s biggest surprise is how unpredictable it is. The trailers sell this as another wacky Minions adventure, only this time with monsters, but that storyline doesn’t really kick into gear until about an hour into the movie. Instead, the first two-thirds act as a collection of affectionate homages to Hollywood throughout the decades. After yet another failed search, our tribe of Minions stumbles into Hollywood and accidentally becomes a mirror of their real-life counterparts: the biggest and most bankable stars on the planet. As they immerse themselves in the world of filmmaking, James’ creative spirit flourishes, inspiring him to make a motion picture of his own. Following a hilarious sequence involving the arrival of the talkies, complete with the Minions unsuccessfully attempting to recite some of the most iconic lines in movie history, the majority of the tribe heads off in search of their next boss, while James, Henry, and Ed stay behind to chase their filmmaking dreams.

Unfortunately, this is where the movie drops a notch. I have almost no complaints about the opening 45 minutes, but once the Minion gang sets off to locate monsters for their motion picture and summon Trey Parker’s Goomi, otherwise known as Gary Orkam Oliver Magma Ichabod the Deceiver, I feel as if we lose some of the magic. I love Parker’s work on *South Park* and found his villain in *Despicable Me 3* to be creative, but Goomi feels like a lot of potential that’s largely wasted. I love his exaggerated line readings and the hint of something more sinister (especially the times when the line readings come off in a very Eric Cartman way), but once the rest of the monsters are introduced, the film regresses into something much closer to the previous Minions movies. It’s obviously still fun, but it never quite reaches the creative highs of its Hollywood homage. Goomi and his monsters provide a handful of laughs, although they wear thin fairly quickly and fall into the same repetitive beats. Thankfully, that storyline is intercut with the rest of the Minion tribe’s quest to find a new big boss. After they stumble into a robot overlord named Dort, voiced by the zany Jesse Eisenberg, we’re treated to some of the film’s biggest laughs. Eventually, all of these storylines converge for a finale packed with the chaotic Minions humor we’ve all come to know and love, heavily teased in the trailer.

*Minions and Monsters* will win over so many new fans of the series. As someone who has always had a soft spot for the Minions dumb fun, I can definitely see people who have been averse to this franchise before finding themselves interested due to all of the cinematic references. If you are someone who has studied film, appreciates old cinema, or simply likes to watch fun parodies, *Minions and Monsters* might just be for you. I do believe this is the best film of the three Minions spinoffs, and it could even be the best in the larger *Despicable Me* franchise. As James boldly proclaims at the end of the film, “Minions y Monstras is Fantástico.”
