With the launch of the Fantasy Filmball website, a platform dedicated to reviewing, discussing, and forecasting films in the Oscar conversation, it felt only right for our staff to come together and celebrate some of our favorite works of the year so far. Eight Fantasy Filmball writers submitted ballots across 11 categories, voting for the films, performances, and craftspeople that have impressed them most through the first half of 2026.

[Project Hail Mary](review.html?slug=project-hail-mary) earned Fantasy Filmball's Midseason Best Picture award by winning over our staff for entirely different reasons. Some were swept up by its crowd-pleasing spectacle, others by its technical craftsmanship, and others by the warmth and humanity that Phil Lord and Chris Miller bring to the material.

<blockquote>
<p>“Project Hail Mary has everything. Not only is it an immensely entertaining blockbuster with a career defining performance from Hollywood’s most charming movie star, it’s also a thought provoking piece of science fiction that demands that we face the end of the world with a smile. It’s the type of movie we need in these dark times, a movie that keeps its chin up and says ‘we can make it through this — we always do.’”</p>
<p style="font-style:normal;font-family:'JetBrains Mono',ui-monospace,monospace;font-size:0.82rem;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:var(--ink-faded);text-align:right;margin-top:0.65rem;">— Matthew Downs</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote>
<p>“Project Hail Mary has been the most enjoyable time in the theater so far this year. It has great humor, a wide array of impressive technical elements, and characters it’s hard not to fall in love with.”</p>
<p style="font-style:normal;font-family:'JetBrains Mono',ui-monospace,monospace;font-size:0.82rem;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:var(--ink-faded);text-align:right;margin-top:0.65rem;">— Andy Funke</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote>
<p>“Lord &amp; Miller have always been great at crafting fun cinematic experiences, and they elevate their game here with added emotional heft. Gosling turns in a dynamite movie star performance, and Rocky is one of the most iconic new characters in years!”</p>

<h2>Best Director — Phil Lord &amp; Christopher Miller, Project Hail Mary</h2>

<figure class="award-winner-card" style="margin:2.5rem auto;max-width:820px">
<img src="/posters/midseason-2026/best-director.png" alt="Fantasy Filmball Midseason Awards — Best Director: Phil Lord &amp; Christopher Miller, Project Hail Mary" loading="lazy">
</figure>

<blockquote>
<p>“Back in 2017, directing duo Phil Lord and Chris Miller were fired from Solo: A Star Wars Story, as the film’s star complained to Lucasfilm that the movie they were making was perhaps too silly for Star Wars. In 2026, we finally see what Lord &amp; Miller would have done with the material, as they release their epic adaptation of Andy Weir’s novel Project Hail Mary, which is heart-pounding, emotional, and yes, extremely silly. The pop auteurs behind The LEGO Movie, 21 Jump Street, and the Spider-Verse franchise can be accused of many things, but never of being too serious. What they bring to Project Hail Mary is a sense of whimsy, and an improvisational lightness that allows the film to float above other films like it. More than Interstellar, Arrival, or The Martian, Project Hail Mary is directed with a feeling of love for the human race. Lord &amp; Miller understand that for us to truly care about the outcome of the apocalyptic situation in their film, we must see everything that makes humanity beautiful on screen. Our kindness, our creativity, and our humor.”</p>
<p style="font-style:normal;font-family:'JetBrains Mono',ui-monospace,monospace;font-size:0.82rem;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:var(--ink-faded);text-align:right;margin-top:0.65rem;">— Matthew Downs</p>
</blockquote>

<h2>Best Leading Performance — Emily Blunt, Disclosure Day</h2>

<figure class="award-winner-card" style="margin:2.5rem auto;max-width:820px">
<img src="/posters/midseason-2026/best-lead.png" alt="Fantasy Filmball Midseason Awards — Best Leading Performance: Emily Blunt, Disclosure Day" loading="lazy">
</figure>

<blockquote>
<p>“Emily Blunt serves as a human conduit for Alien’s observation of humankind in Disclosure Day, playing to both the physically familiar and the psychologically extraterrestrial. What is so enthralling about her performance is not simply the many dialects she speaks nor the abducted physicality she brings to the screen, but, moreover, the sense of sublime empathy she delivers with a certain humility that helps affirm her place as a human conduit, not a messiah. Empathy, and its display, is regarded as humanity’s greatest quality and, in turn, the key to unlocking Disclosure Day. Without her characterization of these qualities, Disclosure Day lacks direct emotional weight. She plays an intermediary figurehead for both Alien understanding and Human disclosure, with a degree of earnestness that helps wash away saccharine feelings that could easily be overbearing if played incorrectly.”</p>
<p style="font-style:normal;font-family:'JetBrains Mono',ui-monospace,monospace;font-size:0.82rem;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:var(--ink-faded);text-align:right;margin-top:0.65rem;">— Terence Peterson</p>
</blockquote>

<h2>Best Supporting Performance — Chi Lewis-Parry, 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple</h2>

<figure class="award-winner-card" style="margin:2.5rem auto;max-width:820px">
<img src="/posters/midseason-2026/best-supporting.png" alt="Fantasy Filmball Midseason Awards — Best Supporting Performance: Chi Lewis-Parry, 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple" loading="lazy">
</figure>

<blockquote>
<p>“It’s hard to clearly define what ‘good’ acting is. Sometimes it’s more of a feeling than a concrete, objective measure. Furthermore, not all performances are created equal, and Chi Lewis-Parry is perfect evidence of that. This is not a performance with scenes of yelling, crying, and dramatics that would make the viewer instantly think that this person should be up for awards. It’s an intimidating, physically directed performance, where Lewis-Parry utilizes his stature to tower over others, despite seeming so empathetic and genuine deep within him. When you look into his eyes, you don’t see a monster, you see a human; without that delivery, the whole theme of 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple would collapse. Watching this character lumber across the screen makes your heart grow a size, and you truly do believe the journey he goes on in this work. This would not be possible without the soulful, painful, beautiful performance of Lewis-Parry.”</p>
<p style="font-style:normal;font-family:'JetBrains Mono',ui-monospace,monospace;font-size:0.82rem;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:var(--ink-faded);text-align:right;margin-top:0.65rem;">— Ray Siddiqi</p>
</blockquote>

<h2>Best Screenplay — Kristoffer Borgli, The Drama</h2>

<figure class="award-winner-card" style="margin:2.5rem auto;max-width:820px">
<img src="/posters/midseason-2026/best-screenplay.png" alt="Fantasy Filmball Midseason Awards — Best Screenplay: Kristoffer Borgli, The Drama" loading="lazy">
</figure>

<blockquote>
<p>“My vote for our Screenplay prize has to go to Kristoffer Borgli’s for his incredibly provoking script for The Drama. One of the reasons why this movie works so well is the story that is crafted on the page. From that initial gripping premise and the utterly dynamic aspects on display during that ‘What is the worst thing you have done?’ discussion around the dinner table, the following events that play out during The Drama feel so expertly crafted. From the pitch-perfect timing to some of the absurdist directions these characters find themselves in, Borgli’s script allows the entire ensemble to have their moments to shine, as well as start a conversation not just inside of the cinema, but with your friends and family when you leave. To me, that is truly the mark of a great screenplay.”</p>

<h2>Best Cinematography — Greig Fraser, Project Hail Mary</h2>

<figure class="award-winner-card" style="margin:2.5rem auto;max-width:820px">
<img src="/posters/midseason-2026/best-cinematography.png" alt="Fantasy Filmball Midseason Awards — Best Cinematography: Greig Fraser, Project Hail Mary" loading="lazy">
</figure>

<blockquote>
<p>“Greig Fraser has had a hell of a decade. From Dune, to The Batman, now to Project Hail Mary, this Australian cinematographer has basically become Hollywood’s go-to guy when it comes to putting sci-fi worlds on screen. But Project Hail Mary’s cinematography feels quite unlike his other sci-fi works. While his other recent works tend to have a massive scale, being shot in beautiful locations that do a lot of heavy lifting for the visuals, Project Hail Mary primarily takes place inside of a tiny metal box floating in space. It is Fraser’s job to find interesting ways to capture the claustrophobic and solitary interior of a tin can, and he does it beautifully, playing with the small blinking lights of the ship command modules, and letting light slip in through the windows of the ship. There are also those big moments in the film, like the infrared images of fishing in outer space. But a film is so much more than its biggest set pieces, and Fraser is on his A-game for every single scene. He can make a board room or a laboratory look as visually compelling as a ship floating through a nebula.”</p>
<p style="font-style:normal;font-family:'JetBrains Mono',ui-monospace,monospace;font-size:0.82rem;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:var(--ink-faded);text-align:right;margin-top:0.65rem;">— Matthew Downs</p>
