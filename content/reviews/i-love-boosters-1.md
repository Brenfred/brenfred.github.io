Approximately four months ago, I was seated in one of the pristine leather recliners of the famed AMC Georgetown 14 to watch *How to Make a Killing* (I think). Little did I know, my life was about to change. Before I was treated to some Glen Powell goodness, I was met with the teaser trailer for Boots Riley’s latest feature film, *I Love Boosters*. This trailer may truly be the best 86 seconds that humanity has ever created, and the film immediately shot up from the far reaches of my 2026 watchlist to the undisputed #1 slot. On May 22nd, I made sure to have a prime seat in my local AMC, and I can officially say that… the trailer might be better than the movie itself. 

I don’t want this to sound too harsh, because *I Love Boosters* is still a strange and often hilarious ride, with more life than most modern studio comedies could ever dream of. At its best, director Boots Riley has crafted a controlled chaos that makes the trailer so exciting. Keke Palmer is the glue for Riley’s vision, with her role as Corvette giving the film a center of gravity, around which the rest of our characters orbit. Palmer is charismatic throughout and seems fully tapped into whatever frequency Boots operates on. Corvette is given a more grounded angle, even in some of the later, sci-fi-oriented scenes. 

The supporting cast is a bit more uneven. We’re treated to solid but unremarkable performances from Naomi Ackie, Taylour Paige, and Poppy Liu, who round out the team of boosters.  Demi Moore and LaKeith Stanfield are standouts, with Moore’s artificial nature perfectly matching the film’s critique of fast fashion, and Stanfield offers arguably the most creative subplot of 2026 (which I won’t spoil here!). The crafts are also extremely consistent, with almost every scene offering different (AND COLORFUL) outfits, hair and makeup design, and distinct sets, which include an apartment building that sits at a 45-degree angle. Riley truly did not cut any corners here, and I’d assume much of the $20 million budget, the largest ever for a Neon-produced film, went to making the film look as cartoonish as possible, in the absolute best way. 

*I Love Booster* begins to falter, however, in its inability to bridge the gap between its political intentions and the actual dramatic execution. Riley has never been one to hide his politics, nor should he have to, and *I Love Booster* is, at its core, a takedown of the exploitation of labor due to our capitalist society. However, the film too often slides into simplistic, almost “Disney XD-esque” scenes in order to get its point across. This is most notable in the film’s ending, where one character’s cancer is healed through the power of workers unionizing and going on strike. This is a bit of an exaggeration, but, truly, only a BIT of an exaggeration. For a movie that promises so much “weirdness,” its ending just feels too milk toast. This is even more frustrating when looking at the film as a whole because, for so much of its runtime, *I Love Boosters* works BECAUSE it trusts its own weirdness, rather than in spite of it, but, by the end, Riley almost seems worried that the viewer might miss his point and loses any sense of subtlety. 

Still, even though the film fell below my expectations, I had a good time. *I Love Boosters* is nothing if not messy, but I wasn’t bored for a second. The film is overstuffed and blunt in its politics, but it's never lazy. There’s something inspiring about a movie that is willing to be so goofy, but I just wish Boots Riley had managed to craft a tighter final product. So yes, the trailer might have been better than the movie, but the movie is still worth seeing, especially for fans of Riley’s other works or just anyone interested in the off-the-wall comedy. *I Love Boosters* promises just enough charm to fill theaters and, if his recent Twitter/X presence tells us anything, Boots himself really wants YOU to buy a ticket.
