In a galaxy far, far away comes a film saga spanning 12 feature films, with the latest edition being *The Mandalorian and Grogu*, which hit cinemas worldwide this weekend, and I have to say, the *Star Wars* franchise has been a massive hit with the Academy Awards. Obviously, the original entry, *Star Wars: A New Hope*, racked up not just 10 nominations, not just a Best Picture slot in a year of five, but also a total of seven wins on the night. And with the release of *The Mandalorian and Grogu*, I want to dive a little deeper into the *Star Wars* saga’s constant presence at the Academy Awards. All previous 11 *Star Wars* films have made a dent at the Academy. Some may be as small as a single nomination, some are as large as *A New Hope* getting seven wins, 10 nominations, and branching a new path for sci-fi spectacles to succeed on movies’ biggest night.

While, obviously, we have the reactions for *The Mandalorian and Grogu*, and this is not going to be the Best Picture film. It’s not gonna reach anything above the line, and it’s not going to be an across-the-board tech and craft contender. However, with the previous 11 entries, they have shown a very consistent hit rate in three key categories. Those being Best Visual Effects, Best Sound, and Best Original Score. Between the 11 previous entries, 10 have gotten a nomination in Best Visual Effects. The only film to miss the nomination was *Star Wars: Revenge of the Sith*. *Revenge of the Sith* is by far the *Star Wars* saga’s worst-performing film at the Academy, only securing a single nomination for Best Makeup and Hairstyling.

The original trilogy of *A New Hope*, *The Empire Strikes Back*, and *Return of the Jedi* all won the Best Visual Effects category, with a minor asterisk. While *A New Hope* did win this category outright, the Academy handed out a Special Achievement Award to both *Empire Strikes Back* and *Return of the Jedi* for visual effects. The Academy handed out these Special Achievement Awards in special. However, *The Phantom Menace*, *Attack of the Clones*, *The Force Awakens*, *Rogue One*, *The Last Jedi*, *Solo*, and *The Rise of Skywalker* all have gotten nominated in the Best Visual Effects category. That is a 90.9% hit rate.

Just purely looking at the stats, *The Mandalorian and Grogu* should be in your Best Visual Effects lineup. I understand if someone says this film is going to underperform at the box office, it is not a critical success, and it’s not a part of the main canon, but you can point to films like *Solo*, which fit all of the above and still got nominated in a year of five.

You could theorize that in a guaranteed lineup of five films, *Revenge of the Sith* would have gotten the Best Visual Effects nomination. Back in 2005, the Academy only nominated three films in Visual Effects. *King Kong* by Peter Jackson famously won the prize that year, but its fellow nominees were *The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe* and the Tom Cruise *War of the Worlds*.

Now that is a pretty stacked category. *King Kong* being the technical marvel it was, tying for the most wins on the night, and being a breakthrough for photorealistic animal visual effects. *Narnia* just being a major craft player, with the addition of the visual effects for the animals. There was, of course, the world-building and, of course, the action set pieces. And then *War of the Worlds*, you watch any frame from the second half of that film and you fully understand why it got the Visual Effects nomination.

I would argue if there were five slots, *Revenge of the Sith* would have found its way into the nomination lineup, but you could argue it would not have since it was a lone nominee just for Makeup and Hairstyling, not even in the sound category.

That moves this conversation around *The Mandalorian and Grogu’s* chances at the Academy Awards in 2027 over to Best Sound because, from the 11 theatrically released *Star Wars* films, eight of them have gotten this nomination. Eight *Star Wars* movies have gotten a nomination in a Sound category, with two of them winning the award: *A New Hope* and *The Empire Strikes Back*.

That is an amazing 72.7% hit rate. Now, this is a category where you could argue maybe *The Mandalorian and Grogu* does not need to show up in 2027. Some examples of *Star Wars* films missing out in the sound categories would be *Attack of the Clones*, *Revenge of the Sith*, and *Solo*. You could easily argue these are the three worst critically and audience-received *Star Wars* films so far, so it makes sense why they would miss out on these nominations.

However, you can point to a film like *The Rise of Skywalker* or even *The Last Jedi*, which were super divisive, and they still managed to showed up in their respective years.

I think more recently, with the bake-offs and the reels they get to showcase, the sound category has gotten a little cooler with what they nominate, but they still do love their blasters, they still do love their spaceships, they still do love their mindless action movies.

Just going to the past few years, we go to 2023 where both *Mission: Impossible – Dead Reckoning* and *The Creator* showed up in the Best Sound category. *The Creator* is essentially a *Star Wars* movie. It has robot noises, it has spaceships, it has blasters, it has explosions. *Mission: Impossible* checks off the boxes of an action spectacle, which *The Mandalorian and Grogu* is.

Other recent examples since the sound category shrunk from being split by Mixing and Editing to just Best Sound that I think would overlap with *The Mandalorian and Grogu* would be from 2020 when we saw the likes of *News of the World* and *Greyhound* get this nomination behind their various vehicular audio, as well as guns and sounds of action. Then 2021 with *No Time to Die*, which was not an above-the-line player, but showed up in its necessary and essential below-the-line categories.

Now, if one was going to be critical of this potential nomination for *The Mandalorian and Grogu*, I think they could point to last year as a prime example. As I said above, the sound branch is becoming more unique, more interesting, and more receptive to different types of films and their sound work. I really do think the bake-offs and the reels they get to showcase play a major role in this.

We saw no Marvel film make the shortlist last year. We have yet to see a superhero film get this nomination since 2022, and last year we even got the likes of films like *Sirat* behind its rave EDM music-heavy effects, and *Frankenstein* behind its dark-age and manor craftwork.

While the *Star Wars* films typically show up in Best Sound, I think a lot of the negative energy associated with *The Mandalorian and Grogu*, whether that is its potential box office returns, its current critic scores, or the ongoing growth of the sound branch could cause it to join the likes of the three *Star Wars* films to miss out on this nomination and continue to drop the average score in the Best Sound category.

And lastly, we must talk about Best Original Score. Between the 11 *Star Wars* films to come out, six of them have received a Best Original Score nomination. That is a 54.5% hit rate. On paper, that is more than half. You would say, “Hey, this should be taken seriously,” especially when you add the fact that Ludwig Göransson, three-time Academy Award winner, the man behind the original scores of *Black Panther*, *Oppenheimer*, and *Sinners*, composed the score for *The Mandalorian and Grogu*.

It just makes sense. However, when I tell you of the 11 films released by the *Star Wars* saga, the six that received a nomination were all done by the man, the myth, the legend John Williams.

The five films not to receive a nomination, some included Williams, some didn’t, but they all did not get the nomination. Williams got nominations for the original trilogy and the new trilogy, Episodes I, II, and III, and more importantly, *Rogue One* and *Solo* did not get nominated for Best Original Score.

You also throw in the detail that Ludwig is scoring Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey* later this year, and one can easily come to the conclusion that maybe Ludwig can score an additional shortlist nomination for *The Mandalorian and Grogu*. After all, 15 films do pop up on that list. But if Ludwig is going to get in this year, it’s not going to be for the *Star Wars* film. It is going to be for Christopher Nolan’s epic retelling of Homer’s *Odyssey*.

All in all, I wanted to write this quick article to really discuss *The Mandalorian and Grogu’s* Oscar potential because I feel like it’s something people aren’t taking very seriously. Whether it’s the community’s distaste for the *Star Wars* saga at this moment, the hesitation on a film being adapted from a TV show, or whatever reason it may be, I don’t think one should completely write this movie off for Oscar prospects.

As I have just laid out here, *Star Wars* more often than not gets nominated in three key categories, and if you have seen *The Mandalorian and Grogu*, essentially the first 15 minutes of the movie are your showcase reel, whether that is for the visual effects department, the sound branch, or the score community.

All three of these technical crafts are showcased to their fullest form right out of the gate. One may have reservations about the movie at large, but one simply cannot deny its possibility to add to the *Star Wars* saga’s legacy with the Academy Awards.
