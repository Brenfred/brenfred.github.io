Most of the lineup for this year’s Toronto International Film Festival is live, including Cannes darlings, buzzy world premieres, hidden gems that may take everyone by surprise, and everything in between. It’s one of the biggest Oscar precursors, with the TIFF People’s Choice Award winner going on to receive a Best Picture nomination in 13 of the last 14 years, 2024’s *The Life of Chuck* being the unfortunate exception. Importantly, it’s where many films’ awards campaigns get started, and where others are declared dead on arrival. Let’s take a look at which films are most likely to kick-start their awards campaigns this September and become eventual Oscar players in the new year.

To kick us off, four previous Best Picture-winning directors will premiere films at TIFF this year. Siân Heder’s follow-up to *CODA*, *Being Heumann*, will open the festival, which may give it some extra buzz. That said, opening-night films have a poor track record of becoming major awards players; *The Fifth Estate*, *The Judge*, *Demolition*, and *Outlaw King* all had buzz going into the festival before largely getting shut out of awards consideration altogether (a Robert Duvall Supporting Actor nomination for *The Judge* notwithstanding). *Being Heumann* stars Ruth Madeley as disability rights activist Judith Heumann, as well as Mark Ruffalo as her political adversary Joseph Califano.

Peter Farrelly’s *I Play Rocky* looks like a potential crowd-pleaser, and I’m sure much will be made of Anthony Ippolito’s performance as Sylvester Stallone. It seems like the kind of movie that could become an audience favourite and compete for the People’s Choice Award, but the same was said about *Rental Family* last year and we all know how that turned out. After *Green Book*’s somewhat divisive Best Picture win, it will be interesting to see how willing the Academy is to embrace another Farrelly film. His last TIFF premiere, *The Greatest Beer Run Ever*, was widely panned and failed to mount a serious awards campaign; Farrelly will hope *I Play Rocky* doesn’t suffer the same fate.

*Ink*, directed by Danny Boyle, follows a young Rupert Murdoch (played by Guy Pearce) and rogue editor Larry Lamb (Jack O’Connell) as they transform *The Sun* into the biggest tabloid in the U.K. Based on its premise, I could see it resonating with BAFTA, though I’m not sure how big of a hit it will be in Toronto.

Lastly, John Madden, who directed *Shakespeare in Love*, has a new film called *Onwards and Sideways*, about a school teacher (Laura Linney) and a composer (Rhys Ifans) who meet after both being diagnosed with Parkinson’s on the same day. It could be a tearjerker, which could play well with the TIFF crowd, as *Hamnet* did last year, but given that none of Madden’s films have been serious Oscar contenders since his Best Picture win in 1999, this isn’t one I’m watching particularly closely.

Will we see some other acclaimed filmmakers who’ve flirted with the Academy have a surprise hit at the festival? Florian Zeller, director of 2020’s *The Father*, has a new film called *Bunker* that’s gotten some early buzz. His last film, *The Son*, which also went to TIFF, received mixed reviews and failed to gain awards traction, so he’ll be looking for a comeback here.

Mike Leigh, whose films have received a combined 18 Oscar nominations, will premiere *Tender Loving Care* at the festival. Plot details remain under wraps, but you can never count Leigh out when it comes to earning a sixth Original Screenplay nomination.

Jesse Eisenberg, who was himself nominated for *A Real Pain*'s screenplay, will see his latest film, *The Debut*, have its world premiere at TIFF. Starring Julianne Moore, *The Debut* follows a woman who is unexpectedly cast in the lead role of a local musical by a theater director played by Paul Giamatti. Our community has the film ranked 10th in Best Picture, has Moore as their Best Actress winner heading into the month of August, Giamatti as the runner-up in Best Supporting Actor, and sixth in Best Original Screenplay. We'll have to wait and see whether it can meet, or even surpass, those lofty expectations.

While many Oscar prognosticators understandably have their International Feature predictions stacked with Cannes films, a few unseen contenders premiering at TIFF could easily challenge for a spot in the lineup. Hirokazu Kore-eda’s *Sheep in the Box* may have received mixed reviews this spring, but the director has a second film coming out in 2026 titled *Look Back*, adapted from the manga of the same name. The film I have more confidence in, however, is *Possible Love*, Lee Chang-dong’s follow-up to his acclaimed psychological thriller *Burning*. It will be distributed by Netflix, which has earned four International Feature nominations over the past five years.

Other films worth watching include Chris Rock’s *Misty Green*, which stars Daniel Kaluuya and Adam Driver alongside Rosalind Eleazar in the title role. Two-time Oscar winner Mahershala Ali stars as a hitman in *Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother*, while Chase Infiniti leads the coming-of-age drama *The Julia Set*, the actress’ follow-up to her breakout role in *One Battle After Another*.

Acclaimed director Andrew Haigh will premiere *A Long Winter* at TIFF, hoping to build on the critical success of *All of Us Strangers* with an Academy Award nomination; he did previously direct Charlotte Rampling to a Best Actress nomination for *45 Years*. The courtroom drama *Prima Facie*, starring Cynthia Erivo, has emerged as a potential Best Actress contender due to Jodie Comer winning a Tony Award for playing the lead role on Broadway. Finally, Tim Blake Nelson’s *The Life and Deaths of Wilson Shedd*, a prison thriller starring Amanda Seyfried, has been generating strong pre-festival buzz.

Beyond the unseen Oscar hopefuls premiering at TIFF, many strong Oscar contenders that have already premiered at other festivals will also be making an appearance. That includes Cannes’ Palme d’Or winner *Fjord* and Best Director winner *La Bola Negra* (7th and 2nd in our community’s Best Picture Oscar predictions), as well as *Club Kid* (13th) and *All of a Sudden* (12th). Since both *La Bola Negra* and *Club Kid* were audience favorites at Cannes, I wouldn’t be surprised to see either place on the TIFF People’s Choice Award podium. Other Cannes films screening at TIFF include *Minotaur* and *The Beloved*.

Lastly, out of Sundance, *Josephine*, which won both the Grand Jury Prize and the Audience Award, will play at TIFF, as will *The Only Living Pickpocket in New York*, which has generated plenty of buzz for John Turturro’s lead performance. Both films will look to build on the goodwill they earned in Utah, though I don’t see either becoming a major Oscar player. Turturro, who ranks 9th in our community’s Best Actor predictions, will hope his film picks up steam in Toronto to strengthen his own campaign.

As an attendee at TIFF this year, the films I end up seeing will largely depend on the schedule and ticket availability during my time in Toronto, but there are already a few I’m dying to catch. *Possible Love* and *Tender Loving Care* are near the top of my list as a fan of both directors, even if they’re not the biggest Oscar players across the board. I’m also very excited for *Fjord*, as someone who loved Mungiu’s previous Palme d’Or-winning film. Whether or not *The Debut* surpasses *A Real Pain*’s nominations haul, I have high hopes for it, and would love to see Giamatti pick up a gold statuette at some point in his career. Lastly, for something of a rogue shout, I’m looking forward to *Alpha Gang*. I’ve heard the Zellner brothers’ films described as being transgressive and original, and in a festival where I’ll mostly be watching serious “awards” films, unwinding with a sci-fi comedy starring Channing Tatum and Cate Blanchett seems like a good ol’ time.
