As such, like many on the team here, I enjoy combining these two worlds when I can, focusing on analyzing historical trends to both contextualize and add insight to my current predictions. Essentially, I don't use numbers as my sole metric for predictions, but as one tool in a vast toolkit. As the season progresses and we have some actual precursors, I'll be writing pieces on various analyses and models I've been toying around with, but today I'd like to begin with an open-ended brain dump on *Obsession*'s Best Picture path.

*Obsession* is clearly a unique case. Releasing the calendar year after a festival premiere is not generally a route to awards success, but with a 4.1 on Letterboxd and a 77 on Metacritic, *Obsession* has the metrics to pair with its impressive $375 million worldwide gross (as of July 2).

To put you all in my headspace, I've currently got *Obsession* at #7 in my lineup, in my 3rd tier of films. It's not locked for a nom, but I'd say its odds are close to 50%, which is quite strong for this time of year. I like to do my Best Picture predictions by looking at possibilities for precursor results, or what I refer to as "top down" predicting. This is in contrast to "bottom up," which uses lower categories to build "packages" for films, which inform best picture chances.

The most likely precursor package that I see for *Obsession* includes nominations at the Producers Guild of America (PGA) and Critics' Choice Awards (CCA), with a chance for an appearance at the Golden Globes (GG) but not much more. The case for PGA is clear: *Obsession* currently ranks 2nd in domestic box office totals among all post-COVID original films and feels like a cultural moment in general. *Sinners* and *F1* rank 1st and 3rd on the same list, respectively, and both films were included in the PGA 10 last season. CCA is a bit more of a projection, but more often than not, CCA tends to favor large American films that are currently placed in the awards race, as shown by the inclusion of *Wicked: For Good* and *Jay Kelly* last season. Also, CCA shows less aversion to horror films than more traditional awards bodies, as shown by them giving *Weapons* four nominations and its first major win.

As of now, considering a world in which *Obsession* finds its way into the Screen Actors Guild (SAG) lineup feels unproductive, and I'd say there's no chance for any appearance at the Directors Guild Awards (DGA) or the British Academy Film Awards (BAFTA), with even the BAFTA longlist seeming unlikely. With all of that being said, let's jump right in.

This is the most "open-ended" of the three paths we'll look at today, as it only sets four of these six precursors as known. This is a baseline case for *Obsession*, given my current predictions for PGA and CCA, and it offers an optimistic outlook for its Best Picture chances. Since 2021, 11 of the 13 films with this precursor profile have made Best Picture, including *CODA* and *Train Dreams*, with the two misses being *Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery* and *Tick, Tick... Boom!* Notably, *Obsession* matches the review profile quite well here, with films in this category that eventually made Best Picture averaging 3.82 on Letterboxd and 77.9 on Metacritic, compared to *Obsession*'s 4.1 and 77, respectively.

Interestingly, of these 13 films, only four missed the BAFTA longlist, with *Obsession* definitely at risk, given that BAFTA could be biased against it in several ways. Namely, BAFTA tends to be lower on films that are horror or genre-skewing in general, think *Weapons* blanking entirely, or those that tend to be Gen Z-oriented, like *Barbie*. The BAFTAs definitely feel behind the Academy Awards in a way that would make them lower on *Obsession* than their American counterpart. When looking at the group that did miss the longlist, which consists of *American Fiction*, *Avatar: The Way of Water*, *Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery*, and *Train Dreams*, we see that three of the four still made it into the final Best Picture lineup. From this, even with a small sample size, we can conclude that missing the BAFTA longlist is not the end of the world for films with the PGA + CCA combo.

Moving forward, let's be a bit more specific and look at the sole film that made PGA and CCA but missed out on DGA, BAFTA LL, SAG, and GG. Now, this has occurred only one time in the modern era with *Train Dreams*, an interesting comparison for *Obsession*, to say the least. With an 88 on Metacritic, *Train Dreams* benefited from a much stronger critical reception than *Obsession*'s 77. Even though the two films have similar Letterboxd scores, *Obsession* is clearly in a different universe in terms of audience reception. Importantly, *Train Dreams* missed out on the GG Drama nomination in a very crowded category that included *Sinners*, *Sentimental Value*, *Hamnet*, *Frankenstein*, *The Secret Agent*, and *It Was Just An Accident*. In contrast, *Obsession* would be going up against films like *The Invite*, *Club Kid*, *The Adventures of Cliff Booth*, and *The Drama*, so a miss here might show relatively more weakness than it did for *Train Dreams*, in context.

As such, it's difficult to see where *Obsession* might land with the Academy based on this precursor profile, and I think it would ultimately come down to the strength of the other 9/10-slot contenders. The conclusion here is that while I don't think *Obsession* would be dead by any means with only the PGA + CCA combo, I'd definitely like to see some other signs of strength, like some solid nominations or a win for Inde Navarrette in either acting category.

Now, let's look at the scenario that I'm currently predicting, for now. This would include nominations at PGA, CCA, and the Golden Globes, but not DGA, SAG, or BAFTA LL. Similar to the last scenario, this is a slightly rare precursor path, with only two films since 2021 qualifying: *Avatar: The Way of Water* and *Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery*. Now, I'm not sure that *Obsession* maps too well onto either of these films, given that the *Avatar* franchise thrives on its ever-evolving technology and premier VFX, while *Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery* was a true Netflix #2 that never felt like it was actually going to get into Best Picture. Timeline-wise, this path is reminiscent of *Weapons* from last year, starting with support from non-industry bodies and ending with a PGA nomination, although I definitely have more confidence that *Obsession* will get over the finish line. Notably, the extra CCA nomination over *Weapons'* haul might be exactly what *Obsession* needs to maintain momentum throughout the entire season.

Since the expansion to a fixed 10-film Best Picture lineup, we've repeatedly seen how important #1 passion is for a film to sneak into the back end of a lineup. Whether it was *Drive My Car*, *Triangle of Sadness*, *I'm Still Here*, or even *F1*, *Obsession* is now demonstrating a similar level of passion. At this point, it's just a question of whether or not this passion will translate to the Academy.
