Before the one and only screening of Zack Snyder's *The Last Photograph* at the Toronto International Film Festival, Snyder stated that he could burn the film following its screening, leaving TIFF as the only audience to see the film. If the vast majority of the audience at TIFF had their way, that wouldn't be a joke. If I had my way, the movie would never exist to begin with.

*The Last Photograph* is clearly intended to be Snyder's passion project, which he has been working on for over 2 decades. The film follows professional Hugh Jackman lookalike Stuart Martin as an ex-DEA operative searching for his family in South America. Starring alongside him is professional Kit Harington lookalike Fra Fee as an ex-war photographer. I note the similarities in their looks because it is abundantly clear they couldn't afford or convince any more prolific actors to join this film. No disrespect meant to the actors, but their performances are certainly not it, from the lead duo or the rest of the cast. They range from passable to atrocious within each scene, and nobody feels like they give an ounce of effort with their material. One can't be blamed for a lack of effort, though, especially with this material.

The script is truly awful. Things happen incoherently just to get to the next mediocre action set piece with far too much... "crowdpleasing" if you catch my drift. I could go on and on about how insanely problematic this script is. It successfully has many derogatory portrayals of women and indigenous people, among many, many other topics. However, there are many ways to critique the script without getting a portion of the fanbase to complain about wokeness. The dialogue is awful, the pacing is nonexistent, the plot structure is infuriating, and for a bizarro fusion of *Civil War* and *The Accountant 2*, the plot is just boring.

Zack Snyder's direction of the film is not quite as horrible as the screenplay or acting, but the bar being so low makes it even funnier when it causes Snyder to trip. The way Snyder layers out scenes in the film is frankly unremarkable. The visual style is hilariously misfitting the film Snyder is making. About the only compliment I can give this is that some of his shots aren't just gray trees.

Credit where credit is due: for all the world of this film having 0 artistic merit, it is far from the worst in a technical aspect. Yes, the editing and costume design and makeup and casting and visual effects and the choreography are all mediocre at best, but there's still some decent stuff. The production design is not entirely unremarkable, some of it being fun and good-looking. The sound design is intense and frankly really well done. The cinematography is at many points gorgeous, and is really good work despite being a bit of a weird fit. The score is oft unremarkable, but one action scene has a near-perfect track, likely a result of Hans Zimmer, whose legacy should go unstained from participating in this train wreck.

Zack Snyder is one of, if not the most peculiar, filmmakers alive. An infamously rabid and defensive fanbase, director's cuts that will add an hour and make a whole different film even when the original has near-complete directorial control (Not always obviously), and yet he doesn't seem like a bad guy. Even at the premiere, you do get the sense this is someone who loves their job and this industry deeply, yet it seems whatever brought him onboard this career has left him long ago.

Not so many years ago, a passionate and not yet so rabid fanbase managed to garner enough passion for a release of Zack Snyder's rumored cut of *Justice League*. But not everything is meant to be released. Some things sit in a basement and rot away, for better or worse. All this to say that Snyder's passion project may be destined to die, and I hate to say it, but it would be for the better. What a waste of 25 years.
