*Meet the Parents* came out in 2000, and 26 years later, we get the fourth entry in the franchise with *Focker-In-Law*. A lot of time has passed since the first film, yet this one might as well have been made back then, given its tired early-2000s-style humor. Many of the gags are ones you've seen countless times before, whether it's people getting hurt in ridiculous ways or parents and children talking about inappropriate topics that embarrass their now-grown children. Some attempts land here and there, particularly a comedic scene featuring a John Legend song, but they are sparse. While much of that falls flat, there's still enough to enjoy about *Focker-in-Law*.

The character dynamics are where the movie remains somewhat fun. Ben Stiller’s Greg Focker gets off on the wrong foot with his son’s new girlfriend, Olivia, played by Ariana Grande-Butera, without even knowing it's her when they happen to be on the same flight. They spend the whole movie trying to make up for their less-than-fun first interaction. Greg has a lot of hesitancy when he learns his son, Henry, is planning to propose. Meanwhile, Henry, played by Skyler Gisondo, is scared of doing things that might upset his dad, which gets in the way of him always being honest with Olivia about what he has and hasn't told him. There are plenty of stakes in these character dynamics to keep things interesting as they navigate these complicated relationships.

Ariana Grande-Butera is the main saving grace of this movie. Olivia brings some of the most entertaining moments, partly due to her background as a former FBI hostage negotiator. A scene in a store where she and Greg are buying balloons, and she gets to put her negotiation skills to the test, is a real highlight. She works great opposite Stiller in all of their scenes together, making them an unexpectedly enjoyable duo, even if she often outshines him. This developing relationship that Greg is averse to is the true driving force of the film, and it's always at its best when these two share the screen.

Skyler Gisondo is also a welcome addition to the franchise, working well opposite both Stiller as his son and Grande-Butera as her boyfriend. Gisondo always has a natural screen presence that allows him to fit right in. Robert De Niro also has his moments, still showcasing his natural charm.

There are other characters that don't work as well. Greg's daughter, Samantha, and her partner, Eduardo, played by Beanie Feldstein and Eduardo Franco, respectively, never feel like they quite fit in or are entirely necessary. Their comedic moments typically don't land, although they have a nice payoff for Franco's character arc. Teri Polo's Pam, a staple character from the beginning, also feels majorly underwritten, as if they had no idea what to do with her.

While there are plenty of flaws, if you enjoy the ride, especially the building tension between Greg and Olivia, the movie is completely watchable and makes for a decent enough time. The things that fall flat aren't egregious, mostly just stuff you've seen before in many comedies just like this one. It's a serviceable new entry in this franchise, although we really don't need the sequel it tries to set up.
