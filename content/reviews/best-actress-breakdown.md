We're just a week away from the Venice Film Festival kicking off, which will be shortly followed by Telluride and TIFF, and Oscar pundits worldwide are hoping these festivals will provide them with a little bit of clarity when it comes to the Best Actress slate. As it stands, none of the top six films in our Fantasy Filmball community's Best Picture predictions have a female lead performance, making it especially hard to decipher who the frontrunners will be come the winter months. So, before a potential fall festival breakout performance sweeps everyone away and is declared the inevitable winner, let's take a look at the current field for Best Actress.

<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Julianne Moore</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>The Debut</em> · Jesse Eisenberg · A24</div>
</div>

**The actress:** One of our great, veteran actresses, Moore finally won her first Oscar in 2015 for *Still Alice*, on her fifth nomination. She hasn't been nominated since.

**The role:** Moore plays Mona Friedman, a shy woman who is unexpectedly cast as the lead in a local musical production, in Jesse Eisenberg's *The Debut*.

**Why it's happening:** She looks great in the trailer, she's an industry legend, and Eisenberg impressed many with his last film, *A Real Pain*, which received an Oscar in an acting category. Given the lack of female protagonists in this year's Best Picture contenders, if *The Debut* even sniffs a Best Picture slot, Moore will almost certainly come along for the ride.

**Why it's not happening:** The only way I see Moore missing is if *The Debut* becomes a total non-factor this awards season. It will premiere at TIFF, and if the reviews are lacklustre, I could see Moore sinking with the ship.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 02</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Inde Navarette</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Obsession</em> · Curry Barker · Focus Features</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/obsession-navarette.jpg" alt="Inde Navarette in Obsession" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Inde Navarette as Nikki in <em>Obsession</em></figcaption>
</figure>

**The actress:** Hollywood's new “it girl”, Navarette has appeared in a few high-profile television series, but her big break in film came earlier this year with the horror smash hit, *Obsession*.

**The role:** She plays Nikki, a woman who is cursed by her friend Bear into being head-over-heels in love with him, to a scary degree.

**Why it's happening:** Navarette is the talk of the town, and will almost certainly be considered the breakout star of 2026 come the end of the year. The Oscars love rewarding their breakout ingénues with nominations — Lupita Nyong'o for *12 Years a Slave*, Jennifer Lawrence for *Winter's Bone*, Mikey Madison for *Anora*. Her performance is also considered to be the best thing about *Obsession*, which has been the biggest Hollywood success story of the year.

**Why it's not happening:** Speaking of Lupita Nyong'o, the Academy often ignores great performances from horror films. Things have been looking up, with the likes of *The Substance* picking up five nominations or Michael B. Jordan winning Best Actor last year for *Sinners*, but both films are just as recognised for the social commentary they provide as they are for their thrills. It might be controversial to say, but the main audience for *Obsession* are horror fans, which I don't think can be said for the other examples. Perhaps it will be a bridge too far for the Academy.

<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 03</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Renate Reinsve</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Fjord</em> · Cristian Mungiu · Neon</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/fjord-reinsve.jpg" alt="Renate Reinsve in Fjord" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan in <em>Fjord</em></figcaption>
</figure>

**The actress:** The Norwegian actress has been on a steady rise in Hollywood since her breakout performance in 2021's *The Worst Person in the World*. She has since received her first Oscar nomination for last year's *Sentimental Value*, and stars in one of this year's biggest box office successes, *Backrooms*.

**The role:** Reinsve plays Lisbet, a conservative wife and mother who, along with her husband Mihai, faces scrutiny from the members of the Norwegian village to which the family recently moved. Cristian Mungiu's *Fjord* won the Palme d'Or at this year's Cannes Film Festival.

**Why it's happening:** Four of the last six Palme d'Or winners have been nominated for Best Picture, including all three to be at least partially in English. Chances are, *Fjord* will be a Best Picture nominee, and if that's the case, Reinsve is currently the actress most likely to be a lead in a Best Picture nominee, which gives her a great advantage.

**Why it's not happening:** People who've seen the film say that the lead performances are not very showy, and that there aren't many closeups of the stars to really show off their chops. In a crowded field, she probably would not be so high up this list — the fact that her co-star Sebastian Stan wasn't even listed in our [Best Actor breakdown](/review.html?slug=best-actor-breakdown) should tell you everything you need to know.

<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 04</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Mikey Madison</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>The Social Reckoning</em> · Aaron Sorkin · Sony</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/the-social-reckoning-madison.jpg" alt="Mikey Madison in The Social Reckoning" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Mikey Madison as Frances Haugen in <em>The Social Reckoning</em></figcaption>

**The actress:** Our Best Actress winner of 2024, Madison's breakout performance in *Anora* has cemented her as one of the hottest young stars of Hollywood.

**The role:** Madison plays Frances Haugen, one of the whistleblowers behind the 2021 Facebook leak.

**Why it's happening:** Madison's big follow-up after her Oscar win will surely catch the attention of the Academy, and the status of *The Social Network* as one of the great films of this century will help propel this “sequel” as well. Sorkin is also quite good at getting his actors nominated; his last two films have received a combined four acting nominations.

**Why it's not happening:** The online community was not too impressed by *The Social Reckoning* trailer. If this ends up being a disappointment, especially when compared to its great predecessor, Madison's performance would have to be utterly show-stopping for her to come out unscathed from the fallout.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 05</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Virginie Efira</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>All of a Sudden</em> · Ryusuke Hamaguchi · Neon</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/all-of-a-sudden-efira.jpg" alt="Virginie Efira in All of a Sudden" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto in <em>All of a Sudden</em></figcaption>

**The actress:** The Belgian-French actress has been a mainstay in the French film awards circuit over the last decade, having received her first César Award for Best Actress in 2023, for her performance in *Paris Memories*, after her sixth nomination.

**The role:** She plays Marie-Lou, the director of a nursing home for patients with dementia, who tries to implement a human care technique called Humanitude.

**Why it's happening:** Efira won Best Actress at this year's Cannes Film Festival, along with her co-star Tao Okamoto. People who've seen the film believe her performance is worthy of accolades, and if Hamaguchi manages to get a second film into the Best Picture lineup, after 2021's *Drive My Car*, her nomination will surely come with it.

**Why it's not happening:** At this point, it's hard to know what films Neon will prioritise this awards season. One would assume *Fjord* will be their top priority, given it won the Palme d'Or and features a couple of Oscar nominees in the cast. They've also acquired Luca Guadagnino's *Artificial*, which is said to be receiving an awards push this year. Being a French drama that lasts over three hours, Neon may conclude that *All of a Sudden* is too inaccessible to prioritise over the other two films.

<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 06</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Olivia Wilde</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>The Invite</em> · Olivia Wilde · A24</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/the-invite-wilde.jpg" alt="Olivia Wilde in The Invite" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Olivia Wilde in <em>The Invite</em></figcaption>

**The actress:** Though today she's more recognised as the filmmaker behind *Booksmart* and *Don't Worry Darling*, she had a long career as an actress before her directorial debut.

**The role:** Wilde directs and stars in *The Invite*, a chamber piece comedy about a married couple (played by her and Seth Rogen) who invite their neighbours to their house for a hilariously awkward dinner.

**Why it's happening:** *The Invite* is considered Wilde's best film thus far, as an actress as well as a filmmaker. It was a big hit for A24, and is currently one of the most acclaimed films of 2026. Whether or not that will continue to be the case by awards season is yet to be seen.

**Why it's not happening:** A24's slate looks stacked this year, between *The Invite*, *Club Kid*, *Misty Green*, *Primetime*, and *The Debut*. Though most of these have not yet been screened to the public, it's possible that A24 will look to prioritise the newer releases that will be fresher on voters' minds over the summer comedy.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 07</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Cynthia Erivo</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Prima Facie</em> · Susanna White</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/prima-facie-erivo.jpg" alt="Cynthia Erivo in Prima Facie" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Cynthia Erivo as Tessa Ensler in <em>Prima Facie</em></figcaption>

**The actress:** A Tony Award-winning musical theatre actress before she made her film debut in Steve McQueen's *Widows*, Erivo has since gone on to receive three Oscar nominations — including for her performance as Elphaba in *Wicked* — and is just an Academy Award away from joining the EGOT club.

**The role:** She plays Tessa Ensler, a defence barrister, in Susanna White's *Prima Facie*, based on the stage play of the same name.

**Why it's happening:** Jodie Comer, who played Ensler on Broadway and the West End, won all the major theatre awards for Best Actress. It's not hard to imagine Erivo, who's now a familiar face with the Academy, getting at least nominated for this role that is said to be quite showy.

**Why it's not happening:** The counter-argument is that the play was a one-woman show; the film having a full cast of characters may diminish what made the character so special on stage in the first place.

<div style="max-width:720px;margin:3.5rem auto 1.5rem;border-bottom:2px solid var(--ink);padding-bottom:1.1rem;">
<div style="font-family:var(--font-mono);font-size:0.72rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:0.2em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--crimson);margin-bottom:0.45rem;">Contender 08</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-weight:700;font-size:2.3rem;line-height:1.02;letter-spacing:-0.01em;color:var(--ink);">Rosalind Eleazar</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-body);font-size:0.95rem;color:var(--ink-muted);margin-top:0.5rem;"><em>Misty Green</em> · Chris Rock · A24</div>
</div>

<figure class="article-graphic">
<img src="/posters/misty-green-eleazar.jpg" alt="Rosalind Eleazar in Misty Green" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;" />
<figcaption>Rosalind Eleazar in <em>Misty Green</em></figcaption>
</figure>

**The actress:** English actress Rosalind Eleazar is best known for her work in television, particularly for her role in the series *Slow Horses*. Though she's made some appearances in film, *Misty Green* is said to be her most high-profile project yet.

**The role:** Eleazar plays the title character Misty Green, an award-winning actress who seeks to reconnect with her past, in the A24 project directed by Chris Rock.

**Why it's happening:** With a cast that features Academy Award alumni Adam Driver, Anna Kendrick, and Daniel Kaluuya, *Misty Green* is one of the most promising films slated for a TIFF premiere. Eleazar has already been honoured with a tribute award by the festival for her performance in the film.

**Why it's not happening:** Chris Rock has never directed an Oscar-nominated film before, with his best effort being *Top Five*, which received a handful of Critics Choice nominations in the comedy-specific categories. *Misty Green* would need to be a big step up for the filmmaker if we're to expect it to get into awards conversations.
