Ryan Gosling never fails to command the screen anytime he’s on it. Whether in a comedy or drama, his ability to draw me in is unmatched. Given that we spend the entire movie with him, and that he especially excels at comedic acting, his pitch-perfect casting as lead role Ryland Grace already gives *Project Hail Mary* an advantage.

As we dive into the movie, we go back and forth between two timelines. One is the current day, with Grace on the ship, and the other is the lead-up to the mission, giving us the needed exposition of what led up to the mission and how Grace ended up on the ship himself. It works surprisingly effectively. We don’t get too much exposition up front, leaving us with a sense of mystery as to why he’s the one on the ship and not the original astronauts. It also means we’re not waiting too long for the parts we really want to see, like we would have had the movie been told in chronological order. Everything is balanced well, giving us what we need when we need it.

As great as Gosling is in the movie, the true heart of the movie is Rocky, the alien voiced and puppeteered by James Ortiz. The choice to have Ortiz voice the character was perfect, as not only is his voice work strong, but it makes it feel so much more authentic knowing it’s the same person who controlled the puppet. Rocky is such a great character, bringing both the funniest moments and the most heartfelt moments. The connection he and Grace form to work toward their common goal is so cute and inspiring and adds a lot of levity to the situation at hand.

Despite her more limited screen time, Sandra Hüller also brings a great presence as Eva Stratt. In particular, later in the movie, when we find out how Grace ends up on the ship, Hüller has such a strong, authority-like presence in those scenes. Her earlier karaoke scene is also seen as a standout for many people, and for good reason.

The humor in general is top tier and exactly my type of humor. Everything from Rocky, in particular, lands so perfectly for me, and even Stratt, who’s typically more on the serious side, has a couple of moments that had me laughing hard. It’s mixed perfectly with the more serious, emotional moments, allowing both to shine at the right moments.

As great as the acting, character moments, humor, emotion, and story are, the technical elements are just as strong, if not stronger.

The production design is stellar, both inside and outside the ship, creating an atmosphere that’s fun to explore. Co-director Christopher Miller said in a tweet that the entire ship was built and no green screen was used for location purposes, which is extra impressive. The score has a great blend of music that weaves in perfectly with every scene, regardless of whether it’s an emotional or lighthearted scene. The cinematography and visual effects provide a big scope for the most stunning scenes out in space. Those scenes also do not use green screen, instead opting for a black background for space and a shifting-hue background for planets, also according to Miller’s tweet. This is a movie I’m so glad I saw in the theater, as every shot is so pleasing to look at, and the sound work is just as impressive.

All of these elements work so flawlessly together to create a film that I never wanted to look away from or stop watching. There are no weak-link elements. It was one of the easiest first-watch 10 out of 10s I have given in the last few years, and it won’t surprise me if it remains my favorite film of the year all the way until the end.
