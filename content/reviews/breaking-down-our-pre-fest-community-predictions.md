<h2>The Odyssey</h2>

Now that *The Odyssey* has finally been released, what's changed in our picks? Well, we're higher on it in every above-the-line category except for Robert Pattinson, who actually fell out of our top ten entirely. Of course, John Leguizamo and Samantha Morton more than made up for that, both arriving at 8th in their respective supporting categories after going unpredicted in June. The biggest change here is actually Matt Damon, whose nomination percentage ballooned from 36% to 86%, moving him from 5th to 3rd in Lead Actor.

*The Odyssey* is currently predicted for 9 wins, more than any other film on the board, including Best Picture. We have it winning Cinematography, Editing, Production Design, Costume Design, Sound and Score, with nominations in Casting, Makeup and Hairstyling, and Visual Effects.

At the top, the movement is less about whether and more about how much. Picture went from 98% to a clean 100% nominated, and Christopher Nolan did the same in Director, but the real jump is in the film's win chances. Picture climbed from 33% to 51%, meaning a majority of our predictors now have it taking the whole thing, and Nolan rose from 55% to 66%. Anne Hathaway made the loudest move of any ATL prospects, 74% to 95% nominated, 22% to 50% predicted to win, and a climb from 2nd to 1st in Supporting Actress. Half our community now has her winning an award she was a coin flip to even be nominated for in the spring. Importantly, there have been rumors that Universal is considering a Lead pivot for Hathaway, which will be a story to keep track of as we continue into fall festivals.

The one place *The Odyssey* went backward is Adapted Screenplay. Nolan's nomination is safer than ever, up from 88% to 94%, but his win percentage fell from 33% to 20%. That's the whole story of *The Black Ball*'s summer in a single line, as Ambrossi and Calvo went from 67% to a unanimous 100% nominated in the category, and they've taken the win narrative with them, moving up to 63%. It's a useful reminder that a film can be running away with the race and still have a soft spot.

I'm following the crowd here, and also have *Odyssey* winning Director, Picture, and Supporting Actress, in that order. Nolan's epic is lined up to gross over a billion dollars worldwide, and will definitely be the theatrical story of the year, with its IMAX window extended through mid-September. However, I'm still not sure if there will be enough passion to get Nolan a back-to-back picture win, and my "win tier" still includes *Digger*, *La Bola Negra* and *Wild Horse Nine*.

<h2>Obsession</h2>

<div style="font-family: var(--font-mono); font-size: 0.68rem; letter-spacing: 0.16em; text-transform: uppercase; color: var(--ink-faded); border-left: 3px solid var(--gold); padding: 0.15rem 0 0.15rem 0.85rem; margin: -0.4rem auto 1.75rem; max-width: 720px;">◆ Reporting on this section by <span style="color: var(--ink); font-weight: 600;">Vitor Cecchinato</span></div>

The number one film on the minds of many pundits currently is *Obsession*. The word-of-mouth giant has gone on an unprecedented run, forcing predictors to take a stand on its awards prospects. As of late, our community has come to predict *Obsession* within their Best Picture lineup, with 69% predicting it for a nomination. A nomination that much less seem to doubt is that of the movie's breakout Inde Navarrette, who ranks 2nd, with 82% predicting her for a nomination in Lead Actress. It's worth noting that Inde's category placement is still undecided, and an official shift to Supporting Actress is possible. Navarrette is predicted to win in Lead by 32% of the community's predictors, the most of any individual in the category. Julianne Moore's 91% nomination rate holds Inde to #2, but she remains a potent threat for the award. Notably, we expect Inde's win rate to jump even higher, with *A Place in Hell*'s recent delay, as Michelle Williams was predicted to win by 23% of the community.

The Fantasy Filmball community has 2 other nominations predicted for the film, being Original Screenplay at 64% predicted, and Casting at 35% predicted. If *Obsession* were to make the Best Picture lineup, it would be joining a very short list — of all the horror films ever nominated, only *The Silence of the Lambs* has gone on to win the award.

Compared to the community's last predictions, *Obsession* is the biggest riser in Best Picture, Best Actress, and Original Screenplay. The nominations for Inde Navarrette and the screenplay actually happened to be the 2 biggest risers among any Above The Line category, excluding Best Picture. The drastic change serves as a testament to just how remarkable *Obsession*'s run has been, and all eyes are on Focus Features to maintain this momentum into the fall.

<h2>The Invite</h2>

Now, let's move along to our 2nd largest riser in Best Picture, *The Invite*. Even though it still sits outside of our Best Picture lineup, in 11th place, *The Invite* jumped from 0% nominated to 44% nominated. Olivia Wilde's latest directorial effort was overlooked by the awards community at large for much of the season to date, but some within the Filmball circle, including Dylan McKercher, have been championing the film since its premiere. The film holds a 4.1 Letterboxd average, alongside an 82 Metacritic score, and has now accrued a worldwide gross of nearly $32 million, as of July 28th.

In addition to Best Picture, *The Invite* is a predicted contender in Adapted Screenplay, where 48% of the Filmball community anticipates a nomination, placing it 4th in our lineup. However, we're still much lower on the film's screenplay chances than the Awards Expert community, as 83% of app users have it within their 5.

Although Sundance doesn't generally produce multiple awards contenders within one season, the festival has produced 3 Best Picture nominees in the last 5 years. These films — *CODA*, *Past Lives* and *Train Dreams* — are generally smaller films that enraptured audiences, similarly to *The Invite*. I'm taking a wait and see approach to this one, holding it just outside of my lineup, unless some spots really open up. The biggest roadblock here will be the film's release date, as it will need to sustain momentum from its Winter premiere and Summer wide release. It's a situation where if I see *The Invite* appear in the American Film Institute (AFI) and National Board of Review (NBR) end of year lists, that alone might be enough for me to slot it in.

<h2>Behemoth!</h2>

The last major Best Picture riser of this update is Tony Gilroy's highly anticipated musical drama, *Behemoth!*. The film only rose from 8th to 6th within the ranking, but leapt from 56% to 83% nominated, a 27% gap. There's been rumors for months now about positive test screening reactions, mostly via World of Reel, specifically praising the non-linear structure of the story and Pascal's leading performance, described there as stellar. However, these reactions were already available to predictors prior to our last update, so this jump is likely due to the community at large becoming more receptive to the film, especially as American highbrow contenders, like *Cry to Heaven*, start to fade out of the race. Also, *Behemoth!*'s first teaser trailer released on July 14th, and was largely praised, which could've sparked some of this momentum as well. Notably, since this update occurred, *Behemoth!* was announced to be premiering at the New York Film Festival, which many see as a negative indicator, but we'll have to wait for the next update to see if that impacts the rankings.

Personally, I think our community may be too high on *Behemoth!* at this point, even though I've had it inside my lineup for months. I do like the idea of Pedro Pascal pairing with a director who has received Academy praise in Tony Gilroy, especially given the duo's recent success with *Andor*, but the film is still likely to be Searchlight's #2 priority, to *Wild Horse Nine*. *Behemoth!* currently ranks 9th in my personal predictions, more in line with the Awards Expert community, who has it 11th. Interestingly, Awards Expert is significantly lower on *Behemoth!* than we are, with 45% of users nominating the film in Picture, 38% lower than Filmball. There's a similarly large gap in Lead Actor and Original Screenplay, as the Filmball community is exactly 41% higher on the nomination for both categories when compared to AE.
