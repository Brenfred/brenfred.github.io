With fall festivals looming, another year of Fantasy Filmball is about to kick off, which makes this a perfect time for us to start theorizing what Round One could look like this year. Before we dive into this year's slate, I think it's worthwhile to take a trip down memory lane and see what some of the previous first rounds looked like across the game's history.

As history demonstrates, the number one pick is generally just the consensus Best Picture winner heading into the fall film festival season and, with updated Filmball rules and regulations, acing the number one pick matters more than ever. Of course, a player's first round pick is always supposed to be their MVP, but this year, with the introduction of categories, you have to be a little bit more precise, and with the first overall pick, there's even more pressure, given that the player drafting at 1.01 will need to wait 11 more picks for their 2nd round selection.

Now, many players believe the best strategy is to go for the film they predict will win Best Picture because, more often than not, the BP winner is also the overall points leader. However, another angle to consider is going for a film which is likely to be a mainstay at the top of the Best Picture race throughout the season. Even if this film doesn't come out victorious, it is going to be an Oscar contender across the board. Last year, *Sentimental Value* wasn't predicted to win Best Picture by many, but rivaled *Sinners* for the top pick by virtue of its perceived "safe" status as a first overall selection.

This year, there's one movie that, in my opinion, is head and shoulders above the rest, being Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey*. If you look at [our community consensus](/oscar-race.html), Award Expert, Gold Derby, or various pundits, *The Odyssey* is clearly the favorite to win Best Picture, as of August 6th. It's for good reason; the film has loads of tech and craft potential, is on track to receive at least one, if not up to four, total acting nominations, and looks win competitive in Screenplay, Casting, Director, and, of course, Best Picture.

I think *The Odyssey* is both the safest movie on the board and has one of the highest ceilings. Even if it is not our eventual Picture winner, I could see this being a very similar situation to 2021, where *CODA* may have won Picture, but it was not the highest-scoring film in the game. That honor belonged to *The Power of the Dog*, and I can totally see *The Odyssey* being that type of film in a worst-case scenario.

This is where those who do not have *The Odyssey* winning Best Picture could take the film that they do believe to be the next Best Picture winner. Looking at our community consensus, *La Bola Negra* has 32% of the vote, and *Wild Horse Nine* has 10% of the Filmball community's backing, while on Award Expert, *Digger* has 4% of the community behind it. Therefore, I think these are the films that we're really looking at for these next few picks.

In my opinion, if you have the number two overall pick, this is the year where I may look to trade down. I would be okay with any of the aforementioned three films, so I'd look to see if anyone is offering a better second-round pick, given that their point totals are likely similar in a world where *The Odyssey* wins Best Picture.

But *La Bola Negra*, on average, is likely to be the second film off the board because drafters seem to be heavily influenced by the Cannes Film Festival. Just look at last year, where *Sentimental Value* was going first overall across six leagues in a world where that film was not a favorite to win either Picture or Director. Or, look to previous years, where we've seen *Anatomy of a Fall* go in the first round, higher than its placement in the Gold Derby Best Picture standings, and of course *Anora* going #1 overall in all but five leagues despite not being a dominant frontrunner in August, or for much of the race, for that matter.

This year, Netflix has *La Bola Negra*, the Cannes Best Director winner that many pundits believe is poised to get double-digit nominations and compete for the Best Picture win. If you're taking *La Bola Negra* with the #2 overall pick, you are banking on a very strong nomination haul and for some overperformance, in the form of wins. You'd want to see Penélope Cruz win Supporting Actress; you would want to see the Adapted Screenplay win; you'd want to see the film take Casting or maybe some technical categories…

If you're taking *La Bola Negra* at #2, really, you need to envision the film receiving more Academy love than just the International Feature victory of *Sentimental Value* last year. However, there is still an argument that *La Bola Negra* is the safest option of the next tier of films because it has been seen. We know that audiences like this movie, and it won a major prize at arguably the calendar's most important festival, which we can't yet say about the next films on the table.

I personally believe there are options with higher ceilings than *La Bola Negra*, but, if you need an anchor for your team, this feels like the best option on the table.

As addressed above, I believe the top four movies are all fighting for a spot inside the top four picks. The real question is just, in your league, who has more faith in which films, and I personally have this matchup of three and four as pretty even. The difference between *Wild Horse Nine* and *Digger* comes down to two key categories.

Coming off two straight Screenplay victories at the Venice Film Festival, arguably two films in a row finishing in the top three in the Best Picture race, and two films that ended their respective season as top three point scorers in Filmball, Martin McDonagh seems poised to finally break through and earn at least one Oscar victory to his name from one of his films. While *Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri* may have won multiple acting prizes and *The Banshees of Inisherin* had an amazing performance at the BAFTAs, neither of those films hit paydirt at the Oscars.

*Wild Horse Nine* boasts a pretty hefty potential points package throughout the season. You should get pretty high ratings from your leaguemates, and it's a film that seems like it's going to win a festival prize, have pretty high critic scores, and, as I've mentioned before, be fighting for the win in two key above-the-line categories: Best Lead Actor for John Malkovich as well as Original Screenplay.

However, if you think *Wild Horse Nine* is not going to be victorious in either of these categories, I would let the film slide to someone else. This is a spot where you take it only if you see it as the "best-of-the-rest." I could see that maybe it doesn't win Best Picture, but it's still likely to have multiple prizes to its name.

But the real reason why I think *Wild Horse Nine* will go at pick number three on average over the next film is that it's so much safer than the alternative. We've heard rumblings that this movie has played pretty well at test screenings, and we've seen two straight McDonagh movies be great awards contenders. If you're taking *Wild Horse Nine*, you're at ease of mind, knowing you probably don't have a flop on your team, while on the other hand…

The last of "Tier 1" films, being the films that have a realistic possibility of competing for that Best Picture prize, is Alejandro González Iñárritu's *Digger*.

The real reason *Digger* is at the back of the pack is just the filmmaker himself; AGI is a very divisive individual. We've seen the Academy adore him, all the way to the fact that he went back-to-back with Best Director prizes for *Birdman* and *The Revenant*. We've also seen critics have the knives out for Iñárritu too. Look at *Bardo* a few years ago, a film that was a massive flop in Filmball, as it was often taken within the top five.

As I mentioned before, every year our top six consensus picks have at least one film that flops out of the race. Look to *Bardo* and *The Son* in 2022, *Dune: Part Two* in 2023, *Joker: Folie à Deux* and *Saturday Night* in 2024, and, of course, last year we had *Jay Kelly* and *Wicked: For Good*. Among the films that I think could go in the first round, *Digger* fits this mold the most.

There's all this online chatter about Tom Cruise finally winning his Best Actor prize, the film coming from a director with an amazing track record at the Academy Awards, and it being "too big to fail." Usually when people make these narratives ahead of time, sometimes it really is too big to succeed…

While I personally may be a little bit higher on *Digger* than *Wild Horse Nine* in terms of its ceiling, considering *Digger* could bring more techs along, *Digger* absolutely, 100% has a lower floor than *Wild Horse Nine*. As such, I think we'll see more risk-averse owners wary of spending their first selection on Warner Bros's latest, pushing it down the average draft order a bit.

To round out the first round, and to get into the majority of Round Two, I think you're looking at "Tier 2" contenders. Films that reside here are Best Picture nomination candidates, but most likely are only competing for one above-the-line victory, or are looking at multiple tech prizes with no above-the-line wins and are largely unlikely to win Picture.

But as we've seen in previous years, according to our ADP, Round One typically has one major film that's already been released, adding an element of safety. These films are known quantities, being that we know both audiences and critics are on board, and there are guaranteed awards prospects in store. Even though this archetype is most often not a Best Picture winner, drafters prioritize the safer points, which makes this type of film a good first-round selection.

Think of a film like *Barbie* or *Dune: Part Two*, which were going, on average, between the two and four slots. This year, a film I think perfectly fits into the established mold is *Project Hail Mary*.

Now, of course, this is where subjectivity comes into play, but look yourself in the mirror. Do you really think *Project Hail Mary* is winning multiple above-the-line Oscars? Probably not. Ryan Gosling is not going to win Lead Actor. Sandra Hüller is not winning Supporting Actress. Lord and Miller may not even be nominated for Director. Is Greig Fraser winning you Cinematography, or Daniel Pemberton for Score? I would say no.

You can make an argument for Sound or Visual Effects, but last year showed that contenders outside of the Best Picture lineup can still take VFX home. *Dune: Part Three* isn't winning Sound, I think it would go to the Best Picture winner, *The Odyssey*, before we look at something like *Project Hail Mary*.

So, in my opinion, you're looking at one real win shot for this film, but it's safe. It's going to get those nominations; it's going to be a contender all season long. Just how big of a contender is another question, but I think for the Filmball community at large, it's a movie they like, it's a movie they want to see succeed, and it's a movie they're going to draft in the first round.

Now, we've reached our sixth, and final, pick of the first round, which is likely to be the most varied selection across all leagues. Even if the previous five films don't go in the exact order above, I would expect those five films to, more likely than not, be off the board at this point in the draft. If one of them is somehow available, this selection becomes pretty automatic.

Let's recap so far. Our Best Picture frontrunner, *The Odyssey*, will be long gone by pick number six. Looking to previous years, movies like *Sinners* were taken as late as pick three only once across all 21 leagues. *Anora* only lasted to pick three across two leagues, and, all the way back in 2023, *Oppenheimer* was picked first overall in all but a single league.

Next, we look to our major festival film, the movie that Cannes brings to the table as our Academy darling. This year, that's looking to be *La Bola Negra*. Last year, *Sentimental Value* went by pick three in every league, and in 2024, as I mentioned before, *Anora* similarly was gone by pick three. In 2023, you had two options: *Anatomy of a Fall* and *The Zone of Interest*. Both of these films were around at pick six, with average selections of 7.5 and 9.3, respectively. But as we see, when there's just one major festival player, those films get snatched up pretty quickly.

Moving on, we have films that could be major contenders in Screenplay and Acting, like *Wild Horse Nine* and *Digger*. Last year, *Marty Supreme* averaged third across all films, going as high as pick three but sometimes dropping as low as pick 10. In 2024, you had *Conclave*, which went first overall in one league but lasted all the way until seven in another. And, in 2023, we saw a movie like *Killers of the Flower Moon* being selected at either pick two or three across all leagues.

Of course, there is the populist film to come out before drafts, the film everyone knows is liked and is going to be a contender, with the question being just *how* big. Last year, *Sinners* was selected early and often, just like *Dune: Part Two* and just like *Barbie*, which went first in one league, went second or third across nearly all other leagues. That slot is going to be filled by *Project Hail Mary* this year.

So now, with all of those types of movies out of the way, what are we really left with? We have another movie that has been seen, we know is really liked, is competing for both an above-the-line victory and a Best Picture nomination, and has overall great buzz, but has a questionable package, in *Obsession*.

We saw a film with similar positives, *Past Lives*, get taken off the board on average fourth with a 5.3 ADP back in 2023, and *Sing Sing* went off the board fourth with an ADP of 3.8. Unlike those two movies, *Obsession* was a box office smash. As of writing, it's made over $500 million worldwide, is a true cultural phenomenon, and, without question, is Focus' priority heading into festival season.

*Sing Sing* had to battle with A24 purchasing *The Brutalist* at Venice, and *Past Lives* had to deal with *The Zone of Interest* out of Cannes. Some Focus Features believers think that they will go with another film like *Elsinore*, but, chips on the table, it seems like their priority might simply be *Obsession*.

Then, we have the holiday technical craft movie that is just "too big to fail." Look to last year, *Wicked: For Good* and *Avatar: Fire and Ash*. *Wicked* went on average at 6.1, with *Avatar* going around the 11.2 mark. Both of those films inevitably missed the Best Picture nomination, but you could say that *Dune: Part Three* has a little bit more gas in the tank than either of those movies had.

It's *Dune*. It's more akin to something like *Frankenstein*, which had a 7.6 ADP, getting selected in the first round across six leagues. If we go back one more year, to 2024, *Joker: Folie à Deux* went on average at 4.75, and, yes, missed out on the Best Picture nomination. Finally, in 2023, you had *The Color Purple* go on average at 10.3, just outside the first round.

But, as I said, *Dune* is in much stronger shape than any of those movies I just mentioned. Both previous *Dune* films have won Oscars, have been nominated for Best Picture, and, unlike *Avatar: Fire and Ash*, this *Dune* film seems to be receiving actual promotion, with its studio showing major signs of confidence.

Writing this article as the Na'vi Nation Warrior himself, even I can admit 20th Century did nothing for *Fire and Ash* until the movie was actually released. Critics got to see it in early December, but that was really the first real conversation around *Avatar* all year. We are still over three months from release, and we've had multiple trailers, a Q&A with Denis, and plenty of IMAX theaters already selling out as early as Spring.

But the question still stands: is the Academy's proven track record of being lower and lower on each sequel going to ring true for *Dune* as it did for *Fire and Ash*, *For Good*, or even *Gladiator II*? Or will *Dune: Part Three* instead break the mold and have a massive awards-season performance?

The last film I could see jumping up into the end of the first round is that secondary international contender. Last year we really didn't have a film that fit this mold, outside of maybe *It Was Just an Accident*, which went at pick 14 on average. Granted, the Palme winner was met with significant negativity, with Filmball members who attended the Cannes Film Festival, so I think the Filmball community was a lot lower on the film than the wider awards community.

While *Accident* eventually missed out on the Best Picture nomination, it would've been a value pick at 14 because it ended last year as the ninth-highest scoring title. 2024 saw a film like *Emilia Pérez* go right outside the first round at pick eight, next to movies like *Nickel Boys*, *Saturday Night*, and *A Complete Unknown*, and proved to be a major awards season darling, scoring in the top three in total points for that season.

Then, in 2023, the secondary Cannes Film Festival film would have been *The Zone of Interest*, which went, on average, ninth off the board and finished the season as a top-seven scorer.

While I know the Filmball community is a little lower on *Fjord* than I am, or the awards community at large, we've had three straight years of our community undervaluing these types of movies. Even if they miss the Best Picture nomination, they still outperform their average draft position. While pick six would be a little bit high for *Fjord*, especially with the comparisons to *The Zone of Interest* and *It Was Just an Accident*, I don't think this is the type of film that people should let fall further and further down the draft board, especially when we start to dive into the next tier of Best Picture contenders, like *The Debut*, *Behemoth!*, *The Invite*, *Elsinore*, or *Ink*.
