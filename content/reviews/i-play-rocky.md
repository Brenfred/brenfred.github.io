In 1976, the world met *Rocky*, the underdog boxer who went on to make history. Starring unknown actor and the film's writer Sylvester Stallone, it became a massive success, the year's highest-grossing film and the winner of the Academy Award for Best Picture. This year, *I Play Rocky* premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival to mixed critical reception but overwhelming audience acclaim. In 2018, the man who would go on to direct *I Play Rocky* premiered the film *Green Book* to great acclaim, winning a controversial Best Picture prize at the Academy Awards. That man is Peter Farrelly, and *I Play Rocky* marks his triumphant return to film after his screwball comedies and the mixed reception of Best Picture winner *Green Book*.

For the longest time after the film's announcement, people were hesitant about casting Anthony Ippolito. A practical unknown playing the role of a movie icon with such specific qualities to hit? It sounds difficult to say the least, but Ippolito proved himself, delivering one of the absolute best performances of the year. Most biopic performances are often criticized for lacking emotion or any depth beyond the impression, and while Ippolito nails the impression, he doesn't give up an inch of ability in creating a character with interpersonal conflicts that a viewer can attach themselves to.

Thankfully, Ippolito isn't the only strong performance in the film. Not by a long shot. The obvious pick is AnnaSophia Robb as Sasha Czack, Stallone's girlfriend, wife, and the film's deuteragonist. The actress makes for a likable scene partner to Ippolito. While she never feels like a stronger performance or a more engaging character compared to her costar, she still keeps up with him in most scenes. Another strong on-screen presence comes from the comedic relief duo of *Rocky* producers played by P.J. Byrne and Toby Kebbell. Unlike most comedic relief in films, they get a proper amount of screen time, making it feel like they land enough jokes to fit the film.

However, the two supporting performances that steal the show are Stephan James and Jay Duplass. James plays Carl Weathers in such an engaging, fun way that the audience, including me, just could not get enough of him. But the surprise was Jay Duplass as *Rocky* director John G. Avildsen. He is electric throughout the entire runtime, in a fun, crazy, and perfectly cast performance.

Although Farrelly as a filmmaker has garnered some ill reception since *Green Book*'s Best Picture win, his direction in *I Play Rocky* is really well done. The film looks great, at least in part because of Farrelly's vision for the story. Even if the direction is well done, there's admittedly not much to say about it, so the more notable topic to discuss would be the technical elements.

The cinematography is the surprise star of the film's roster of techs. The framing of many shots is beautifully done, such as the car scene shown in the trailer. The editing is also notably adept, skillfully making its way through multiple periods of *Rocky*'s production. The costume design and production design aren't necessarily showy, but they work as such a fantastic recreation of everything surrounding the story. The makeup and hairstyling work isn't necessarily showy, but it's very well done, particularly on Stephan James. It is overall technically adept, and there's little about the technical design to complain.

Where *I Play Rocky* comes short of being phenomenal is in its script. Admittedly still a well-written film, *I Play Rocky*'s structure works somewhat to ruin it. One major part of *Rocky* is practically glossed over in the film, with mentions of it building it up as a potential climax, only for that climax to work as another scene. It also ends in a bit of a weird way, which, although it still ends up satisfying, just feels weird within the film's pacing. The script also underuses many great supporting roles, most notably Stephan James, who is completely absent from the movie not long after his introduction.

*I Play Rocky* is now in a contentious position. It received mediocre critical ratings from multiple platforms, but is still completely raved about by many of the general viewers. Historically, this is a difficult position to convert, with the sole exception of one factor. The TIFF People's Choice Award. The PCA serves as the quintessential qualifier for crowd pleasers to become Oscar contenders. Other than the very weird situation surrounding *The Life of Chuck*, its winners have become Best Picture nominees for over 10 years. In 2018, *Green Book* won the People's Choice Award, a strong factor in its Best Picture win. Today, *I Play Rocky*'s fate may hang in the balance, but the star-making, heartwarming film may be set to do just as it needs.
