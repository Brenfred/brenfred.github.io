Ever since this year's Cannes Film Festival, the talk of the town has been about what a surefire audience hit *Club Kid* is. Jordan Firstman's directorial debut follows Peter, a gay NYC club promoter whose life takes an unexpected turn when 10-year-old Arlo, a boy from London, shows up on his doorstep claiming to be his long-lost son, the result of a drug-fueled threesome. What follows is an energetic, hilarious, and surprisingly heartfelt coming-of-age story that makes you feel like you're riding a wave for the entire runtime without ever coming crashing down.

Like, sure, *Club Kid* has been called "Gay Big Daddy," and if you know anything about me, you know I grew up loving the Sprouse twins. I watched *The Suite Life of Zack & Cody* and *The Suite Life on Deck* religiously as a child. I even went through a phase where my grandmother Photoshopped me into a picture with them, as if I were their long-lost triplet. That also means I watched *Big Daddy* countless times growing up, probably way too young for a kid to be watching that movie. So, when I heard that Firstman, the really funny guy from *I Love LA*, was writing, directing, and starring in his own version of *Big Daddy*, I was hooked. Then the reactions coming out of Cannes started rolling in, with people calling this thing an absolute sensation, and I could only get more and more excited for what was to come.

Fast-forward to the other night, when I found myself watching *Club Kid* on 35mm in front of a sold-out house of 450 Philadelphians. Let's just say I felt that cinema high we come to festivals for. There's something special about experiencing a film like this with a packed theater, where every laugh, gasp, and reaction becomes part of the experience. And *Club Kid* is exactly the kind of movie that makes you appreciate the magic of seeing something on the big screen alongside an audience.

From the opening moments, *Club Kid* grabs your attention with a 360-degree spin shot reminiscent of the opening of Trey Edward Shults' *Waves* (another incredible A24 film). From there, the energy only continues to build until you feel like you're right there in the clubs, partying and tripping alongside the cast. I especially love the camera movements, pulse-pounding sounds, and visuals in this opening set piece, which create an immersive and intimate experience that makes the nightlife feel real. You're instantly hooked on what this wild trip has in store. But what makes the film work is that beneath all the chaos, neon-soaked visuals, and club-hopping madness is a surprisingly tender story about connection, growing up, and finding family in the most unexpected places. As we're introduced to the characters, you can't help but fall in love with this world.

*Club Kid* has everything you could want from a coming-of-age story. It packs in the laughs that make your cheeks hurt, delivers heartwarming moments between father and son that make your heart grow three sizes, and then rips that very same heart into pieces moments later. *Club Kid* is the kind of film that effortlessly moves between hilarious and heartbreaking without ever losing its distinctive, infectious energy. By the time the credits roll two hours later, you're left with the perfect combination of a smile on your face and tears in your eyes.

Sure, Firstman has delivered an absolute heater of a debut, but the MVP of this film for me is young actor Reggie Abelson, who plays Arlo. There's a joke in the film about how Arlo is destined to be something big, how he's wise beyond his years and incredibly talented. Well, Abelson absolutely embodies that. He has a natural screen presence and the kind of charisma that makes it impossible to take your eyes off him. He's a genuine standout, and I can't wait to see what he has lined up for the future because he completely won me over in *Club Kid*.

Overall, *Club Kid* is an absolute blast. It's hilarious, incredibly rewatchable, and exactly the kind of film that gets people talking long after the credits roll. And judging by the reaction it received throughout the festival, I certainly wasn't alone in feeling that way. The film went on to take home the Narrative Audience Award, earning top honors at the 35th annual Philadelphia Film Festival and proving just how much it connected with audiences.

More importantly, it set the Philadelphia festival audience ablaze, even in the middle of a nasty rainstorm. Gather your friends, gather your loved ones, hell, even bring your parents. Take a trip to the theater, buy a ticket, and have a damn good time. If this is any indication of what's to come for Jordan Firstman, he's absolutely a name worth keeping an eye on.
