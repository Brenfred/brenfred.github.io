Mabel is a young woman that cares about protecting animals and the environment. When she discovers one of her teachers, Dr. Sam is experimenting with technology that lets humans put their mind into a robot version of an animal, Mabel disguises herself as a beaver to help save a dam that Mayor Jerry is trying to destroy. 

We all know the concept of putting your brain into something else and living through that other thing isn’t new. The movie itself even directly references that this is just like *Avatar.* However, that self acknowledgement didn’t feel entirely necessary, as the movie still finds ways to have its own identity.

*Hoppers* lives or dies on the investment of Mabel learning to connect with the animals, and there's some solid world building there. She's immediately thrust into their way of living through the introduction of “pond rules”. When she saves another beaver from being eaten by a bear, shes surprised to learn the beaver was fine with being eaten, because that's just how things work. This leads her to learning all about the ecosystem the animals have set up down there, with each group having their own leaders. This is all a clever way to show how much of a fish out of water she is in this world, despite the fact that she's going into it to lead a charge.

That disconnect becomes more clear when she actually tries to communicate her concerns with humans destroying the animals land. A large portion of the animals respond to this differently than she wants them to, and plan to take action in a way that's not beneficial to her or the rest of the human species at all. She has to pivot and find a way to save everyone along the way. This makes the second half of the movie very thrilling as she has to prevent the movement she unintentionally started while still achieving her initial goal.

The humor in this movie is, at times, quite clearly aimed at kids, but most of it still works. It works because these characters are so likable, and the bonds between them are so easy to invest in. The movie succeeds in laying the foundation that lets these moments be funny because you're locked in to these characters.

*Hoppers* also isn't afraid to go for big, surprising moments. One moment in particular made my eyes go wide in the theater. The movie simply never stops being fun. The animation and voice acting is also top notch, working together to create an experience that's both visually and audibly appealing.

Even if *Hoppers* feels like one you've seen before, which it can, it's one of the best versions of it. *Hoppers* is endlessly entertaining, very funny, and has great themes about community and working together. Best of all, it's a movie people of all ages can enjoy.
