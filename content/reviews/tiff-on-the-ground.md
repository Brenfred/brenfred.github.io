As the fall festival season comes to an end, so does maybe the biggest festival of the year: The Toronto International Film Festival. Home to over a hundred Best Picture winners, TIFF is one of the biggest hubs for award success every year. This year, I came to the film festival to report on the success and failure stories of the festival. From new films like [*I Play Rocky*](/review.html?slug=i-play-rocky) to [*Misty Green*](/review.html?slug=misty-green) to holdover powerhouses like *Fjord* and *Wild Horse Nine*, here’s what succeeded and what didn’t.

<h2>The Hits</h2>

<h3><em>Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother</em></h3>

Of all the TIFF World Premieres, the most unanimous acclaim is that of Bassam Tariq’s *Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother*. Boasting an 85 on Metacritic and highly positive Letterboxd score, it could see itself slingshot into awards season, with acclaim for Mahershala Ali on its side.

<h3><em>I Play Rocky</em></h3>

Despite lukewarm critical reactions, *I Play Rocky* is hitting extremely well at Toronto. An added screening indicating heavy audience passion, resounding applause for actor Anthony Ippolito, and scores on Letterboxd indicate Peter Farrelly’s new film hit in a big way, and could be a bigger contender than many believed.

<h3>The Platform Section</h3>

No, there is not a world premiere titled *The Platform Section* (Actually that sounds cool, I need to trademark that). I am instead referring to the side bar competition at TIFF. Recently, it was announced that the winners of multiple festival awards would automatically be entered into eligibility for the international feature Oscars (pending they meet the foreign language requirements of course). One such award is the prize for the Platform sidebar at TIFF. As such, there are more eyes than ever on the section, and we are left wondering if anything in the category could break out and become a contender.

<h3>Most TIFF Crowdpleasers</h3>

TIFF is known as a hub for crowdpleasers, and this year is no different. At the top of that list there are many that did quite well. There were very positive reviews for films like *Being Heumann* and *Onwards and Sideways*. Even some of the more generic crowdpleasers like *The Julia Set* received some positive reviews from audiences.

<h2>The Misses</h2>

<h3><em>The Last Photograph</em></h3>

It may be cheating to include a film with a singular screening, no distribution, and no eligibility for the People’s Choice Award, but [Zack Snyder’s film](/review.html?slug=the-last-photograph) bombing as badly as it did does not deserve to go unsung.

<h3><em>Misty Green</em></h3>

While the concept of high hopes for a movie directed by the guy who Will Smith slapped may seem ridiculous, it made more sense than it would seem. Chris Rock may be a career actor, but he has a notable track record of directing good films. That track record falls with *Misty Green*, which although has solid critic scores, got wrecked by audiences. This movie had promise, and Rosalind Eleazar is very solid in it, but it simply was not a success.

<h3><em>Prima Facie</em></h3>

Rotten Tomatoes is rarely a reliable metric unless a film does really bad. *Prima Facie* has a 27%. That’s really bad. Decent enough audience scores and praise for Cynthia Erivo won’t stop the adaptation of an award winning play from flopping this awards season.

<h2>The Survivors</h2>

<h3><em>Tender Loving Care</em></h3>

Last year, rumors circled the eligibility of *Hamnet* for TIFF’s International People’s Choice Award. It was said so at its screening but the idea of a slip up may have occurred, but at a screening of *Clarissa*, I had been able to confirm that all that matters for the award is non-Canadian or American production behind it. Another film confirmed eligible for the International People’s Choice Award is Mike Leigh’s supposedly final film. The reactions to the film ended up being phenomenal, receiving a standing ovation from an audience that clearly showed major interest at the TIFF premiere. Bleecker Street has never really proved themselves an awards-savvy distributor, but *Tender Loving Care* may be the movie they need to truly get themselves in the picture.

<h3><em>All of a Sudden</em></h3>

Similar to *I Play Rocky*, a big lucky break for *All of a Sudden* is the addition of a new screening, being one of the first of the festival to receive one. Aside from having good review scores and that added screening, there’s not much to write home about in particular, but the odds seem promising for Ryusuke Hamaguchi once again.

<h3><em>Elsinore</em></h3>

While reactions to the film itself were very good, the factor that matters is a 3 minute long standing ovation devoted to Andrew Scott and his performance as Ian Charleson. The actor’s performance is raved, carrying the movie to a similar level of love from the festival audience. Like *Tender Loving Care*, *Elsinore* is also eligible for the People’s Choice Award.

<h3><em>Wild Horse Nine</em></h3>

Andrew Scott may have received a standing ovation for his film, but what may be just as notable if not more is the whole cast. *Wild Horse Nine* did just that, playing incredibly at its TIFF screenings. I witnessed firsthand the film’s standing ovation on the 14th, with so much support for a film that was nothing less than raved at the end of the day.

<h3><em>La Bola Negra</em></h3>

On the topic of standing ovations, *La Bola Negra* had one of the festival’s longest. The Cannes audience breakout performed phenomenally at TIFF with the screenings it’s already had, and has the most seats of any film at TIFF this year. It’s hard not to say *La Bola Negra* is a success, as it is one of few to well and truly take Toronto by storm.

<h3><em>Club Kid</em></h3>

Speaking of Cannes audience favorites, standing ovations, and added TIFF screenings, *Club Kid* remains at the front of many awards conversations. The directorial debut of Jordan Firstman was heavily acclaimed after its Cannes release, becoming A24’s most expensive acquisition to date.

<h2>The Falloffs</h2>

<h3><em>Fjord</em></h3>

The TIFFr stat. Every year for the longest time, the most shortlisted and anticipated film on the website TIFFr gets a people’s choice nomination, as proven true with *Frankenstein* last year. But if any movie would break that stat, it is *Fjord*. A cold and procedural film, *Fjord* does not at all fit the TIFF People’s Choice Award. For all the crowdpleasers and heartbreakers, you could feel *Fjord* not hit with the crowd, some of whom actively laughed during scenes of the film not even due to the film being bad but a complete lack of emotion. What we have to consider is not a matter of quality but the film’s failure to hit with the TIFF audience. While *Fjord*’s awards odds should not be particularly reliant on it, the odds of it placing at this year’s People’s Choice Awards are frankly nonexistent.

<h3><em>The Debut</em></h3>

On the flip side of the TIFFr stat, *Fjord* is not entirely guaranteed to brave it out, as *The Debut* approaches hot on its tail. *The Debut* is a very promising contender for People’s Choice Award, so there’s likely some confusion as to why I have included it in the falloff section. I by no means wish to indicate *The Debut* will not be an awards contender, but the issue present is that *The Debut* left Telluride with extremely high critic scores, topping out at 90. As of TIFF, it has dropped to 79. Now 79 is by no means bad, it matches two films in the survivor section (*Elsinore* and *Wild Horse Nine*). The fact of the matter though is that it dropped, and maybe the passion it had at its Telluride premiere was not where it would end as this indicates. Either way *The Debut* is still a strong contender for many categories, but is maybe not quite the juggernaut some believed it would be out of the gate.

<h2>People’s Choice Awards: The Final Verdict</h2>

Every year, the Toronto International Film Festival’s attendees vote. And every year, 3 have been selected as the People’s Choice. Recently they have added an international slate of 3 as well. The results of this vote goes on to have gargantuan implications for the remainder of awards season. From my experience speaking with the audience at TIFF and seeing contenders, here is what I predict will be popping up this time.
