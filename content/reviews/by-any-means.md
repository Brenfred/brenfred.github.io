**“There’s no witness, there’s no crime!”**

Diane Warren lives as the bane of Oscar fans for the past decade. Every year, she writes a new original song about something optimistic and uplifting, performed by an artist that you likely respect to some degree but have no strong personal feelings about. The song plays during some random scene near the end of the movie, or the credits. You listen to it in the moment, not overly offended by its sound to a degree that a *Dance Monkey*-esque tune may elicit, but also not really catching on to any sonically interesting moment over the course of the song. By the time you’ve walked out of the theater, it’s already left your mind entirely. Yet, year after year, these forgettable pieces of music are consistently nominated for Academy Awards, snubbing major showstoppers from blockbusters, chart-topping hits, and lyrically important works from better movies. Furthermore, for those brave souls (like myself) that attempt to watch every Oscar nominee every year, it means a strange, random, irrelevant film gets added to the watchlist.

Usually, these films are inoffensively mediocre and leave the mind as quickly as they enter. This one, though… this really grinds my gears to a whole new level. This film is at its absolute best when it’s a total bore, because at least that rescues it from utter tonal confusion and concerningly problematic thematic intent.

Most of the technical choices made in this film feel like the most uninteresting, trite direction one could possibly take the material. Yahya Abdul-Mateen II, an actor I’m quite the fan of, feels listless in the leading role, heartlessly trudging through cliched lines that anyone in the theater could’ve written the moment they saw the premise of the movie. The film is visually drab, with a yellow sheen that makes it feel like it should’ve gone straight to Paramount+ rather than a big showcase on the silver screen. The score is so obvious that it becomes immediately forgotten, and the “action” scenes (if we can call them that) are just terrible gun effects and watching the dull choreography of stuntmen playacting at anything with real stakes.

Credit where credit is due, Mark Wahlberg is unquestionably the performance of the movie… for better or worse. The moment he comes onto the screen, he brings this off-the-wall, manic energy that captivates in the worst way imaginable. He doesn’t feel at home in this film at all, but it’s also hard to say what movie this character would’ve made sense for. Walking with swagger even though his personage elicits no intimidation, but also every joke he says lands with a wet thud because he’s too bizarre to be funny. The prosthetic nose they put on him doesn’t really change his appearance at all, but it’s noticeable enough where his character fully enters the uncanny valley, and one can’t help but stare at his face throughout, wondering why Marky Mark looks ever so slightly different from how one is used to seeing him. It’s a hilarious role in all the wrong ways, and honestly, I hope it’s remembered come time for awards season… they say the Razzies are always looking!

All this time, I’ve been describing the elements of the film that left me in a stupor of boredom. I suppose to avoid “spoilers,” I will refrain from describing the elements of this movie that awakened a genuine rage in me, but suffice it to say that when this movie is bad, it is worrisomely, horrifyingly bad. The peak of the messaging in this film is a “racism is bad” statement that makes *Green Book* look subtextually nuanced, while the nadir is a horrific twisting of the ideals of Malcolm X in a way that implies a grave misunderstanding of the building blocks of the Civil Rights Movement of the 1960s. I find some of the ideas presented here to be legitimately disgusting, and the final act of this made me skin crawl more than any other movie I’ve seen this year.

The thing about Warren songs is that they’re always designed to leave your brain the moment you’re done listening. They are disposable, Styrofoam cups that you throw away without ever thinking about them again. This is the disposable Styrofoam cup of the year in film; something that you don’t even regard with mental energy as you have it, but with insidious effects on the world around it when taken seriously. A nothingburger with *E. coli* baked into the meat.
