Olivia Wilde has been in the news a few times over the past several years, and not always for the best reasons. From her very public separation from Ted Lasso himself, to rumors that Florence Pugh had to step in as director in her absence from the set of box office and critical failure *Don’t Worry Darling*, things have not been looking great for her of late. With her third directorial effort *The Invite*, Wilde has turned in her best work yet both in front of and behind the camera. Hollywood is known for having a short memory, so if you bought any Wilde stock, now is the time to hold!

The film opens as Joe (Seth Rogen) finishes half-heartedly teaching a music class and engages in a prolonged struggle with his foldable bike. He arrives home to find his wife, Angela (Olivia Wilde), hurriedly preparing for a dinner party that he claims to know nothing about. Dev Hynes’ delightful score swells as the tension builds and we get to know this couple through their constant bickering. The crux of the argument: Angela invited the upstairs neighbors, Pina (Penelope Cruz) and Hawk (Edward Norton) over to their apartment, but Joe wants nothing to do with them. The witty screenplay by Rashida Jones and Will McCormack shines as the dialogue lets us in on a long-suffering marriage between two people who have grown apart over the years, to the point that they seem no longer capable of holding a civil conversation.

Once Hawk and Pina arrive, it is immediately apparent that they couldn’t be more different from their host couple for the night. They are free in their conversation, openly affectionate with each other, and take genuine pleasure in getting to know both Joe and Angela. Over the course of the night, each character’s past and motivations are slowly revealed in ways both shocking and hilarious. This is a film that might be described as stagebound, since it takes place in a single location and only includes four characters, but Wilde’s dynamic camera and editing decisions keep it lively and move it along at a brisk pace.

Did I mention that this is *funny*? There was raucous laughter at my screening throughout. Rogen’s typical sense of humor shines here, but it is filtered through an angry and depressed middle aged man whose life hasn’t turned out the way he thought it would. This ranks up there with his best performances as he weaponizes his humor to reveal deep emotions hidden beneath the surface. Norton has an incredible monologue towards the end of the film in which he reveals a crucial part of his backstory. Wilde’s harried, self-loathing housewife is wonderful in her attempts to keep the night on the rails and not let the cracks of her crumbling marriage show. Cruz, perhaps is the most enigmatic of the foursome, as she has a lot of fun with her impatience for social niceties, and her background as a therapist comes into play in one surprising, revelatory scene. On top of the verbal tete a tete throughout, there is some fantastic physical comedy here: dancing, pratfalls, back pain, and more!

*The Invite* is the kind of relationship comedy for adults that doesn’t come around too often these days, so I would recommend that you see this in a theater as soon as you can. It is consistently funny and entertaining while also exploring topics that can lead all of us to reflect on our own relationships. Can you ever truly know another person? What happens when you stop trying to? These questions may never be answered definitively, but there is one that I can answer for you: this invitation is worth accepting!
